Class 10 Science Chapter 7: How do Organisms Reproduce? | EduRain Blog

Class 10 Science Chapter 7 explains how living organisms produce new individuals of their own kind. Important questions come from asexual methods such as fission, budding, and regeneration, sexual reproduction in flowering plants, the human reproductive system, and reproductive health. Practising definitions, differences, diagrams, and reasons helps students answer confidently in CBSE board examinations.

This blog covers the main concepts and question types from Class 10 Science Chapter 7: How do Organisms Reproduce It also includes a preparation plan and common mistakes to avoid.

Why is this chapter important for CBSE Class 10 board examinations?

This chapter links directly to NCERT Class 10 Science and carries a mix of definition, diagram, reasoning, and application questions. It also builds the base for heredity and evolution, so a strong understanding here helps with later topics.

Exact marks distribution can change with the yearly CBSE sample paper and syllabus. Always check the latest official CBSE curriculum document before finalising your study plan.

What are the main modes of asexual reproduction?

Asexual reproduction involves a single parent and produces offspring that are genetically similar. The main modes are fission, fragmentation, regeneration, budding, vegetative propagation, and spore formation.

ModeExampleKey idea
Binary fissionAmoeba, LeishmaniaOne cell divides into two
Multiple fissionPlasmodiumOne cell divides into many daughter cells
FragmentationSpirogyraFilament breaks into pieces that grow
RegenerationHydra, PlanariaCut body parts grow into new organisms
BuddingHydra, yeastA bud grows and separates from the parent
Vegetative propagationBryophyllum, rose, sugarcaneNew plants grow from roots, stems or leaves
Spore formationRhizopusSpores grow into new individuals in suitable conditions


Students often confuse regeneration with reproduction. Regeneration needs specialised cells to form new tissues. Not every organism can do it.

Why are DNA copying and variation important in reproduction?

DNA copying passes the blueprint of body design from parent to offspring. The copying is never perfectly exact, so small variations arise. These variations help a species survive when the environment changes.

Sexual reproduction produces more variation than asexual reproduction because two parents contribute DNA. Board questions often ask why variation is useful, or why DNA copying happens inside a cell.

How does sexual reproduction happen in flowering plants?

In flowering plants, the flower is the reproductive organ. Pollen from the anther reaches the stigma through pollination. The pollen tube then carries male gametes to the ovule, where fertilisation forms a zygote.

Remember these points:

  1. The stamen is the male part and the carpel is the female part.
  2. Self-pollination occurs within the same flower or plant. Cross-pollination occurs between different plants.
  3. After fertilisation, the ovule becomes the seed and the ovary becomes the fruit.
  4. A unisexual flower has either stamens or carpels. A bisexual flower has both.

A diagram of a longitudinal section of a flower is a frequent three-mark question. Practise labelling the anther, filament, stigma, style, ovary and ovule.

How does human reproduction work?

Human reproduction is sexual and involves the fusion of a sperm and an egg. Fertilisation usually occurs in the fallopian tube. The zygote then divides, implants in the uterus, and develops into an embryo supported by the placenta.

Know the functions of these organs:

  1. Testes produce sperm and testosterone. They lie in the scrotum, which keeps them at a lower temperature.
  2. The vas deferens carries sperm. Glands add fluid to form semen.
  3. Ovaries produce eggs and hormones. The fallopian tube carries the egg.
  4. The uterus supports the growing embryo. Its lining thickens every month.
  5. If fertilisation does not occur, the lining breaks down and leaves the body as the menstrual flow.

Reproductive health is also part of the chapter. Study contraceptive methods such as barrier methods, oral pills, copper T, and surgical methods. Understand why sexually transmitted infections such as gonorrhoea, syphilis and HIV AIDS need awareness and prevention. Questions on the declining sex ratio and the social issue of female foeticide can also appear.

Which important questions should students practise from this chapter?

Students should practise short definitions, differences, reasoning questions and labelled diagrams. These formats appear regularly in board-style papers. The list below is a practice guide, not a prediction of the actual paper.

One- or two-mark questions

  1. What is the importance of variation for a species?
  2. Name the part of a flower that develops into a fruit.
  3. Why do testes lie outside the abdominal cavity?
  4. What is vegetative propagation? Give one example.

Three-mark questions

  1. Differentiate between binary fission and multiple fission with examples.
  2. Describe the process of fertilisation in flowering plants.
  3. Explain how the placenta helps the developing embryo.
  4. List any three advantages of vegetative propagation.

Five-mark questions

  1. Describe the male reproductive system with a neat labelled diagram.
  2. Explain the different methods of asexual reproduction in organisms with suitable examples.
  3. Compare sexual and asexual reproduction in terms of variation, parents, and survival.

How can students prepare this chapter step by step?

A steady plan works better than last-minute revision. Follow these steps for better retention.

  1. Read the NCERT chapter once for understanding, without taking notes.
  2. Make a table of asexual methods with one example each.
  3. Practise drawing flower, human male and female reproductive system diagrams until labelling is quick.
  4. Solve NCERT in-text and exercise questions in your own words.
  5. Attempt previous year questions and sample papers from the official CBSE website.
  6. Revise weak diagrams and differences every few days.

Some students benefit from teacher guidance, doubt solving, and regular tests. If you want structured support, exploring the Best CBSE Class 10 Online Coaching can help you follow a syllabus-aligned schedule with live explanations and practice papers for Science.

What common mistakes should students avoid?

Most lost marks come from missing keywords, incomplete diagrams, and mixed-up terms. Avoid these errors.

  1. Writing "pollination" and "fertilisation" as the same process.
  2. Forgetting arrows and labels in diagrams.
  3. Mixing up the functions of the uterus and the fallopian tube.
  4. Saying all asexual reproduction needs two parents.
  5. Giving examples that do not match the method asked in the question.
  6. Leaving out reasoning words such as "because" in reason-based answers.

Key Takeaways

  1. Asexual reproduction involves one parent and produces similar offspring.
  2. Variation arises from inexact DNA copying and is useful for survival.
  3. Flowering plants reproduce sexually through pollination, fertilisation, seed and fruit formation.
  4. Human reproduction needs correct knowledge of organs, fertilisation, implantation and the placenta.
  5. Reproductive health topics include contraception, sexually transmitted infections and the sex ratio.
  6. Diagrams and difference tables are high-value revision tools for this chapter.

Conclusion and Next Steps

Chapter 7 rewards clear concepts, neat diagrams and precise terms. Revise the modes of reproduction, practise the listed questions, and test yourself with timed papers. A good next step is to prepare one page of diagrams and differences for last-minute revision. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in Class 10 Science Chapter 7?

Focus on modes of asexual reproduction, DNA copying and variation, reproduction in flowering plants, the human reproductive system, fertilisation, the placenta, contraception and reproductive health.

FAQ 2: Which diagrams should students practise from this chapter?

Practise the longitudinal section of a flower, binary fission in Amoeba, budding in Hydra, and the male and female reproductive systems. Label every part and keep the diagrams neat.

FAQ 3: What is the difference between pollination and fertilisation?

Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma. Fertilisation is the fusion of the male and female gametes, which forms the zygote inside the ovule.

FAQ 4: Why is variation important for species?

Variation helps some individuals survive environmental changes. Over generations, this improves the chances of the species continuing, which is why sexual reproduction is important for populations.

FAQ 5: How should students answer reasoning questions from this chapter?

Give the exact term, state the reason in one or two clear sentences, and add an example if the question allows. Avoid long, unfocused answers.

FAQ 6: Is this chapter enough to score well in Biology for Class 10?

It is an important chapter, but marks depend on the whole Science paper. Revise NCERT carefully and practise sample papers from the official CBSE website.
