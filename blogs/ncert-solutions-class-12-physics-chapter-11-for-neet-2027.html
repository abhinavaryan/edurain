NCERT Solutions Class 12 Physics Chapter 11 for NEET: Important Questions & Formula Sheet | EduRain Blog

Class 12 Physics Chapter 11 is Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter in the rationalised NCERT book. For NEET, focus on the photoelectric effect, Einstein's equation, stopping potential, photon momentum, and de Broglie wavelength. This guide gives a formula sheet, solved questions, and common mistakes for revision.

The chapter is short, so it rewards careful reading. Students who work through NCERT Solutions Class 12 Physics Chapter 11 for NEET learn how each concept connects to the next. The chapter moves from experimental observations to Einstein's explanation to matter waves.

What Does Class 12 Physics Chapter 11 Cover for NEET?

Chapter 11 covers electron emission, the photoelectric effect, Einstein's photoelectric equation, the particle nature of light, de Broglie waves, and the Davisson-Germer experiment. NEET questions are mostly direct, formula-driven, or graph-based.

Key subtopics to study:

  1. Work function and electron emission methods
  2. Hertz, Hallwachs and Lenard observations
  3. Effect of intensity, frequency, and potential on photocurrent
  4. Failure of the wave theory of light
  5. Photon energy and momentum
  6. Wave nature of matter and the Davisson-Germer experiment

Which Formulas Should Students Remember From This Chapter?

Students should remember the photon energy relation, Einstein's equation, the stopping potential relation, threshold conditions, and the de Broglie formulas. These cover most numerical questions on the Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter.

Working through NCERT Solutions Class 12 Physics Chapter 11 for NEET also helps with numerical accuracy. Here are four solved questions for NEET Physics Chapter 11 Important Questions practice.

Question 1. Light of wavelength 400 nm falls on a metal with work function 2.0 eV. Find K_max and the stopping potential.

Photon energy = 1240/400 = 3.1 eV. K_max = 3.1 − 2.0 = 1.1 eV. The stopping potential is therefore 1.1 V.

Question 2. Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through 100 V.

λ = 1.227/√100 = 0.1227 nm.

Question 3. What happens to K_max if the intensity of incident light is doubled, with the frequency unchanged?

K_max stays the same. The number of photoelectrons emitted per second doubles, so the saturation current increases.

Question 4. Find the threshold wavelength for a metal with work function 2.5 eV.

λ₀ = 1240/2.5 = 496 nm.

What Common Mistakes Should Students Avoid?

The most common errors are mixing units, confusing intensity with frequency, and misusing the de Broglie shortcut formula. Careful unit checks and concept clarity prevent most lost marks in Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter NEET Questions.

MistakeCorrect approach
Using 1240 with wavelength in metresUse 1240 only with wavelength in nm and energy in eV
Thinking higher intensity raises K_maxK_max depends on frequency and work function only
Expecting emission below ν₀ at high intensityNo emission occurs below the threshold frequency
Mixing J and eV in one equationConvert everything to one unit first
Using 1.227/√V for protonsThat shortcut is for electrons only
Forgetting the sign of φ₀ on graphsThe y-intercept is −φ₀

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in Class 12 Physics Chapter 11 for NEET?

Focus on the photoelectric effect, Einstein's equation, stopping potential, threshold frequency, photon momentum, and de Broglie wavelength. Graph interpretation and the Davisson-Germer experiment also deserve revision.

FAQ 2: Which formulas should students remember for Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter?

Remember E = hν, K_max = hν − φ₀, eV₀ = K_max, λ = h/p and p = h/λ. Also remember hc ≈ 1240 eV nm for quick calculations.

FAQ 3: How can students practise numerical questions from this chapter?

Start with NCERT solved examples, then attempt the exercises. After that, solve timed sets that mix stopping potential, threshold wavelength, and de Broglie wavelength problems.

FAQ 4: Does the intensity of light change the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons?

No. The maximum kinetic energy depends only on the frequency of light and the work function of the metal. Intensity changes the number of emitted photoelectrons per second.

FAQ 5: Is the Davisson-Germer experiment important for NEET?

Yes, but mainly as a concept. Remember that it confirmed the wave nature of electrons through diffraction from a nickel crystal, supporting de Broglie's hypothesis.

FAQ 6: Is the NCERT book enough for this chapter in NEET?

NCERT covers the core theory and is the main source for this chapter. Add extra numerical and graph practice from test papers to build speed and accuracy.
