Best Mnemonics & Tricks to Remember Plant Growth Regulators (Hormones) for NEET | EduRain Blog

Plant growth regulators are one of the most scoring yet confusing topics in NEET Biology. Students often struggle to remember which hormone does what, their functions, and their effects on plant growth. If you are preparing for NEET 2025 or 2026, this article is made for you. Here, you will learn powerful plant hormone tricks for NEET aspirants to memorize all five major hormones quickly. You will also discover easy mnemonics, common mistakes to avoid, and practical revision strategies that make this topic stick in your memory for exam day.


What Are Plant Growth Regulators?


Plant growth regulators are chemical substances that control growth, development, and responses in plants. They are also called phytohormones. These regulators work even in very small concentrations.


There are five major categories every NEET aspirant must know:


Auxins: Promote cell elongation

Gibberellins: Promote stem elongation and seed germination

Cytokinins: Promote cell division

Abscisic Acid (ABA): Inhibits growth and causes dormancy

Ethylene: Promotes fruit ripening and leaf fall


For NEET, you need to remember specific functions, discovery, and effects of each hormone. That is where mnemonics become your best friend.


How to Remember All Five Hormones Easily?


The simplest trick is to use a single sentence mnemonic that covers all five hormones in order.


Mnemonic: "Aunty Gita Calls Ethylene Abscisic"

A – Auxins

G – Gibberellins

C – Cytokinins

E – Ethylene

Ab – Abscisic Acid


This one sentence helps you recall all five hormones in seconds during the exam.

Another popular mnemonic is:


"All Girls Can Eat Apples"


Both work perfectly. Pick whichever feels natural and repeat it during revision.


What Are the Best Tricks for Auxins and Gibberellins?


Auxins


Auxins are the first plant hormones discovered. Here is a quick trick to remember their functions:

Mnemonic: "CLAP D"

C – Cell elongation

L – Lateral bud suppression (apical dominance)

A – Apical dominance

P – Phototropism and gravitropism

D – Differentiation of xylem


Quick fact: Auxin was discovered from experiments on coleoptiles of oat and canary grass. Remember "Darwin started it, Went continued it, and F.W. Went named Auxin."


Gibberellins


Gibberellins were discovered from a fungus called Gibberella fujikuroi. This fungus causes "foolish seedling disease" (Bakanae disease) in rice.


Mnemonic for Gibberellin functions: "SAFE B"

S – Stem elongation

A – Alpha-amylase production in barley

F – Fruit elongation

E – Early flowering in long-day plants

B – Bolting (sudden elongation of internodes before flowering)


Exam tip: If any NEET question mentions "bolting" or "Bakanae disease," the answer is always Gibberellin.


How to Remember Cytokinins, Ethylene, and ABA?


Cytokinins


Cytokinins promote cell division. The name itself is a clue. "Cyto" means cell, and "kinin" relates to kinesis (division).


Mnemonic: "CD MAN"

C – Cell division

D – Delay of senescence (aging)

M – Morphogenesis

A – Activation of lateral buds

N – Nutrient mobilization


Key point: Cytokinins oppose auxin in controlling apical dominance. If the question says "promotes lateral bud growth," the answer is Cytokinin, not Auxin.


Ethylene


Ethylene is the only gaseous hormone. This one fact alone helps answer many questions.

Mnemonic: "RALF"

R – Ripening of fruits

A – Abscission (leaf and fruit fall)

L – Lateral growth (horizontal growth)

F – Feminizing effect in flowers (promotes female flowers in cucumber)


Common exam trap: Students confuse ABA and Ethylene for leaf fall. Both are involved, but Ethylene is the primary answer when "ripening" or "abscission" appears in the question.


Abscisic Acid (ABA)


ABA is called the "stress hormone" because it responds to drought, salinity, and other stress conditions.

Mnemonic: "SOS WD"

S – Stress hormone

O – Opposes growth

S – Stomatal closure

W – Wilting response

D – Dormancy of seeds and buds


Exam tip: Whenever you see "inhibits" or "dormancy" in a NEET question, think ABA first.


What Are Common Mistakes Students Make in This Topic?


Many students lose marks not because they did not study, but because they confuse similar functions. Here are the top mistakes to avoid:


Confusing Auxin and Cytokinin – Auxin promotes apical dominance. Cytokinin breaks it. They are antagonistic.

Mixing up ABA and Ethylene – Both cause leaf fall, but their primary roles differ. ABA is for stress and dormancy. Ethylene is for ripening and abscission.

Forgetting Gibberellin's role in barley – Alpha-amylase production in barley endosperm is a frequently tested fact.

Ignoring the gaseous nature of Ethylene – This is a direct one-mark question in many NEET papers.

Not revising discovery details – NEET sometimes asks who discovered a specific hormone. Revise names such as F.W. Went (Auxin), Kurosawa (Gibberellin), and Skoog & Miller (Cytokinin).


Using plant hormone tricks NEET toppers recommend, you can avoid these traps easily.


How to Revise Plant Hormones Effectively for NEET?


Revision is where most students either win or lose marks. Here is a practical plan:


Create a comparison table – List all five hormones with columns for discovery, functions, effects, and examples. Stick it on your study wall.


Use flashcards – Write the mnemonic on one side and full functions on the other. Test yourself daily.


Solve previous year questions – At least 20 to 30 questions from past NEET papers focusing on this chapter. Practice them all.


Group study sessions – Quiz each other using mnemonics. Teaching helps retention.


Use trusted resources – Online education platforms offer chapter-wise practice tests that help you test your memory under timed conditions.


Revise every 3 days – Spaced repetition is proven to improve long-term memory. Do not leave this chapter for the last week.


Many students also benefit from watching short video lessons on online education platforms such as Edurain that break down each hormone with diagrams and real exam questions.

Key Takeaways


Use simple mnemonics such as "All Girls Can Eat Apples" to remember all five plant hormones.

Learn function-specific tricks such as "CLAP D" for Auxins and "RALF" for Ethylene.

Avoid common mistakes by understanding which hormone promotes and which inhibits growth.

FAQs


1. How many questions come from plant hormones in NEET?

Typically, 2 to 4 questions appear from plant growth and development in NEET every year. Plant hormones form a significant part of this section.


2. Is this topic difficult for average students?

No. With proper mnemonics and regular revision, even beginners can score full marks from this chapter. The key is understanding functions, not rote memorization.


3. Which NCERT chapter covers plant growth regulators?

Chapter 15, "Plant Growth and Development," in the Class 11 NCERT Biology textbook covers all five hormones in detail.


4. Can I rely only on NCERT for this topic in NEET?

Yes, NCERT is the primary source. Most NEET questions are directly or indirectly from NCERT lines. Supplement with previous year questions for practice.


5. What is the best way to remember hormone functions quickly?

Use the mnemonics shared in this article. Write them on sticky notes, revise daily, and test yourself with MCQs. Spaced repetition works best.


6. Do coaching institutes cover tricks for this topic?

Most coaching institutes and many trusted study resources do share mnemonics. However, creating your own personalized tricks often leads to better recall during the actual exam.
