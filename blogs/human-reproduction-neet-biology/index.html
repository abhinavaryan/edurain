Male and Female Reproductive Systems

The male reproductive system includes testes, epididymis, vas deferens, and accessory glands. The female system comprises ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, and vagina. NEET questions frequently test the location and function of each organ.

Sertoli cells nourish developing sperm within the seminiferous tubules. Leydig cells secrete androgens under the influence of luteinizing hormone. In females, the corpus luteum secretes progesterone to maintain the endometrial lining.

Gametogenesis and Menstrual Cycle Explained

Spermatogenesis occurs in the seminiferous tubules and produces four functional sperm. Oogenesis begins during embryonic development and yields one functional ovum per cycle. The contrast between these two processes is a favorite NEET topic.

The menstrual cycle has four distinct phases to remember. The menstrual phase lasts 3 to 5 days with endometrial shedding. The follicular phase involves FSH-driven growth of the Graafian follicle. Ovulation occurs around day 14, triggered by the LH surge.

The luteal phase follows ovulation and lasts about 14 days. Progesterone levels peak during this phase to prepare the uterus. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates. This triggers menstruation and the entire cycle restarts.

Fertilization, Implantation and Embryonic Development

Fertilization takes place in the ampullary region of the fallopian tube. The sperm must penetrate the zona pellucida to reach the ovum. The cortical reaction prevents polyspermy immediately after sperm entry.

The zygote undergoes cleavage divisions as it moves toward the uterus. It forms a morula with 16 cells by day 3 or 4. The blastocyst implants into the endometrium around day 7 after fertilization.

The inner cell mass differentiates into the embryo proper. The trophoblast layer attaches to the uterine wall and forms the placenta. Gastrulation establishes the three germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm.

Parturition is triggered by the fetal ejection reflex and oxytocin release. Lactation begins after delivery under the influence of prolactin hormone. Colostrum produced in the first few days contains essential antibodies for the newborn.

Common Mistakes Students Make in This Chapter

Many students confuse the roles of Sertoli cells and Leydig cells. Remember that Sertoli cells provide nutrition while Leydig cells produce hormones. Mixing these two functions is a frequent error in NEET.

Another common mistake involves the site of fertilization. Students often write the uterus instead of the ampulla. The fallopian tube ampulla is the only correct site of fertilization in humans.

Students also miscount the number of cells in the morula stage. The morula contains 8 to 16 cells and not 32. Confusing morula with blastocyst stages leads to negative marking.

Misunderstanding the menstrual cycle hormone graph is another major error. Students fail to identify the LH surge peak correctly on day 14. Practice reading and interpreting the NCERT hormone graph repeatedly.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How many questions come from human reproduction in NEET UG?

NTA typically asks 3 to 4 questions from this chapter every year. These questions cover diagrams, hormonal regulation, and embryonic development. Thorough NCERT reading ensures you can attempt all of them correctly.

2. Which diagrams are most important for the human reproduction chapter?

The sectional view of the seminiferous tubule and the female reproductive system are critical. The ovarian follicle stages and blastocyst implantation diagrams also appear frequently. Practice all NCERT-labeled diagrams thoroughly.

3. How should I study the menstrual cycle for NEET?

Read the NCERT hormone graph and understand each phase clearly. Memorize the roles of FSH, LH, estrogen, and progesterone. Practice identifying the LH surge and its timing around day 14.

4. What is the difference between spermatogenesis and oogenesis?

Spermatogenesis produces four functional sperm from one primary spermatocyte. Oogenesis produces one functional ovum and polar bodies. Spermatogenesis is continuous, while oogenesis has a long resting phase.

5. Where does fertilization occur in humans?

Fertilization occurs in the ampullary region of the fallopian tube. This is a very common NEET question. Do not confuse it with the uterus or the isthmus region.
