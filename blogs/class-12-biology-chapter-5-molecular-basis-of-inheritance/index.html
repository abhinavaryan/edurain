How Did Experiments Prove That DNA Is the Genetic Material?

Griffith showed that a transforming principle changes bacteria. Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty identified it as DNA. Hershey and Chase confirmed it using radioactive phosphorus in DNA and radioactive sulphur in protein.

Keep these points clear:

  1. Griffith (1928) used Streptococcus pneumoniae and mice. Heat-killed smooth-strain bacteria transformed live rough-strain bacteria.
  2. Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty found that DNase stopped transformation. Protease and RNase did not.
  3. Hershey and Chase (1952) used bacteriophages. Radioactive DNA (32P) entered the bacteria, while radioactive protein (35S) stayed outside.

What Are the Key Facts About DNA Structure and Packaging?

DNA is a right-handed double helix of two antiparallel polynucleotide chains. Adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, and guanine pairs with cytosine through three. In eukaryotes, DNA wraps around histones to form nucleosomes.

How Does DNA Replication Work, and What Did Meselson and Stahl Show?

DNA replication is semiconservative, meaning each new DNA molecule keeps one old strand and one new strand. Meselson and Stahl proved this in 1958 using heavy nitrogen (15N) and a caesium chloride density gradient.

Remember these points:

  1. E. coli was grown in 15N medium, then shifted to 14N medium.
  2. After 20 minutes, all DNA was hybrid. After 40 minutes, equal amounts of hybrid and light DNA appeared.
  3. DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only in the 5' to 3' direction.
  4. The leading strand forms continuously. The lagging strand forms as Okazaki fragments, joined by DNA ligase.
  5. Replication begins at the origin of replication and forms a replication fork.

What Should You Know About Transcription and the Genetic Code?

Transcription copies one DNA strand into RNA using RNA polymerase. The genetic code is a set of triplet codons in mRNA that specify amino acids. Both topics produce frequent NEET questions on enzymes, codons, and processing.

The code is degenerate, unambiguous, nonoverlapping, commaless, and nearly universal. Har Gobind Khorana, Marshall Nirenberg, and Severo Ochoa contributed to cracking it.

How Does Translation Build a Protein?

Translation is the synthesis of a polypeptide on a ribosome using the sequence of mRNA. The tRNA acts as an adapter, bringing amino acids matched to codons through its anticodon. The process has initiation, elongation, and termination.

Key points to revise:

  1. Aminoacylation, or charging of tRNA, attaches an amino acid to its tRNA.
  2. The small ribosomal subunit binds mRNA first. The large subunit joins at the start codon.
  3. Peptide bond formation is catalysed by 23S rRNA in bacteria, so it acts as a ribozyme.
  4. Termination occurs at a stop codon, which is recognised by release factors.

What Are the Lac Operon, the Human Genome Project, and DNA Fingerprinting?

The lac operon is a model of gene regulation in bacteria, where lactose acts as the inducer. The Human Genome Project mapped the human genome. DNA fingerprinting identifies individuals using variable repeat sequences called VNTRs.

Lac operon: Jacob and Monod proposed it. It has the structural genes lacZ, lacY, and lacA, plus a promoter, operator, and the regulatory i gene. The i gene makes a repressor that binds the operator. Lactose binds the repressor and allows transcription.

Human Genome Project: It ran from 1990 to 2003. NCERT states the genome has about 3164.7 million base pairs and about 30,000 genes. Chromosome 1 has the most genes, and the Y chromosome has the fewest. Less than 2 percent of the genome codes for proteins.

DNA fingerprinting: Alec Jeffreys developed it. It uses VNTRs, Southern blotting, and probe hybridisation to compare DNA patterns.

What Common Mistakes Cost Marks in This Chapter?

Most lost marks come from mixing similar facts, such as which strand is the template or which polymerase makes which RNA. Careless reading of options also hurts.

  1. Confusing the template strand (3' to 5') with the coding strand.
  2. Mixing up RNA polymerase I, II, and III.
  3. Forgetting that 61 codons code for amino acids, not 64.
  4. Swapping the labels for DNA and protein in the Hershey and Chase experiment.
  5. Ignoring numerical facts such as 200 base pairs in a nucleosome.

Key Takeaways

  2. Replication is semiconservative and proceeds in the 5' to 3' direction.
  3. Transcription in eukaryotes needs splicing, capping, and tailing of hnRNA.
  4. The genetic code has 61 sense codons and three stop codons.
  5. The lac operon is switched on when lactose acts as an inducer.

FAQ 4: Why is the Meselson and Stahl experiment important?

It proved that DNA replication is semiconservative. After one generation, all DNA was hybrid, and after two generations, hybrid and light DNA appeared in equal amounts.

FAQ 5: What is the difference between the template strand and the coding strand?

The template strand runs 3' to 5' and is copied by RNA polymerase. The coding strand has the same sequence as the RNA, with thymine in place of uracil.
