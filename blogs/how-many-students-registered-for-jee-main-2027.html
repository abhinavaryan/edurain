How Many Students Registered for JEE Main 2027? Expected Registration Numbers & Latest Updates | EduRain Blog

NTA had received 15,04,866 JEE Main 2027 Session 1 applications by November 22, 2026, and late-fee registration runs until November 26. The final count is not public yet. The question of How Many Students Registered for JEE Main 2027 will be settled only after both sessions close, and NTA releases its statistics. This guide covers official numbers, past year trends, category data, cutoffs, and how to use them.

What Is the Latest JEE Main 2027 Registration Status?

As of November 22, 2026, NTA reported 15,04,866 submitted Session 1 forms. Late fee registration stays open until November 26, so this number is not final. NTA will publish verified registered and appeared figures after the exam.

Reports on the 2027 bulletin say candidates must log in through Meri Pehchaan, using DigiLocker or other listed options. Registering for Session 1 does not register a candidate for Session 2.

One comparison is useful. For 2026 Session 1, total registrations across all papers were 14,52,445, made up of 13,55,293 for Paper 1 and 97,152 for Paper 2. If the 2027 count of 15,04,866 also covers all papers, it is about 3.6% higher. NTA's final breakdown will confirm this.

How Many Candidates Registered in Earlier JEE Main Years?

NTA's unique registrations across both sessions rose from 10,26,799 in 2022 to 16,04,854 in 2026, an increase of about 56%. Unique appeared candidates rose from 9,05,590 to 15,38,468 over the same period.

Some websites quote slightly different figures for the same year, especially for 2024 and for early 2026 counts. NTA's result notices are the safest reference.

What Is the Expected JEE Main 2027 Registration Number?

NTA has not issued a forecast. Session 1 applications already exceed 15 lakh, and one report expects over 13 lakh candidates to appear in January. Unique registrations across both sessions could cross the 2026 level.

Treat the following as arithmetic, not an NTA forecast. If unique registrations grow by 3% to 4%, as they did in 2025 and 2026, the total would land near 16.5 to 16.7 lakh. With attendance near 95%, about 15.8 to 16 lakh candidates would then appear. Session 2 will add new and repeat applicants, so the final figure may differ.

How Does a Larger Candidate Pool Affect Competition?

A larger pool of appeared candidates means each percentile point covers more students. In 2026, 2,50,182 candidates qualified for JEE Advanced, roughly 16% of the 15,38,468 unique candidates who appeared.

In Session 1 of 2026, 13,04,653 candidates appeared. One percentile point therefore covered about 13,000 students. Qualifying cutoffs have also risen in recent years.

How Can Students Track Official Registration Figures?

Official figures come from NTA notices and result announcements. News counts during the registration window are provisional. Students should compare the registered and appeared columns separately and rely only on NTA data for final statistics.

  1. Visit jeemain.nta.nic.in and open the public notices section.
  2. Note the date of every update, because counts change until registration closes.
  3. Check the NTA statistics released with the Session 2 result, as happened in April 2026.
  4. Compare the registered and appeared columns, and Paper 1 against Paper 2.
  5. Treat website tallies as provisional until NTA confirms them.

The complete answer to How Many Students Registered for JEE Main 2027 will come only after Session 2 results, when NTA releases unique candidate statistics.

How Can Students Use These Numbers in Preparation?

Plan for a pool above 15 lakh, expect high cutoffs, and treat each session as a percentile contest. Timed mocks, accuracy, and revision help more than guessing exact registration counts.

  1. Treat January as the main attempt, and use Session 2 to improve your percentile.
  2. Aim above recent cutoffs, since the General cutoff has stayed above 93 percentile since 2024.
  3. Take full-length timed mocks every week and review each subject separately.
  4. Keep a list of repeated errors by chapter, and revise those first.
  5. Check your application during the correction window, since errors can create problems later.

Key Takeaways

  1. NTA reported 15,04,866 Session 1 forms by November 22, 2026, and late fee registration closes on November 26.
  2. JEE Main 2026 recorded 16,04,854 unique registrations and 15,38,468 unique candidates who appeared.
  3. Unique registrations grew by about 56% from 2022 to 2026, but yearly growth has slowed to about 4%.
  4. The 2026 General cutoff for JEE Advanced eligibility was 93.41 percentile, with 2,50,182 candidates qualifying.
  5. Estimates for 2027 are projections. Only NTA's final statistics are official.

Conclusion and Next Steps

JEE Main 2027 is on course to draw one of the largest applicant pools in its history, but the confirmed figure will come only from NTA after both sessions. Until then, use official notices for facts and treat projections as estimates.

As a next step, check your application during the correction window, note the Session 1 and Session 2 dates, and start a weekly timed mock test routine for the January attempt.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: How many candidates appeared in JEE Main 2026?

NTA statistics report 15,38,468 unique candidates who appeared across both sessions. Session 1 had 13,04,653 appearing candidates, and Session 2 had 10,34,330.

FAQ 2: What is the last date for JEE Main 2027 Session 1 registration?

The regular deadline was November 22, 2026. Candidates can still apply with a late fee of INR 500 until November 26, 2026.

FAQ 3: Does registering for Session 1 cover Session 2?

No. Session 2 registration opens separately on January 5, 2027. Candidates use the same application number and pay the fee again.

FAQ 4: Is JEE Main 2027 expected to be more competitive?

Session 1 forms already exceed the early 2026 count, which points to a large pool. Competition will be clear only after NTA publishes appeared candidate figures.

FAQ 5: What is the difference between registered and appeared candidates?

Registered candidates completed the application. Appeared candidates actually sat the exam. In 2026, attendance was about 96% in Session 1 and 93% in Session 2.

FAQ 6: When will the final JEE Main 2027 applicant numbers be known?

NTA released participation figures with the Session 2 result in 2026. Expect the same pattern in 2027, with complete unique candidate statistics after the April attempt.
