What Are Chemical Reactions and Equations Class 10?

In simple words, this chapter explains how substances change into new substances during a chemical process.

A chemical reaction happens when bonds break and new bonds form, creating a product that is different from the starting material. Chemical Reactions and Equations Class 10 teaches students how to write, balance, and identify these reactions using symbols and formulas.

This topic builds the base for higher classes and competitive exams such as NEET and JEE, so a clear understanding here helps later too.

Why Is Balancing Chemical Equations Important?

Balancing shows that mass is conserved during a reaction. According to the law of conservation of mass, matter can neither be created nor destroyed. This means the number of atoms on the reactant side must equal the number of atoms on the product side.

Steps to balance an equation:

  1. Write the correct formulas of reactants and products.
  2. Count atoms of each element on both sides.
  3. Add coefficients to balance unequal atoms.
  4. Check the equation again for accuracy.

Common mistake: Many students change subscripts instead of adding coefficients. This changes the compound itself, which is incorrect.

What Are the Main Types of Chemical Reactions?

There are five main types of chemical reactions in this chapter.

  1. Combination reaction – Two substances join to form one product.
  2. Decomposition reaction – One compound breaks into two or more simple substances.
  3. Displacement reaction – A more reactive element replaces a less reactive one.
  4. Double displacement reaction – Ions exchange between two compounds.
  5. Oxidation and reduction reaction – One substance gains oxygen while another loses it.

Simple example: Rusting of iron is a slow oxidation reaction that happens in the presence of moisture and oxygen.

Students build strong observation and logical thinking skills.

How Can Students Prepare Effectively for This Chapter?

A clear plan makes this chapter easy to score well in.

Practical tips for preparation:

  1. Read the NCERT textbook first, since board papers are based on it.
  2. Practice balancing at least 15 to 20 equations daily.
  3. Make short notes for reaction types with one example each.
  4. Revise using diagrams for reactions such as photosynthesis and rusting.
  5. Solve previous year CBSE question papers for practice.
