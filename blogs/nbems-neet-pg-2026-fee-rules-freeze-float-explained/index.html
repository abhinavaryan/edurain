NBEMS NEET PG 2026 Fee Rules: Float Candidates Can Delay First-Year Fee Payment | EduRain Blog

NBEMS NEET PG 2026 Fee Rules now say that Float candidates on NBEMS seats pay no first-year course fee while awaiting upgradation. Accredited hospitals have been told not to collect the fee from them, and Freeze candidates pay the allotted hospital directly, as per the notice dated October 24, 2026. This blog explains the rule, the Freeze and Float difference, and the mistakes to avoid.

What do the NEET PG 2026 counselling fee rules say?

NBEMS will not collect the first-year course fee during MCC counselling. The revised rule covers 50% All India Quota seats at accredited hospitals, and Float candidates pay nothing until upgradation is settled.

NBEMS has revised the first-year course fee payment rule for 50% All India Quota seats, and the notice is dated October 24, 2026. Read the official NBEMS notice together with the MCC bulletin. NBEMS handles the course fee for its accredited seats, while MCC runs the counselling rounds.

NBEMS has also said its Online Portal for Joining and Registration will stay closed until MCC finishes all rounds. Do not expect to pay through that portal for now.

How do Freeze and Float differ for fee payment?

Freeze means you accept the seat and pay the first year fee to the allotted hospital. Float means you keep the allotment while seeking a better seat, and you pay nothing during the wait.

Float means accepting the current allotment while expressing willingness to upgrade to another seat in the next round. The fee treatment follows from that choice.

OptionWhat it meansFirst year fee
FreezeCandidate accepts the allotted seatPaid to the allotted NBEMS-accredited hospital
FloatCandidate seeks upgradation in later roundsNo fee while awaiting upgradation

Other steps differ too. Online resignation applies to Float candidates, while Freeze candidates still resign by reporting physically to the allotted institute.

How did the fee process change from 2024 to 2026?

In 2024, the first-year fee went to NBEMS online before joining. The 2026 notice moves payment to the allotted hospital, and Float candidates pay nothing while awaiting an upgrade.

Under the November 19, 2024 notice, the fee was paid to NBEMS before joining, and the 2026 notice moves it to the hospital.

Particulars2024 notice2026 notice
Fee paid toNBEMS, through the online portalAllotted accredited hospital
Float candidatesPaid before joiningNo fee while awaiting upgradation

This change affects the NEET PG 2026 seat upgradation fee rules directly. Candidates no longer need to pay early and then wait for a refund after an upgrade.

Who still needs to pay the first-year fee early?

Freeze candidates pay the hospital. In-service doctors who need time to get relieved from their employer must still report, complete document verification, and pay the first-year fee while seeking an extension.

These candidates must complete the steps even while they seek an extension. The amount is not fixed for everyone. The first-year course fee can differ from one medical college to another. Check the hospital's fee details before the reporting date, and keep every receipt safe.

What steps should candidates follow before paying any fee?

Check the official notice first, confirm your option, and pay only when the rule applies to you. A wrong payment or a missed deadline can create avoidable problems during joining.

  1. Open the latest NBEMS notice and read the NEET PG counselling 2026 Freeze option and Float option instructions.
  2. Confirm whether your allotted seat is an NBEMS-accredited hospital seat.
  3. Decide between Freeze and Float before the round deadline.
  4. If you Freeze, ask the hospital for the exact first-year fee and payment mode.
  5. If you Float, do not pay the hospital, and report any demand for the fee to NBEMS.
  6. Check every message you receive against the NBEMS NEET PG 2026 Fee Rules published officially.

Common mistakes include trusting social media screenshots and confusing counselling charges with course fees. MCC registration fees and security deposits are separate from the first-year course fee.

Key Takeaways

  1. The NBEMS notice dated October 24, 2026 covers first-year fee payment for NBEMS seats.
  2. Float candidates pay no first-year fee while awaiting upgradation.
  3. Freeze candidates pay the first-year fee directly to the allotted accredited hospital.
  4. In-service doctors seeking relief from their employer must still pay while requesting an extension.
  5. The fee amount varies by institution, so confirm it with the hospital.

Conclusion and Next Steps

The 2026 rule reduces early financial pressure for candidates who want a better seat. Float candidates wait without paying, and Freeze candidates pay the hospital. Read the original notice before you act, since official notices can change between rounds.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: Do Float candidates have to pay the first year fee in NEET PG 2026?

No. Candidates allotted NBEMS seats who choose upgradation pay no fee while they wait. The rule applies before the final seat is decided.

FAQ 2: Who collects the fee from Freeze candidates?

A candidate who accepts the allotted NBEMS seat pays the first-year annual course fee to the accredited hospital where the seat was allotted. Confirm the amount and payment mode with that hospital.

FAQ 3: Can a hospital demand the fee from a Float candidate?

Accredited hospitals have been instructed not to collect the first-year fee from candidates who selected Float. If a demand is made, keep a record and contact NBEMS.

FAQ 4: Is the joining portal open for fee payment now?

NBEMS has said the portal will stay closed until MCC finishes all rounds of NEET PG 2026 counselling. Follow the hospital payment route for now.

FAQ 5: Do in-service doctors get an exemption?

Not from reporting and fees. In-service doctors who need relief from their employer must still report, verify documents, and pay the first-year fee while seeking an extension.

FAQ 6: Are MCC registration fees the same as the first-year course fee?

No. MCC registration fees and security deposits are counselling payments. The first year course fee is the training fee for the allotted seat and varies by institution.
