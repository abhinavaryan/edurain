NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Maths Chapter 6 Triangles: Formulas, Properties and Important Questions | EduRain Blog

Triangles is a core geometry chapter in Class 10 CBSE Maths. It teaches similarity, proportional sides, areas of similar triangles, and the Pythagoras theorem. This guide on NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Maths Chapter 6 Triangles explains the key theorems, formulas, and properties in simple steps. It also includes worked examples, common mistakes, and a preparation plan for board examination practice.

Chapter Overview and Topics Covered

Chapter 6 covers similar figures, criteria for similarity of triangles, the Basic Proportionality Theorem, areas of similar triangles, and the Pythagoras theorem with its converse. Board questions often ask for proofs and numerical problems.

Learn these basic terms first:

  1. Similar figures: figures with equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
  2. Congruent figures: figures that match exactly in size and form.
  3. Corresponding sides: sides that sit opposite equal angles in similar triangles.
  4. Scale factor: the constant ratio between corresponding sides.

Always confirm the chapter list and numbering on the official CBSE and NCERT pages for your academic year. Syllabus documents can change, and chapter numbers may differ across editions.

Similarity of Triangles and Its Criteria

Two triangles are similar when their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional. Three criteria let you prove similarity without checking every angle and side.

CriterionCondition neededKey point
AAAAll three angles equalTwo equal angles are enough, since the third follows
SSSAll three pairs of sides proportionalCompare the ratios of all three pairs
SASOne angle equal and the including sides proportionalThe angle must lie between those two sides


Similar triangles are written with matching vertex order. If triangle ABC is similar to triangle PQR, then angle A equals angle P. Side AB corresponds to side PQ.

Basic Proportionality Theorem and Its Converse

The Basic Proportionality Theorem, also called Thales theorem, states that a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides in the same ratio. The converse also holds.

In triangle ABC, let DE be parallel to BC, with D on AB and E on AC. Then AD/DB = AE/EC.

The converse says that if a line divides two sides in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side. Use the theorem when a parallel line is given. Use the converse when you must prove that lines are parallel.

Areas of Similar Triangles and Pythagoras Theorem

The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles equals the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides. The Pythagoras theorem states that in a right angled triangle, the hypotenuse squared equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

For similar triangles ABC and PQR:

Area of ABC / Area of PQR = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (CA/RP)²

The same square ratio applies to corresponding altitudes and medians. The perimeters of similar triangles are in the simple ratio of their sides.

The converse of the Pythagoras theorem is also important. If the square of one side equals the sum of the squares of the other two, the angle opposite that side is a right angle.

Essential Formulas and Worked Examples

Most numerical questions use a small set of results. Learn each with its condition, because every result applies only to a specific situation.

ResultFormulaCondition
Basic Proportionality TheoremAD/DB = AE/ECDE is parallel to BC
Similar trianglesAB/PQ = BC/QR = CA/RPTriangles are similar
Area ratioArea ratio = (side ratio)²Triangles are similar
Pythagoras theoremAB² + BC² = AC²Right angle at B


Example 1: In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC. If AD = 2 cm, DB = 3 cm, and AE = 4 cm, find EC. By the theorem, AD/DB = AE/EC. So 2/3 = 4/EC, which gives EC = 6 cm.

Example 2: Two similar triangles have areas in the ratio 9 : 16. Find the ratio of their corresponding sides. The side ratio is the square root of the area ratio, so it is 3 : 4.

Example 3: A triangle has sides 5 cm, 12 cm, and 13 cm. Check if it is right angled. Here 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169, which equals 13². So it is a right angled triangle.

Questions that ask for proofs of the main theorems are common in board practice. Treat that as a general pattern, not an official weightage statement.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Most lost marks come from wrong correspondence and missing conditions, not from missing concepts. Checking conditions before applying a theorem prevents many errors.

  1. Confusing similar with congruent: Similar figures need equal angles and proportional sides, not equal sides.
  2. Wrong vertex order: Write similar triangles with matching vertices, or the side ratios will be wrong.
  3. Using the theorem without parallel lines: The Basic Proportionality Theorem needs a line parallel to a side.
  4. Forgetting to square the ratio: Area ratios use the square of the side ratio.
  5. Applying Pythagoras to any triangle: It works only when one angle is a right angle.
  6. Misusing SAS: The equal angle must lie between the two proportional sides.

Key Takeaways

  1. Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional corresponding sides.
  2. AAA, SSS, and SAS are the three similarity criteria.
  3. A line parallel to one side divides the other two sides in the same ratio.
  4. The area ratio of similar triangles equals the square of the side ratio.
  5. The Pythagoras theorem and its converse apply only to right angled triangles.
  6. Check the official CBSE and NCERT documents for your academic year.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in Class 10 Maths Chapter 6 Triangles?

Similarity criteria, the Basic Proportionality Theorem, areas of similar triangles, and the Pythagoras theorem with its converse are the most important topics. Proofs and numerical problems both need practice.

FAQ 2: Which formulas should students remember for this chapter?

Remember AD/DB = AE/EC, the side ratios of similar triangles, the area ratio equal to the square of the side ratio, and AB² + BC² = AC². Note the condition for each.

FAQ 3: How can students practise numerical questions from this chapter?

Start with NCERT examples, then move to exercise questions. Draw the figure, write the given data, identify the theorem, and check that every ratio uses corresponding sides.

FAQ 4: What is the difference between similar and congruent triangles?

Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides, so they can differ in size. Congruent triangles have equal angles and equal sides, so they match exactly.

FAQ 5: When can the Basic Proportionality Theorem be used?

Use it when a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle and meets the other two sides. The converse is used to prove that such a line is parallel.

FAQ 6: Is the AA (Angle-Angle) similarity criterion sufficient to prove two triangles are similar?

Yes, the AA criterion is sufficient. If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, their third angles are automatically equal because the sum of angles in any triangle is always 180°. Therefore, proving just two pairs of corresponding angles equal is enough to prove similarity.
