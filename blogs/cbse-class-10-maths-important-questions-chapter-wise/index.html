CBSE Class 10 Maths Important Questions Chapter-Wise | EduRain Blog

Mathematics decides a large part of your Class 10 board score. Every chapter carries fixed marks, and smart students study those chapters first. This guide breaks down CBSE Class 10 Maths Important Questions by chapter, formula, and strategy. You will find the unit-wise weightage, the formulas worth memorizing, a weekly study plan, and the mistakes that quietly cost marks every year.

Chapter Overview and Why This Guide Matters

CBSE Class 10 Maths covers seven units spread across fourteen chapters, each carrying a fixed mark allocation. Knowing this allocation lets you spend more time on high-scoring chapters and less time on low-weightage ones.

The syllabus has been rationalized in recent years to remove lengthy derivations and repeated proofs. The board now focuses more on application-based questions than pure memory questions. This shift means practicing varied important questions matters more than memorizing textbook examples alone.

Here is the current unit-wise mark distribution for the eighty-mark theory paper.

Algebra alone holds twenty marks. Geometry follows with fifteen. Together they make up nearly half the paper, so your revision plan should reflect that weight.

High Weightage Chapters You Must Master

Certain chapters return every year in nearly every question paper pattern. Ranking them by return on effort helps you plan revision sessions with purpose.

  1. Quadratic Equations: nature of roots, word problems, and discriminant-based questions appear almost every year.
  2. Arithmetic Progressions: sum of n terms and nth term formulas are tested through direct and applied questions.
  3. Triangles: similarity theorems and the Basic Proportionality Theorem carry heavy long-answer weight.
  4. Circles: tangent properties generate one guaranteed proof or application question.
  5. Trigonometry Applications: height and distance problems test both formula recall and diagram reading.
  6. Statistics: mean, median, and mode from grouped data are asked in nearly every paper.

A Study Plan Built Around Important Questions

A focused plan works better than random practice. Follow CBSE Class 10 Maths Important Questions in this order for consistent progress.

  1. Revise the chapter's theory and formulas first, without skipping definitions.
  2. Solve five NCERT textbook questions per chapter before moving to extra practice.
  3. Attempt previous year important questions from that same chapter only.
  4. Time yourself on long answer questions to build exam speed.
  5. Revisit mistakes weekly instead of only before the exam.

Common Mistakes Students Make on This Topic

Most marks are lost through small, repeated errors rather than a lack of knowledge. Recognizing your own pattern early prevents it from repeating on exam day.

  1. Skipping the sign convention in coordinate geometry distance calculations.
  2. Forgetting to check both roots when solving quadratic word problems.
  3. Mixing up sine and cosine ratios during height and distance questions.
  4. Leaving the units out of mensuration answers, which costs presentation marks.
  5. Rounding values too early in statistics means calculations, causing wrong final answers.
  6. Not writing the tangent theorem statement before using it in circle proofs.

Key Takeaways

  1. Algebra and Geometry together hold thirty-five of the eighty theory marks.
  2. Quadratic Equations and Arithmetic Progressions return every year in some form.
  3. Practice previous years' important questions chapter by chapter, not randomly.
  4. Small errors in signs, units, and rounding cost more marks than gaps in knowledge.
  5. NCERT exercises remain the foundation for every important question you will face.
  6. A fixed weekly revision cycle beats last-minute cramming every time.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Which chapter carries the highest weightage in CBSE Class 10 Maths?

Algebra carries the highest weightage at twenty marks, covering polynomials, linear equations, quadratic equations, and arithmetic progressions.

2. How many important questions should I practice per chapter?

Aim for fifteen to twenty varied questions per chapter, covering short answer, long answer, and case study formats.

3. Are deleted topics still tested in the current syllabus?

No, questions strictly follow the current NCERT rationalized syllabus, so deleted proofs and topics will not appear.

4. What is the best way to revise trigonometry formulas quickly?

Group formulas by ratio type and practice one height and distance problem daily to keep the applications fresh.

5. How much time should Geometry get in my weekly schedule?

Since Geometry holds fifteen marks, allot at least two structured sessions each week for triangle and circle proofs.

6. Do statistics and probability questions repeat often in board papers?

Yes, mean, median, and mode questions from grouped data appear almost every year in some form. Structured revision, whether self-guided or through a platform such as Edurain, helps track this pattern chapter by chapter.
