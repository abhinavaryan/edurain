Electric Charges and Fields Class 12: Important Concepts, Formulas & Questions for CBSE Board Exam 2027 | EduRain Blog

Electric Charges and Fields Class 12 is the first chapter of NCERT Physics Part 1 and begins the electrostatics unit. It covers charge, Coulomb's law, electric field, dipoles, flux, and Gauss's law. Below you will find tables of formulas, derivation steps, solved numericals, and question types for board practice.

What topics does Class 12 Physics Chapter 1 include?

Class 12 Physics Chapter 1 includes electric charge, Coulomb's law, superposition, electric field, field lines, electric dipole, electric flux, and Gauss's law with its applications. Confirm the final topic list on the official CBSE Academic website each session.

What are the properties of charge and Coulomb's law?

Charge is additive, conserved, and quantised. Coulomb's law says the force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their distance, acting along the line joining them.

PropertyMeaning
AdditivityTotal charge is the algebraic sum of individual charges
ConservationTotal charge of an isolated system stays constant
Quantisationq = ne, where e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
Like chargesRepel each other
Opposite chargesAttract each other


Method of chargingWhat happens
FrictionElectrons transfer from one body to another
ConductionCharge flows by direct contact, giving the same sign of charge
InductionCharge is produced without contact, giving the opposite sign of charge

Key points about Coulomb's law:

  1. The force is a vector, and the forces on the two charges are equal and opposite.
  2. In a medium of dielectric constant K, the force becomes F / K.
  3. The law holds for point charges at rest and follows the inverse square rule.
  4. Conductors allow charge to move freely, while insulators do not.

How do the electric field, dipole, and field lines work?

The electric field at a point is the force per unit positive test charge placed there. For many charges, the net field is the vector sum of each field, which is called the superposition principle.

Property of field linesReason or meaning
Start on positive charge, end on negative chargeShows the direction of the field
Never cross each otherThe field has one direction at a point
Closer lines mean a stronger fieldLine density shows magnitude
Never form closed loops in electrostaticsThe field is conservative
Meet a conductor surface at right anglesNo tangential field on the surface

Points to remember about a dipole:

  1. A dipole is a pair of equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.
  2. In a uniform field, the net force is zero but the torque is not.
  3. Torque is maximum at θ = 90° and zero at θ = 0° or 180°.
  4. The axial field is twice the equatorial field at the same distance.

How does Gauss's law simplify field calculations?

Gauss's law states that the net flux through a closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by ε₀. It works best when symmetry lets you pick a Gaussian surface where the field is constant or parallel to the surface.

ApplicationGaussian surfaceResult
Infinite line of chargeCoaxial cylinderE = λ / (2πε₀r)
Infinite plane sheetCylinder or pillbox through the sheetE = σ / (2ε₀)
Thin spherical shellConcentric sphereOutside kq / r², inside zero

Steps for any derivation:

  1. Name the symmetry and draw the Gaussian surface.
  2. Write the flux through each part of the surface.
  3. Show that flux is zero through the parts where E is perpendicular to the area.
  4. Equate the total flux to q(enclosed) / ε₀ and solve for E.

A charge outside the surface adds zero net flux, but it still affects the field at points on the surface. A charge q at the centre of a cube gives flux q / (6ε₀) through each face.

What mistakes cost marks in this chapter?

The common mistakes are wrong units, ignoring vector direction, using the wrong dipole formula, and misreading flux. A short checklist before submitting each answer removes most of these errors.

MistakeCorrect approach
Using μC or cm directlyConvert to C and m first
Adding fields as plain numbersAdd as vectors using components
Equatorial field along pIt points opposite to p
Field outside a shell taken as zeroOnly the field inside is zero
Applying Gauss's law to an open surfaceUse a closed surface only
Confusing flux with fieldFlux depends on enclosed charge, field depends on all charges

Key Takeaways

  1. Charge is conserved, additive, and quantised as q = ne.
  2. Coulomb's law gives the force, and the field gives force per unit charge.
  3. Superposition handles systems of many charges.
  4. Dipole results on the axis and equatorial plane are frequent derivation topics.
  5. Gauss's law works best with symmetry and a closed Gaussian surface.
  6. Convert units first, then check vector directions before the final answer.

Conclusion and Next Steps

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in this chapter?

Coulomb's law, superposition, electric field, field lines, dipole field and torque, electric flux, and Gauss's law applications are the most important topics. Give extra time to the dipole and Gauss's law derivations.

FAQ 2: Which formulas should students remember from this chapter?

Remember F = kq₁q₂ / r², E = kq / r², p = q × 2a, τ = pE sinθ, Φ = EA cosθ, and Φ = q / ε₀. Also learn the field results for a line, a plane sheet, and a spherical shell.

FAQ 3: How can students practise numerical questions from this chapter?

Start with NCERT solved examples, then solve the exercise questions. Use SI units, draw a vector diagram when several charges are involved, and check the unit of each answer. Finish with sample paper numericals.

FAQ 4: Why is the field inside a charged spherical shell zero?

A Gaussian sphere drawn inside the shell encloses no charge, so the net flux is zero by Gauss's law. Spherical symmetry then makes the field zero at every interior point.

FAQ 5: What is the difference between electric field and electric force?

Electric force acts on a particular charge placed in a field. Electric field is the force per unit positive test charge, and it exists at a point whether or not a charge is placed there.

FAQ 6: Are NCERT questions enough for board exam preparation?

NCERT examples and exercises cover the core concepts and are the best starting point. Sample papers and previous year questions add practice with timing and different question styles.
