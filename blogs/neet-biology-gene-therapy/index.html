NEET Biology Gene Therapy: NCERT Notes & Study Guide

Gene therapy is such a small topic in NCERT, yet it gives NEET aspirants steady and easy marks every year. The topic is built around one clear example: the treatment of ADA deficiency. A solid grasp of NEET Biology Gene Therapy helps you answer statements, assertion-reason, and match-the-column questions quickly. Most students lose marks only because they mix up the enzyme, the vector, and the cure. This guide covers NCERT concepts, question patterns, common mistakes, a study plan, and FAQs for this topic.

Chapter Overview

Gene therapy is a method that corrects a faulty gene by inserting a working copy into a patient's cells. NCERT places it in Class 12, Biotechnology and Its Applications, under the health applications section.

The topic connects three ideas. These are genetic disorders, recombinant DNA tools, and the use of vectors. Revise the chapter Biotechnology: Principles and Processes first. Without restriction enzymes, cloning vectors, and ligases, the therapy steps will feel disconnected.

Stick to the rationalized NCERT text. NEET frames its options around the exact wording of the chapter, so read it line by line.

Core Concepts You Must Know

What Is ADA Deficiency and Why Does It Matter?

ADA deficiency is a genetic disorder caused by a missing enzyme, adenosine deaminase. It damages the immune system and leads to severe combined immunodeficiency, called SCID. This is the standard NCERT example of gene therapy.

The enzyme is vital for the survival of lymphocytes. Without it, the immune response collapses. Remember that the defect lies in the gene, not in a pathogen.

NCERT also notes that some patients can be treated by bone marrow transplantation or enzyme replacement. These options are not always fully effective. This gap is why gene therapy matters.

How Was the First Gene Therapy Performed?

The first clinical gene therapy took place in 1990 on a four-year-old girl with ADA deficiency. Doctors isolated her lymphocytes, added a functional ADA gene, and returned the cells to her body.

Learn the sequence as an ordered list:

  1. Lymphocytes are taken from the patient's blood.
  2. The cells are grown in a culture outside the body.
  3. A functional ADA cDNA is introduced using a retroviral vector.
  4. The modified lymphocytes are returned to the patient.

The cells are not immortal. This is why the treatment is not a permanent cure.

Why Is the Treatment Not Permanent?

The treatment needs repeating because the modified lymphocytes have a limited lifespan. The patient requires periodic infusions of genetically engineered cells. NCERT presents this as a clear limitation.

A permanent cure needs the gene to enter cells that divide through life. NCERT suggests that introducing the functional gene into marrow cells at an early embryonic stage could achieve this. Such cells would keep producing the enzyme.

What Role Do Vectors Play?

A vector carries the desired gene into the host cell. In the NCERT example, a disarmed retrovirus delivers the ADA gene into lymphocytes. Retroviruses integrate easily into the host genome.

Link this to the cloning chapter, where plasmids work as vectors. Both serve one purpose: delivering foreign DNA into a host.

Which Ethical Points Can Appear in Questions?

NCERT links modern biotechnology with ethical and regulatory concerns. The Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee, called GEAC, decides on the safety of genetically modified research and its public use.

Questions here are mostly factual. Know the full form of GEAC and its purpose. Also revise biopiracy and patents, since these share the same section.

Important Questions Pattern for NEET

NEET questions on this topic fall into four types. Practice each one separately.

  1. Statement-based: Judge whether a statement on ADA or gene therapy is correct.
  2. Assertion and reason: Decide if the reason explains the assertion.
  3. Match the column: Pair terms such as ADA, SCID, retroviral vector, and 1990.
  4. Direct recall: Name the enzyme, the vector, or the cell type used.

Practice for NEET Biology Gene Therapy works best when you frame your own questions from each NCERT line. For example, ask why lymphocytes were used. The answer is that they carry the immune response and can be grown in culture.

Include related application topics when practising. Recombinant insulin, Bt toxin, RNA interference, and PCR-based diagnosis often appear in the same set. They are separate concepts, but they share a chapter and a question style.

Common Mistakes Students Make on Gene Therapy

Most errors come from half-remembered facts. These are the mistakes to watch for.

  1. Calling ADA a gene. It is an enzyme. The gene codes for it.
  2. Calling the first therapy a permanent cure. It required repeated infusions.
  3. Mixing vectors. Retroviral vectors are used here, not bacterial plasmids.
  4. Skipping the vector basics. Without them, application questions feel confusing.
  5. Ignoring ethics. Biopiracy and patent issues appear as easy factual questions.
  6. Memorizing without sequence. Steps in the wrong order cost marks in ordering questions.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Which NCERT chapter covers gene therapy for NEET?

Gene therapy appears in Class 12, Biotechnology and Its Applications. It is explained under the health applications section. Read the first clinical case closely, as most questions come from it.

2. Which enzyme deficiency is linked to the first gene therapy?

The first gene therapy treated adenosine deaminase deficiency. This enzyme is essential for immune cell function. Its absence causes severe combined immunodeficiency, called SCID.

3. Is gene therapy a permanent cure for ADA deficiency?

Not in the NCERT example. The modified lymphocytes do not last forever, so patients need periodic infusions. A permanent cure requires correcting early marrow cells.

4. Which vector is used in the NCERT gene therapy example?

A retroviral vector carries the functional ADA cDNA into the patient's lymphocytes. Retroviruses can integrate into the host genome. Remember this vector for match-the-column questions.

5. What types of questions come from this topic in NEET?

Expect statement-based items, assertion-reason questions, match the column, and direct recall. Most test the enzyme name, the vector, or the cure versus treatment difference.

6. How many days should I give to this topic?

Four to five short sessions are usually enough. Revise vectors first, read the NCERT text, draw the flowchart, and then solve previous year questions.
