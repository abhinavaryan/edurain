Rotational Motion NEET Physics: Notes & Formulas Guide

Rotational motion is a core Class 11 Physics unit that links torque, angular momentum, and moment of inertia. This guide on Rotational Motion NEET Physics gives concise notes, formula tables, and practice tips based on NCERT. It covers key concepts, rolling motion, common mistakes, and a study plan.

Rotational Motion Fundamentals for NEET

Rotational motion describes how a rigid body turns about an axis. Every particle moves in a circle, so angular displacement, velocity, and acceleration describe the whole body. NCERT covers this in the chapter System of Particles and Rotational Motion.

A rigid body keeps fixed distances between its particles. Pure translation moves all particles equally. Pure rotation about a fixed axis makes every particle share the same angular velocity. Most real motion combines both, as in a rolling wheel.

Important Concepts and Subtopics

The key subtopics are centre of mass, torque, angular momentum, moment of inertia, equilibrium, and rolling. Each one appears in NEET numericals and conceptual statements, so learn how they connect.

  1. Centre of mass: The point where the total mass behaves as if concentrated. For particles, it is the mass weighted average position.
  2. Torque: The turning effect of a force about an axis. Its magnitude is force times the perpendicular distance from the axis.
  3. Angular momentum: The rotational counterpart of linear momentum. It stays constant when the net external torque is zero.
  4. Moment of inertia: The resistance of a body to change in rotation. It depends on mass and on how the mass is distributed about the axis.
  5. Equilibrium of a rigid body: Both the net force and the net torque must be zero.

Moment of Inertia and Rolling Motion

Moment of inertia values and rolling results are frequent exam targets. Memorise the standard values below and the rolling formula that follows from them.

BodyAxisMoment of inertia
Thin ringThrough centre, perpendicular to planeMR²
Uniform discThrough centre, perpendicular to plane½MR²
Solid sphereThrough any diameter(2/5)MR²
Hollow thin spherical shellThrough any diameter(2/3)MR²
Thin rodThrough centre, perpendicular to lengthML²/12
Thin rodThrough one end, perpendicular to lengthML²/3


For rolling without slipping, the condition is v = Rω. The total kinetic energy is ½mv²(1 + K²/R²), where K is the radius of gyration. On an incline of angle θ, acceleration is a = g sinθ / (1 + I/mR²).

Rolling bodyAcceleration down incline
Ring(1/2) g sinθ
Solid cylinder or disc(2/3) g sinθ
Hollow sphere(3/5) g sinθ

A smaller moment of inertia ratio gives greater acceleration. So a solid sphere reaches the bottom first, and a ring reaches it last.

Common Mistakes and Their Solutions

Most errors come from wrong axes, ignored conditions, and sign confusion. Each mistake below has a simple fix that you can apply during practice.

  1. Using the wrong axis: Moment of inertia changes with the axis. Always read the axis in the question before choosing a formula.
  2. Applying the parallel axis theorem from a non central point: The theorem needs the axis through the centre of mass as the reference.
  3. Forgetting rolling energy: A rolling body has both translational and rotational kinetic energy. Add both.
  4. Mixing up conservation laws: Angular momentum is conserved only when net external torque is zero.
  5. Ignoring direction of torque: Torque is a vector. Use the right hand rule to decide the sign.

  2. Torque equals moment of inertia times angular acceleration, and angular momentum is conserved without external torque.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in rotational motion for NEET?

Torque, angular momentum and its conservation, moment of inertia with both axis theorems, centre of mass, and rolling motion on an incline are the most important topics. Practise numericals and conceptual statements from each.

FAQ 2: Which formulas should students remember for this chapter?

Remember τ = Iα, L = Iω, rotational kinetic energy ½Iω², and the parallel and perpendicular axis theorems. Also learn the standard moment of inertia values and the rolling condition v = Rω.

FAQ 3: How can students practise numerical questions from this chapter?

Start with NCERT solved examples and exercises. Then group questions by type, such as moment of inertia, torque, conservation of angular momentum, and rolling. Finish with timed mixed sets and review each mistake.

FAQ 4: What is the difference between the parallel and perpendicular axis theorems?

The parallel axis theorem relates the moment of inertia about any axis to that about a parallel axis through the centre of mass. The perpendicular axis theorem applies only to planar bodies.

FAQ 5: Why does a solid sphere roll down an incline faster than a ring?

A solid sphere has a smaller ratio of moment of inertia to mR². Less energy goes into rotation, so more is available for translation. This gives it a higher acceleration.

FAQ 6: Is the syllabus for this chapter the same every year?

Not always. Students should check the latest official NTA syllabus and NCERT edition before preparing. Do not rely on older notes or unofficial lists for syllabus decisions.
