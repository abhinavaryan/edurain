NEET UG Statistics 2019 to 2026: What They Tell Us About NEET 2027 | EduRain Blog

NEET UG registrations rose from 15.19 lakh in 2019 to about 22.79 lakh in 2026. The qualification rate stayed close to 56 percent throughout. This guide reviews NEET UG Statistics 2019 to 2026 to explain participation and competition ahead of NEET 2027.

Year-wise NEET Registration and Attendance Data

Registrations grew from 15.19 lakh in 2019 to a peak of 24.06 lakh in 2024. Candidates appearing rose from 14.11 lakh to 23.33 lakh in the same period, then eased in 2025 and 2026.

From 2019 to 2024, the number of candidates appearing grew by roughly 65 percent. Attendance stayed between 93 and 98 percent in most years. The exceptions were 2020, at about 86 percent, and 2026, at about 88 percent.

Qualification Rate Patterns Across Eight Years

The share of appearing candidates who qualified stayed near 56 percent in every year from 2019 to 2026. This happens because NTA sets the qualifying cutoff as a percentile, not a fixed mark.

The general category cutoff is the 50th percentile. So the qualifying marks change each year with paper difficulty and candidate performance. The rate stays stable even when the number of candidates changes sharply.

A stable rate does not mean easy competition. Qualifying only makes a candidate eligible for counselling. Admission depends on rank, category, and seat availability.

This NEET UG data analysis shows that growth in qualified candidates came from larger participation, not from a change in the qualifying rule. Qualified candidates increased from 7.97 lakh in 2019 to 13.16 lakh in 2024.

How to Prepare for NEET 2027 Using These Numbers

Treat the data as a guide to competition, not a prediction of marks. Set a percentile-based target, build NCERT strength, and practise timed full-length mocks, ideally on a screen.

What Is Confirmed and Still Awaited for NEET 2027

The Centre announced on 15 May 2026 that NEET UG will be computer-based from 2027. As of the latest reports in October 2026, NTA had not announced the exam date or bulletin.

This NEET 2027 exam analysis separates official facts from expectations.

Confirmed by official sources:

  1. The May 3, 2026 exam was cancelled after paper leak allegations, and the re-examination was held on June 21.
  2. A total of 19,99,895 candidates appeared in the re-examination, against 22.05 lakh in the original test.
  3. NTA declared the result on 16 July 2026, with 11.21 lakh candidates qualifying.
  4. More than 93 percent of those who appeared were attempting NEET UG for the first time.
  5. More than 58 percent of qualified candidates were women.

Expected, not official:

  1. Registration around February 2027 and the exam around early May 2027 are projections based on past years.
  2. Reports mention a schedule spread over several days and shifts. NTA has not confirmed this.

The 2026 numbers are unusual because of the cancellation. Treat them as an exceptional year when reading NEET exam trends.

Common Mistakes When Reading NEET Exam Trends

Students often misread registration and qualification numbers. The most common errors are treating registrations as actual test takers, predicting cutoff marks from counts, and trusting unofficial tables without checking NTA data.

Avoid these mistakes:

  1. Counting registered candidates as competitors. Only candidates who appear affect the percentile. Compare "appeared" columns for real competition.
  2. Predicting cutoff marks from past qualifying numbers. Cutoff marks depend on paper difficulty each year.
  3. Treating 2026 as a normal year. The re-examination changed attendance, so NEET participation trends for 2026 need careful reading.
  4. Using unverified websites. Compare any figure with NTA statistics before using it for planning.
  5. Assuming qualification means admission. Counselling rank and seat availability decide the final outcome.

A sound NEET competition analysis uses appeared candidates, qualification percentile, and your own mock performance together.

Key Takeaways

  1. Registrations grew from 15.19 lakh in 2019 to 24.06 lakh in 2024, then settled near 22.8 lakh.
  2. The qualified share of appeared candidates stayed close to 56 percent every year.
  3. NTA sets the qualifying cutoff as a percentile, so marks change yearly.
  4. The 2026 exam was cancelled and re-held on 21 June, with 19,99,895 candidates appearing.
  5. NEET 2027 is announced as computer-based, but the date is not yet official.

Conclusion and Next Steps

The data shows rising participation and a steady qualification rate from 2019 to 2026. Competition grows with the number of candidates, so preparation quality and mock accuracy matter more than any single trend.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: How many candidates appeared and qualified in NEET UG 2026?

According to NTA, 19,99,895 candidates appeared in the 21 June 2026 re-examination. Of these, 11.21 lakh qualified, which is about 56 percent of those who appeared.

FAQ 2: Which year had the highest NEET UG registrations?

The year 2024 had the highest registrations in this period, with 24,06,079 candidates. About 23.33 lakh appeared, and about 13.16 lakh qualified.

FAQ 3: Why was NEET UG 2026 held twice?

The 3 May 2026 exam was cancelled after allegations of a paper leak. NTA conducted a fresh examination on 21 June 2026, and only that performance counted for results.

FAQ 4: Will NEET UG 2027 be computer-based?

The Centre announced on 15 May 2026 that NEET UG will be computer-based from 2027. NTA has not released the exam date or information bulletin in the latest reports. Check neet.nta.nic.in for updates.

FAQ 5: Does a 56 percent qualification rate mean the same cutoff marks every year?

No. The qualifying cutoff is a percentile, so the marks change with paper difficulty and candidate performance. The share qualifying stays similar, but the marks needed can differ.

FAQ 6: How can students use year-wise data in preparation?

Use the data to understand competition, not to predict marks. Focus on NCERT mastery, timed mocks, and error review, and confirm all dates and rules on the official NTA website.
