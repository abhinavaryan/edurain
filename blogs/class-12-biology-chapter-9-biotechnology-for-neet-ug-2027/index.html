Class 12 Biology Chapter 9: Biotechnology: Principles and Processes Notes for NEET UG 2027 | EduRain Blog

This chapter explains how scientists cut, join, and copy DNA to make recombinant organisms. Biotechnology principles rest on genetic engineering and bioprocess engineering. Class 12 Biology Chapter 9: Biotechnology is a regular source of NEET UG questions on enzymes, vectors, PCR, and bioreactors. These notes cover the key tools, steps, tables, and practice questions.

What are the core principles of biotechnology?

The two core principles are genetic engineering and bioprocess engineering. Genetic engineering alters the genetic material of an organism. Bioprocess engineering maintains sterile conditions so that only the desired microbe or cell grows and makes the product in large quantities.

Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer built the first recombinant DNA in 1972. They isolated an antibiotic resistance gene from a plasmid of Salmonella typhimurium and linked it to a plasmid vector. The recombinant DNA was then transferred to E. coli.

What are the three basic steps of genetic modification?

Genetic modification needs three steps: identifying the DNA with the desired genes, introducing that DNA into a host, and maintaining it so that it passes to the progeny. A successful recombinant needs a vector, a cutting tool, and a joining tool.

Which tools are used in recombinant DNA technology?

The main tools are restriction enzymes, polymerases, ligases, vectors, and the host organism. Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sites. DNA ligase joins the fragments. A vector carries the DNA into the host cell.

How do restriction enzymes work?

Restriction endonucleases recognise a specific palindromic nucleotide sequence in DNA. They cut both strands a little away from the centre of the palindrome. This leaves single-stranded overhangs called sticky ends, which help the fragments join with the same enzyme cut.

Key facts to remember:

  1. The first restriction endonuclease isolated was HindII. Its action depended on a specific DNA nucleotide sequence.
  2. The name EcoRI comes from the genus Escherichia, the species coli, the strain RY13, and the order of discovery (I).
  3. EcoRI recognises the sequence GAATTC and cuts between G and A.
  4. The cut fragments are separated by gel electrophoresis. DNA is negatively charged and moves toward the anode.
  5. Agarose gel is the common matrix. Ethidium bromide staining and UV light make DNA bands visible as orange coloured bands.
  6. Separated bands are cut from the gel and extracted. This step is called elution.

What features should a cloning vector have?

A good cloning vector needs an origin of replication, a selectable marker, and cloning sites. The origin of replication (ori) controls the copy number. The marker helps identify transformants. A unique recognition site lets the foreign DNA be inserted.

Selection of transformants once relied on antibiotic resistance genes. In pBR322, the BamHI site lies in the tetracycline resistance gene. A recombinant plasmid loses tetracycline resistance but keeps ampicillin resistance. This process is called insertional inactivation.

Modern vectors use a colour test instead. A recombinant plasmid inactivates the gene for beta-galactosidase. Colonies with the recombinant plasmid stay colourless. Colonies without an insert turn blue in a chromogenic substrate. This is called blue-white selection.

How is recombinant DNA introduced into a host cell?

DNA is a hydrophilic molecule, so it cannot pass through the cell membrane easily. The host cell is made competent, meaning ready to take up DNA. Common methods are chemical treatment, heat shock, microinjection, and biolistics.

MethodHow it works
Calcium chloride and heat shockCells are treated with calcium ions, then kept on ice, placed at 42°C briefly, and returned to ice
MicroinjectionRecombinant DNA is injected directly into the nucleus of an animal cell

What are the steps in the process of recombinant DNA technology?

The process runs in order: isolation of DNA, cutting with restriction enzymes, amplification of the gene using PCR, insertion into a vector, transfer into a host, culturing of host cells, and extraction of the product. Each step has its own technique.

  1. Isolate the DNA. Cells are broken open using lysozyme (bacteria), cellulase (plants) or chitinase (fungi). RNA is removed with ribonuclease and proteins with protease. Cold ethanol then precipitates the DNA as fine threads.
  2. Cut the DNA and the vector with the same restriction enzyme.
  3. Amplify the gene of interest using PCR.
  4. Ligate the gene into the vector with DNA ligase.
  5. Transfer the recombinant DNA into a competent host.
  6. Culture the host cells in a bioreactor and obtain the product.

How does PCR amplify a gene?

PCR is the polymerase chain reaction. It makes many copies of a gene in vitro using two sets of primers, a thermostable DNA polymerase, and nucleotides. Each cycle has three steps: denaturation, annealing, and extension.

  1. Denaturation: The double-stranded DNA is heated at about 94°C to separate the strands.
  2. Annealing: Primers attach to the complementary regions of the template strands.
  3. Extension: Taq polymerase adds nucleotides to the primers and makes new strands.

Taq polymerase comes from the bacterium Thermus aquaticus. It remains active at the high temperature used in denaturation. This is why it is preferred for PCR. The gene can be amplified many times with repeated cycles.

How does a bioreactor produce the desired product?

A bioreactor is a large vessel in which raw materials are converted into products using microbes, plants, animal or human cells. It provides controlled temperature, pH, substrate, salts, vitamins, and oxygen. This supports large-scale growth of cells.

Two common types appear in NCERT. The simple stirred tank reactor has a stirrer that mixes the contents and distributes oxygen. The sparged stirred tank reactor sends sterile air bubbles through the medium to improve oxygen availability. Bioreactors usually have an agitator system, an oxygen delivery system, a foam control system, a temperature control system, a pH control system, and a sampling port.

After synthesis, the product goes through downstream processing. This includes separation and purification, then formulation with preservatives and clinical trials where needed. Quality control tests follow before marketing.

What common mistakes should students avoid?

Students often confuse similar terms and miss small details. Revise these points before the exam.

  1. Confusing exonucleases with endonucleases. Exonucleases remove nucleotides from the ends, and endonucleases cut inside the DNA.
  2. Mixing up the BamHI site and the ampicillin resistance gene in pBR322.
  3. Forgetting that DNA moves toward the anode in electrophoresis.
  4. Writing the wrong enzyme for breaking cell walls in bacteria, plants, and fungi.
  5. Mixing up denaturation, annealing, and extension in PCR.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in this chapter for NEET UG?

Focus on restriction enzymes, cloning vectors, pBR322, competent host methods, PCR, bioreactors, and downstream processing. Questions often test enzyme names, vector features, and the order of steps.

FAQ 2: Why is EcoRI named so?

The letters E and co come from Escherichia coli, R comes from the strain RY13, and I was the first enzyme isolated from that strain.

FAQ 3: What is insertional inactivation?

It is the loss of a marker gene function when foreign DNA is inserted into it. In pBR322, inserting DNA at the BamHI site inactivates the tetracycline resistance gene.

FAQ 4: Why is Taq polymerase used in PCR?

Taq polymerase is thermostable, so it stays active during the high-temperature denaturation step. This allows repeated cycles without adding fresh enzymes.

FAQ 5: What is the difference between a stirred tank and a sparged stirred tank bioreactor?

A stirred tank uses a stirrer to mix and distribute oxygen. A sparged stirred tank also bubbles sterile air through the medium for better oxygen supply.

FAQ 6: How should students study this chapter for NEET?

Read NCERT carefully, make tables for enzymes and vectors, draw the PCR cycle, and solve past NEET questions. Revise weak topics every few days.
