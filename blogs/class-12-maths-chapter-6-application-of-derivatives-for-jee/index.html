Class 12 Maths Chapter 6: Application of Derivatives for JEE

Application of Derivatives uses differentiation to study change. It tells where a function rises or falls and where it reaches maximum or minimum values. In Class 12 Maths Chapter 6: Application of Derivatives, JEE Main aspirants need to understand monotonicity, tangents, extrema, and optimization. Regular practice with formulas builds speed and accuracy.

This chapter belongs to Calculus, a major unit of JEE Main Mathematics. The guide lists the topics, core formulas, worked examples, practice questions, and common mistakes. Formulas are standard results from NCERT and JEE preparation books.

Which formulas must students remember?

Remember the derivative-based conditions for monotonicity and extrema, the tangent and normal equations, the differential approximation, and the two mean value theorems. The table below collects them for quick revision.

Both mean value theorems need continuity on [a, b] and differentiability on (a, b).

How do you solve a maxima and minima question step by step?

Differentiate the function, solve f′(x) = 0 to find critical points, then apply the second derivative test. Compare function values at endpoints when the interval is closed. State the final value, not only the point.

  1. Write the quantity as a function of one variable.
  2. Find f′(x) and solve f′(x) = 0.
  3. Check the sign of f″(x) at each critical point.
  4. If f″(c) = 0, use the first derivative test.
  5. On a closed interval, compare values at critical points and endpoints.

Example 1. Find the local extrema of f(x) = 2x³ − 9x² + 12x + 5. Here f′(x) = 6(x − 1)(x − 2), so the critical points are x = 1 and x = 2. Since f″(x) = 12x − 18, f″(1) = −6 gives a local maximum of 10. Also f″(2) = 6 gives a local minimum of 9.

Which worked examples show tangents, normals, and rates?

Tangent and normal questions need the point, the slope f′(x₁), and the line equations. Rate questions need a relation between quantities, then differentiation with respect to time. These two examples show both methods.

Example 2. Find the tangent and normal to y = x² + 3x at x = 1. The point is (1, 4) and the slope is 2x + 3 = 5. The tangent is y = 5x − 1. The normal is x + 5y − 21 = 0.

Example 3. The radius of a circle grows at 2 cm per second. Find the rate of change of area when r = 5 cm. Here dA/dt = 2πr × dr/dt = 2π × 5 × 2 = 20π cm² per second.

Which mistakes lose marks in this chapter?

Most lost marks come from treating every critical point as an extremum, ignoring endpoints, and making sign errors in slopes. Check domain, interval, and units before finalizing each answer.

  1. Critical point confusion. For f(x) = x³, f′(0) = 0, yet there is no extremum at 0.
  2. Skipped endpoints. On a closed interval, the absolute maximum may occur at an endpoint.
  3. Normal slope errors. The normal slope is the negative reciprocal of the tangent slope.
  4. Ignoring f″(c) = 0. The second derivative test fails here, so use the first derivative test.
  5. Wrong units. Rate answers need units such as cm² per second.
  6. Missing domain checks. A critical point outside the domain is not valid.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: Is Application of Derivatives important for JEE Main?

Yes. It belongs to Calculus and supports graph and integration problems. Official chapter weightage is not published, so use previous years' papers to judge question patterns.

FAQ 2: Which formulas should students learn first?

Begin with tangent and normal equations, monotonicity conditions, the second derivative test, and both mean value theorems. Then add differential approximation and rate formulas.

FAQ 3: How can students practise maxima and minima questions?

Start with polynomial functions, then move to trigonometric and word problems. Always check endpoints on closed intervals and confirm the result with the second derivative.

FAQ 4: Does f′(c) = 0 always give a maximum or minimum?

No. It only gives a critical point. For f(x) = x³, the derivative is zero at 0, but the function has no extremum there.

FAQ 5: Are tangents, normals, and approximations asked in board exams?

Coverage can change with CBSE syllabus revisions. Check the latest CBSE syllabus and your textbook, and prepare these topics anyway for JEE Main.

FAQ 6: How should students use previous year questions?

Solve them after finishing each subtopic. Sort mistakes by type, such as sign errors or skipped endpoints, and revisit those concepts before the next timed test.
