What Are Chapter-Wise MCQs in Class 10 Maths?

Chapter-wise MCQs are multiple-choice questions organized according to each chapter in the CBSE Class 10 Maths syllabus. Each question has four options, and you must select the correct one. Since 2020, CBSE has increased the number of MCQs in board exams. For the 2027 exams, students can expect a significant portion of marks from objective-type questions, including MCQs, assertion-reasoning, and case-based questions.

Practicing Class 10 Maths MCQs chapter by chapter allows you to:

  1. Cover every topic systematically
  2. Understand which concepts are frequently tested
  3. Build accuracy and time management skills
  4. Score full marks in the objective section

Which Chapters Are Most Important for MCQs?

Not all chapters carry equal marks. Based on the CBSE marking scheme and previous year trends, here is a breakdown of high weightage chapters:

High Weightage Chapters

  1. Real Numbers: HCF, LCM, and irrational number proofs
  2. Polynomials: Zeroes and their relationship with coefficients
  3. Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables: Graphical and algebraic methods
  4. Quadratic Equations: Discriminant and nature of roots
  5. Arithmetic Progressions: nth term and sum of n terms
  6. Triangles: Similarity theorems and BPT
  7. Coordinate Geometry: Distance, section, and midpoint formulas
  8. Trigonometry and Its Applications: Ratios, identities, and height and distance
  9. Surface Areas and Volumes: Combination of solids
  10. Statistics and Probability: Mean, median, mode, and basic probability

Quick Tip

Start with chapters that carry the most marks. Once you are comfortable, move to the remaining ones. This approach saves time and builds momentum early in your preparation.

How to Practice MCQs Effectively for the 2027 Exams?

Simply reading questions and checking answers is not enough. You need a focused strategy.

Step 1: Study the Concept First

Before attempting MCQs from any chapter, read the NCERT textbook carefully. Understand definitions, formulas, and solved examples.

Step 2: Solve Without Looking at Options

Try to solve the question on your own before looking at the four choices. This builds genuine problem-solving ability.

Step 3: Time Yourself

Set a timer while practicing. Aim to solve each MCQ within 60 to 90 seconds. Board exams reward speed along with accuracy.

Step 4: Review Mistakes

After every practice session, go through the questions you got wrong. Write down the correct method in a separate notebook. This mistake log becomes a powerful revision tool before exams.

Step 5: Take Full-Length Mock Tests

Once you finish chapter-wise practice, attempt full syllabus mock tests. This builds exam-day stamina and helps you manage the three-hour duration confidently.

What Common Mistakes Should Students Avoid?

Even well-prepared students lose marks because of avoidable errors. Here are the most frequent mistakes in MCQ sections:

  1. Misreading the question: Always read the full question twice before selecting an answer.
  2. Skipping negative or fractional answers: Many correct options include negative values or fractions. Do not dismiss them.
  3. Not simplifying fully: Partial calculations lead to selecting a distractor option that looks close to the correct answer.
  4. Guessing without elimination: If you must guess, first eliminate two clearly wrong options. This doubles your chance of picking the right one.
  5. Ignoring units and conditions: Questions about surface area, volume, or probability often include specific conditions. Missing them leads to wrong answers.
  6. Over-relying on shortcuts: Shortcuts are helpful, but only when you understand the underlying concept. Build the foundation first.

MCQ practice benefits go beyond just the objective section. Here is how it helps across the entire exam:

  3. Confidence building: Getting quick answers right builds positive momentum during the exam.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How many MCQs are expected in the CBSE Class 10 Maths 2027 exam?

Based on recent patterns, CBSE includes around 20 objective-type questions, which cover MCQs, assertion reasoning, and case-based questions. The exact number will be confirmed in the official sample paper released by CBSE.

2. Is NCERT enough for MCQ preparation?

NCERT is the most important resource and covers the core syllabus. However, practicing additional MCQs from exemplar books and sample papers gives you wider exposure to different question formats.

3. How much time should I spend on MCQ practice daily?

Spending 20 to 30 minutes daily on MCQs from one chapter is a good habit. Over a few months, this adds up to thousands of questions and thorough coverage of the syllabus.

4. Are MCQs easier than subjective questions?

MCQs test specific concepts in a focused way. While they may seem quicker to answer, tricky distractors can catch you off guard. Proper practice is necessary to avoid losing easy marks.

5. Can I prepare for MCQs using mobile apps?

Yes, several trusted apps and websites offer chapter-wise MCQs with instant solutions. Make sure the app follows the latest CBSE syllabus and provides accurate explanations for every answer.

6. What is the best strategy to attempt MCQs during the exam?

Read each question carefully, solve it mentally or on rough paper before looking at the options, and eliminate clearly wrong answers first. Never leave an MCQ unanswered, as there is usually no negative marking in CBSE board exams.
