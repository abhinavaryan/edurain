Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Current: NEET

A changing magnetic flux induces an emf. This one idea explains generators, braking systems and the behaviour of alternating current circuits. This guide on Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Current covers Faraday's law, Lenz's law, eddy currents, RMS values, reactance, impedance, resonance and power. Each formula comes with its condition and a worked numerical.

What Is Electromagnetic Induction and How Does a Changing Magnetic Flux Produce an emf?

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an emf in a coil or conductor when the magnetic flux linked with it changes. The change may come from field strength, area or orientation.

Magnetic flux is Φ = BA cosθ, where θ is the angle between the field B and the normal to the area A. Its unit is the weber (Wb). A steady flux gives no emf. Only a changing flux does.

Faraday's Law and Lenz's Law: How Do You Find the Size and Direction of the Induced emf?

Faraday's law says the induced emf equals the negative rate of change of flux linked with a coil. For N turns, e = −N dΦ/dt.

Lenz's law supplies the negative sign. The induced current flows in the direction that opposes the change producing it. This follows from conservation of energy.

LawStatementWhat it tells you
Faraday's lawe = −N dΦ/dtMagnitude of the induced emf
Lenz's lawInduced current opposes the change in fluxDirection of the induced current

Suppose a north pole moves towards a loop. The face of the loop towards the magnet becomes a north pole and repels it. Ask what the loop must do to resist the change.

Motional emf: How Is an emf Induced in a Rod Moving Through a Magnetic Field?

Motional emf is the emf induced in a conductor moving through a magnetic field. It equals e = Blv when B, l and v are mutually perpendicular.

A rod sliding on rails changes the enclosed area, so the flux changes. With circuit resistance R, the current is I = Blv/R. The magnetic force on the rod is F = BIl, and it opposes the motion.

Worked example: A rod of length 0.4 m moves at 5 m/s in a field of 0.5 T. The circuit resistance is 2 Ω.

  1. Emf: e = Blv = 0.5 × 0.4 × 5 = 1 V
  2. Current: I = 1/2 = 0.5 A
  3. Opposing force: F = BIl = 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.1 N
  4. Mechanical power: Fv = 0.1 × 5 = 0.5 W, which equals I²R = 0.25 × 2 = 0.5 W

The two powers match, which confirms energy conservation.

Eddy Currents: What Are They and Why Do They Matter in Cores and Braking Systems?

Eddy currents are circulating induced currents formed inside a bulk conductor when the flux through it changes. By Lenz's law, they oppose the change that creates them.

They heat metal cores and waste energy in machines. Laminated cores reduce this loss because thin insulated sheets break up the current loops.

Eddy currents are also useful. Induction furnaces, electromagnetic braking and galvanometer damping all depend on them.

Self Inductance: Why Does a Coil Oppose Any Change in Its Own Current?

Self inductance is the property of a coil by which it opposes a change in its own current. The induced emf is e = −L dI/dt, and L is measured in henry (H).

The energy stored in a coil is U = ½LI². Treat this as a supporting concept, because it explains inductive reactance. Check the official notification for how inductance is listed in the syllabus.

Alternating Current and RMS Value: What Do Peak, Average and Effective Values Mean?

An alternating current changes its magnitude and direction periodically. For a sinusoidal source, i = I₀ sin ωt, where ω = 2πf and I₀ is the peak value.

The RMS value is the steady direct current that gives the same heating effect in a resistor. For a sine wave, I_rms = I₀/√2 ≈ 0.707 I₀. The same relation holds for voltage.

The average over a full cycle is zero. Over a half cycle it is 2I₀/π, about 0.637 I₀.

Worked example: A supply is rated 220 V RMS. Its peak voltage is 220 × √2 ≈ 311 V. In NEET 2027 Online Preparation, always note whether a given value is peak or RMS before substituting.

Reactance and Impedance: How Do Resistors, Inductors and Capacitors Oppose Alternating Current?

Reactance is the opposition of an inductor or capacitor to alternating current. Impedance is the total opposition of a circuit that contains resistance and reactance.

ResistorRVoltage and current in phase
InductorX_L = ωL = 2πfLVoltage leads current by 90°
CapacitorX_C = 1/ωCCurrent leads voltage by 90°


For a series LCR circuit, Z = √[R² + (X_L − X_C)²]. The phase angle follows from tan φ = (X_L − X_C)/R.

Worked example: A series circuit has R = 30 Ω, X_L = 60 Ω and X_C = 20 Ω. The source is 100 V RMS.

  1. Net reactance: 60 − 20 = 40 Ω
  2. Impedance: Z = √(30² + 40²) = 50 Ω
  3. Current: I = 100/50 = 2 A
  4. Phase angle: tan φ = 40/30, so φ ≈ 53°, and voltage leads current

LCR Series Circuit and Resonance: When Does the Current in the Circuit Become Maximum?

Resonance occurs in a series LCR circuit when X_L equals X_C. The impedance then drops to its minimum value, R, and the current is maximum.

The resonant angular frequency is ω₀ = 1/√(LC). The resonant frequency is f₀ = 1/(2π√LC). At resonance, I_max = V/R, and voltage and current are in phase.

Worked example: Let L = 0.1 H and C = 10 μF = 10⁻⁵ F.

  1. LC = 10⁻⁶, so √(LC) = 10⁻³
  2. ω₀ = 1/10⁻³ = 1000 rad/s
  3. f₀ = 1000/(2π) ≈ 159 Hz

During NEET 2027 Online Preparation, redraw the phasor diagram from memory. It shows why the phase angle changes sign on either side of resonance.

Power in an AC Circuit and Wattless Current: Why Can Current Flow Without Using Power?

Average power in an AC circuit is P = V_rms I_rms cos φ. The factor cos φ is the power factor, and for a series LCR circuit it equals R/Z.

In the earlier example, P = 100 × 2 × (30/50) = 120 W. The check I²R = 4 × 30 = 120 W agrees.

In a pure inductor or capacitor, φ = 90°, so cos φ = 0 and the average power is zero. The current is then called wattless current. Energy moves into the element and returns to the source every cycle.

Common Mistakes Students Make in Induction and Alternating Current Numericals

Most errors come from direction, phase and RMS confusion. Check these before you finalise any answer.

  1. Using e = Blv at any angle: B, l and v must be mutually perpendicular. Otherwise use the perpendicular component.
  2. Reading Lenz's law as opposing the flux: It opposes the change in flux, not the flux itself.
  3. Swapping phase leads: Voltage leads current in an inductor. Current leads voltage in a capacitor.
  4. Adding R and X directly: Impedance needs the square root formula.
  5. Using peak values for power: Average power uses RMS values and cos φ.
  6. Skipping unit conversion: Convert μF and mH before using ω₀ = 1/√(LC).
  7. Calling wattless current zero current: The current exists. Only the average power is zero.

Key Takeaways: Concepts and Formulas to Remember for NEET Physics

  1. Induced emf depends on the rate of change of flux, and Lenz's law gives its direction.
  2. For a moving rod, e = Blv, and mechanical power equals electrical power.
  3. I_rms = I₀/√2 and V_rms = V₀/√2 for a sinusoidal supply.
  4. Z = √[R² + (X_L − X_C)²], and tan φ = (X_L − X_C)/R.
  5. Resonance occurs at f₀ = 1/(2π√LC), where Z = R and the current is maximum.
  6. Average power is V_rms I_rms cos φ, and it is zero in a pure inductor or capacitor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is the Difference Between the Peak Value and the RMS Value of Alternating Current?

The peak value is the maximum instantaneous value. The RMS value is the steady equivalent that gives the same heating effect. For a sine wave, RMS equals peak divided by √2.

How Do I Find the Direction of Induced Current Using Lenz's Law in a Numerical?

First decide whether the flux through the loop is rising or falling. The induced current creates a field that opposes that change. Then use the right hand rule to get the current direction.

Why Is the Average Power Zero in a Pure Inductor or a Pure Capacitor?

Voltage and current differ in phase by 90°, so cos φ is zero. The element stores energy in one quarter cycle and returns it in the next. No net energy is used.

What Happens to Impedance and Current at Resonance in a Series LCR Circuit?

Impedance falls to its minimum value, equal to R, because X_L and X_C cancel. The current becomes maximum and stays in phase with the applied voltage.

Why Are Eddy Currents Reduced by Using a Laminated Core?

Lamination splits the bulk conductor into thin insulated sheets. This limits the size of the circulating current loops. The heating loss then becomes much smaller.

Why Does a Capacitor Block Direct Current but Allow Alternating Current to Pass?

Capacitive reactance is X_C = 1/ωC. For direct current, ω is zero, so the reactance is infinitely large. For alternating current, the capacitor charges and discharges every cycle, so current flows in the circuit.
