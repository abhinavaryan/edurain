CBSE Class 10 Maths Coordinate Geometry: Important Formulas and Questions | EduRain Blog

Coordinate Geometry is a scoring unit in the Class 10 board paper, because most questions depend on a few clear formulas. This guide explains the distance formula, the section formula and the midpoint formula. It also covers solved questions and common mistakes. It suits students revising CBSE Class 10 Maths Coordinate Geometry from the NCERT textbook.

Coordinate Geometry Chapter Overview for Class 10

Coordinate Geometry in CBSE Class 10 Maths uses the Cartesian plane to find distances between points and the coordinates of a dividing point. The chapter focuses on the distance formula, section formula and their use in geometry problems.

The chapter is Chapter 7 in the NCERT Class 10 Maths textbook. Recent CBSE marking schemes have given this unit about 6 marks out of 80 in the theory paper. Marks and syllabus details can change, so confirm them in the current year's CBSE syllabus and sample paper.

Important Concepts and Subtopics to Master

Distance Formula for Two Points

The distance formula gives the length between points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) as √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]. It comes from the Pythagoras theorem and is used for sides, diagonals and equal distance problems.

For a point (x, y), the distance from the origin is √(x² + y²). Use this when one of the points is (0, 0).

Section Formula and Midpoint Formula

The section formula finds the point dividing a line segment internally in the ratio m:n. The midpoint is the special case where m and n are equal, so each coordinate becomes the simple average of the two ends.

Here, m is the part nearer the first point, and n is the part nearer the second. Keep this order fixed, because swapping it gives the wrong point.

Using Coordinates to Identify Figures

Coordinate methods prove the type of a triangle or quadrilateral by comparing side lengths, diagonals and midpoints. Equal sides, equal diagonals or a common midpoint of the diagonals identify figures such as isosceles triangles, rectangles and parallelograms.

  1. Parallelogram: the diagonals share the same midpoint.
  2. Rectangle: opposite sides are equal and the diagonals are equal.
  3. Rhombus: all four sides are equal.
  4. Square: all four sides are equal and the diagonals are equal.
  5. Collinear points: the sum of two distances equals the third.

Important Question Types with Solved Examples

Board questions from this chapter mostly ask for a distance, a point equidistant from two points, a ratio of division, or the type of a figure. The examples below follow these patterns.

Example 1: Find the distance between (2, 3) and (4, 1).

d = √[(4 − 2)² + (1 − 3)²] = √(4 + 4) = √8 = 2√2 units.

Example 2: Find a point on the x axis equidistant from (2, −5) and (−2, 9).

Let the point be (x, 0). Then (x − 2)² + 25 = (x + 2)² + 81. This simplifies to −4x + 25 = 4x + 81, so x = −7. The point is (−7, 0).

Example 3: Find the point dividing (4, −3) and (8, 5) internally in the ratio 3:1.

x = (3 × 8 + 1 × 4)/4 = 7 and y = (3 × 5 + 1 × (−3))/4 = 3. The point is (7, 3).

Board papers may include objective, short answer and case based questions from this unit. Practise all three types.

Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most errors come from sign mistakes, a wrong ratio order or a missing square root. Writing each substitution in brackets and checking the final answer prevents these avoidable losses of marks.

  1. Dropping brackets, so (−2)² is written as −4 instead of 4.
  2. Swapping m and n in the section formula.
  3. Forgetting the square root in the distance formula.
  4. Subtracting coordinates in mixed order, such as x₂ − x₁ with y₁ − y₂.
  5. Naming a figure a square without checking both the sides and the diagonals.
  6. Leaving the answer as a decimal when the question expects a surd.

Step by Step Preparation Strategy

Prepare this unit by learning the formulas, solving NCERT exercises and then practising board style questions. A regular weekly revision cycle keeps formulas fresh and speeds up calculation.

Students working on CBSE Class 10 Maths Coordinate Geometry can follow these steps.

  1. Read the NCERT chapter and understand how the distance formula comes from the Pythagoras theorem.
  2. Write all formulas on one page, with a sample question beside each.
  3. Solve every NCERT exercise question without looking at the solution first.
  4. Practise figure identification questions, such as proving a parallelogram or a rhombus.
  5. Attempt sample papers and previous year questions on a timer.
  6. Keep an error log and review it every week.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: Which formulas are most important in Class 10 Coordinate Geometry?

The distance formula, section formula and midpoint formula are the most important. Also remember the distance from the origin and the collinearity condition AB + BC = AC.

FAQ 2: How many marks does this unit carry in the CBSE Class 10 board exam?

Recent marking schemes have allotted about 6 marks out of 80 to Coordinate Geometry. Check the current year's sample paper, because marking schemes can change.

FAQ 3: How do I find the ratio in which a point divides a line segment?

Assume the ratio is k:1 and apply the section formula to one coordinate. Equate it to the given coordinate of the point and solve for k.

FAQ 4: How can I prove that four points form a parallelogram or a rectangle?

For a parallelogram, show that both diagonals have the same midpoint. For a rectangle, show that opposite sides are equal and the two diagonals are equal.

FAQ 5: Is the area of a triangle part of this chapter now?

Syllabus revisions have affected this topic. Check the latest CBSE syllabus and NCERT textbook for your exam year before including it in your preparation.

FAQ 6: How can I practise Coordinate Geometry questions effectively?

Solve all NCERT exercises first, then move to sample papers and previous year questions. Time yourself and note each mistake, especially sign errors and ratio order.
