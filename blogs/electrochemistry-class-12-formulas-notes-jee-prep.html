Electrochemistry Class 12: NCERT Solutions, Important Formulas, Solved Numericals & Important Questions for JEE Main 2027 | EduRain Blog

Electrochemistry Class 12 explains how chemical reactions produce electricity and how electricity drives chemical change. For JEE Main 2027, focus on electrode potential, the Nernst equation, conductivity, Kohlrausch's law, and Faraday's laws. This guide covers NCERT-based concepts, formulas, solved numericals, and practice questions in a clear order.

Syllabus note: JEE Main follows the official NTA syllabus. Confirm the 2027 syllabus from the NTA notice when it is released.

Important Topics of Electrochemistry for JEE and NEET

Key topics are galvanic and electrolytic cells, electrode potential, the Nernst equation, conductance, Kohlrausch's law, Faraday's laws, batteries, fuel cells, and corrosion. Both JEE Main and NEET test these areas.

What Are the Important Formulas of Electrochemistry in Class 12?

Important formulas include E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode, ΔG° = −nFE°cell, the Nernst equation, κ = G × (l/A), Λm = κ × 1000 / M, Kohlrausch's law, and m = (M/nF) × I × t. Always check units.

These formulas are the basis for most numericals in the chapter.

Class 12 Electrochemistry NCERT Solutions

To solve NCERT questions, identify the cell or process, write the half-reactions, choose the formula, substitute values with units, and check the sign. Practise in-text and exercise questions before JEE-level problems.

Take the reaction Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu. Zinc oxidises at the anode. Copper ions reduce at the cathode. Subtract the anode potential from the cathode potential to get E°cell. This pattern repeats in many NCERT solutions for this chapter.

How to Solve Electrochemistry Numericals in Class 12

Write the given data, convert units, select the formula, substitute carefully, and check the sign and unit of the answer. Most errors come from the value of n, unit conversion, and the reaction quotient.

These solved numericals cover common patterns.

Example 1: Nernst equation. A Daniell cell has E°cell = 1.10 V. Here [Zn²⁺] = 0.1 M and [Cu²⁺] = 0.01 M. Find Ecell.

  1. Q = [Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺] = 0.1/0.01 = 10 and n = 2.
  2. Ecell = 1.10 − (0.0591/2) × log 10 = 1.10 − 0.02955.
  3. Ecell ≈ 1.07 V.

Example 2: Molar conductivity. The conductivity of 0.20 M KCl is 0.0248 S cm⁻¹. Find Λm.

  1. Λm = (0.0248 × 1000)/0.20 = 124 S cm² mol⁻¹.

Example 3: Gibbs energy. Find ΔG° for the Daniell cell using E°cell = 1.10 V and n = 2.

  1. ΔG° = −2 × 96500 × 1.10 = −212300 J mol⁻¹.
  2. ΔG° ≈ −212.3 kJ mol⁻¹.

Example 4: Faraday's law. A current of 2 A flows for 30 minutes through a Cu²⁺ solution. Find the mass of copper deposited (M = 63.5 g mol⁻¹).

  1. Q = 2 × 1800 = 3600 C, and n = 2.
  2. m = (63.5 × 3600)/(2 × 96500) ≈ 1.18 g.

Students who join JEE online coaching classes should still practise these patterns on their own until each step becomes routine.

Common Mistakes and Important Practice Questions

Common mistakes include using the wrong n in the Nernst equation, forgetting the 1000 factor in Λm, multiplying E° by coefficients, and mixing up the anode and cathode. Targeted practice removes these errors.

MistakeCorrect approach
Multiplying E° by stoichiometric coefficientsE° is an intensive property, so do not multiply it
Using kJ and J togetherConvert ΔG° and F × E to the same unit
Including pure solids in QLeave out pure solids and liquids
Wrong sign after reversing a half-reactionReverse the sign of E° when you reverse the reaction
Skipping the 1000 factor in ΛmConvert M from mol L⁻¹ to mol cm⁻³

Practice questions

  1. Find E°cell for a cell made of Zn²⁺/Zn (−0.76 V) and Ag⁺/Ag (+0.80 V). Answer: 1.56 V.
  2. How much charge is deposited by 1 mole of aluminium from Al³⁺? Answer: 3F = 289500 C (using F = 96500).
  3. Why does aqueous KCl not give potassium on electrolysis? Answer: Water is reduced more easily at the cathode, so hydrogen forms.
  4. Why does the molar conductivity of a weak electrolyte rise sharply on dilution? Answer: Dissociation increases, so more ions are available.

Key Takeaways

  1. Learn the Nernst equation, ΔG° = −nFE°cell, and the conductivity formulas with their units.
  2. Never multiply E° by stoichiometric coefficients.
  3. Write half-reactions before substituting values, and keep the sign of E° consistent.
  4. Finish NCERT examples and exercises before attempting advanced problems.
  5. Use previous year questions and timed tests to find weak areas.
  6. Confirm the syllabus from official NTA and NCERT sources, not from estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in Electrochemistry for JEE Main?

Focus on electrode potential, the Nernst equation, Gibbs energy and EMF, conductivity, Kohlrausch's law, and Faraday's laws. Batteries, fuel cells, and corrosion are also useful for theory questions.

FAQ 2: Which formulas should students remember for this chapter?

Remember E°cell, ΔG° = −nFE°cell, the Nernst equation, κ, Λm, Kohlrausch's law, α = Λm/Λ°m, and m = (M/nF) × I × t. Learn the units with each formula.

FAQ 3: How can students practise numerical questions from this chapter?

Start with NCERT examples, then solve topic-wise problems, and finish with JEE Main previous year questions. Write each step and check units before comparing your answer.

FAQ 4: Is NCERT enough for Electrochemistry in JEE Main?

NCERT builds the base and is essential. JEE Main numericals may need extra practice, so add previous year questions and mixed problem sets after completing the textbook.

FAQ 5: Which value of the Faraday constant should be used in numericals?

The Faraday constant is about 96485 C mol⁻¹ and is often rounded to 96500. Use the value given in the question to avoid small differences in the answer.

FAQ 6: What is the difference between a galvanic cell and an electrolytic cell?

A galvanic cell converts chemical energy into electrical energy through a spontaneous reaction. An electrolytic cell uses external electrical energy to drive a nonspontaneous reaction. In both cells, oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction occurs at the cathode.
