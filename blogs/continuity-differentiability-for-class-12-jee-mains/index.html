Continuity & Differentiability for Class 12 & JEE Mains 2027 | EduRain Blog

Every graph in this chapter answers two questions. Does it break anywhere, and does it turn sharply anywhere? This guide explains Continuity & Differentiability with concepts, high-weightage subtopics, formulas, solved important questions, a study plan, and common mistakes for Class 12 and JEE Mains 2027.

Chapter Overview for Class 12 and JEE Mains

This chapter studies when a function has no gaps and no sharp corners. Continuity means the graph has no breaks. Differentiability means a unique tangent exists at each point.

Class 12 builds the base, and JEE Mains adds tricky functions. The chapter stays in the rationalized NCERT Class 12 Mathematics syllabus. Check the NTA notification for any 2027 changes.

Important Concepts

Four ideas drive every question: continuity, types of discontinuity, differentiability, and their relation. Master these definitions first, because each problem reduces to checking limits and derivatives at one point.

ConceptMeaningQuick Test
Continuity at a pointNo break at that pointLeft limit, right limit, and value are equal
Removable discontinuityLimit exists but differs from the valueRedefine the value to fix it
Jump discontinuityLeft and right limits differBoth limits are finite but unequal
DifferentiabilityUnique slope at the pointLeft and right derivatives are equal
RelationDifferentiable implies continuousThe reverse is false


Sums, differences, products, and composites of continuous functions stay continuous. A quotient stays continuous where the denominator is not zero.

Important Formulas and Theorems

Nine results cover most questions in this chapter. They include the definitions of continuity and the derivative, standard limits, differentiation rules, and the two mean value theorems. Memorize them with their conditions.

ResultStatement
Continuity at x = aLimit as x approaches a equals f(a)
Derivative at x = af'(a) = limit of [f(a + h) − f(a)] / h as h approaches 0
Standard limitssin x / x, tan x / x, (e^x − 1) / x, log(1 + x) / x all tend to 1
Product rule(uv)' = u'v + uv'
Quotient rule(u/v)' = (u'v − uv') / v²
Chain ruled/dx f(g(x)) = f'(g(x)) · g'(x)
Parametric formdy/dx = (dy/dt) / (dx/dt)
Rolle's theoremIf f(a) = f(b), then f'(c) = 0 for some c in (a, b)
Lagrange's theoremf'(c) = [f(b) − f(a)] / (b − a) for some c in (a, b)


Also revise the derivatives of inverse trigonometric functions. They appear in many composite questions.

Important Questions with Solutions

These five questions cover piecewise continuity, modulus differentiability, unknown constants, Rolle's theorem, and logarithmic differentiation. Solve each by hand before reading the answer.

  1. Find k so that f(x) = sin 3x / x for x ≠ 0 and f(0) = k is continuous at 0.
  2. The limit of sin 3x / x as x approaches 0 is 3. Set k = 3.
  3. Is f(x) = |x − 1| differentiable at x = 1?
  4. The left derivative is −1 and the right derivative is 1. They differ, so f is not differentiable at x = 1.
  5. Let f(x) = x² for x ≤ 1 and f(x) = ax + b for x > 1. Find a and b for differentiability at x = 1.
  6. Continuity gives a + b = 1. Equal derivatives give a = 2. So a = 2 and b = −1.
  7. Verify Rolle's theorem for f(x) = x² − 4x + 3 on [1, 3].
  8. The function is a polynomial, and f(1) = f(3) = 0. Solve f'(x) = 2x − 4 = 0 to get c = 2.
  9. Differentiate y = x^x.
  10. Take logarithms: log y = x log x. Differentiate to get dy/dx = x^x (1 + log x).

Common Mistakes Students Make in This Chapter

Students often treat Continuity & Differentiability as one idea. They are related but different. The errors below cost marks in both board exams and JEE Main. Check each item before submitting an answer.

  1. Checking only the left and right limits and forgetting the function value.
  2. Assuming a continuous function is always differentiable.
  3. Using the derivative formula at a breakpoint without checking one-sided derivatives.
  4. Misreading the greatest integer function graph near integers.
  5. Applying L'Hospital's rule when the form is not 0/0 or ∞/∞.
  6. Missing the inner derivative in chain rule and inverse trigonometric problems.
  7. Using Rolle's or Lagrange's theorem without verifying the conditions.

Key Takeaways

These points summarize the technical advice for this chapter.

  1. Continuity needs three equal quantities: left limit, right limit, and function value.
  2. Differentiable functions are always continuous, but continuous functions can have sharp corners.
  3. Modulus and greatest integer functions need a one-sided check at each breakpoint.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check continuity of a piecewise function at a point?

Find the left limit, the right limit, and the function value at that point. If all three are equal, the function is continuous there. Solve for unknown constants by equating them.

Is every continuous function differentiable?

No. A continuous function can have a sharp corner. The modulus function is continuous at zero but not differentiable there, because its left and right derivatives are unequal.

How do I test the differentiability of a modulus function?

Rewrite the function as separate expressions on each side of the breakpoint. Find the left and right derivatives at that point. The function is differentiable only when both are equal.

Which standard limits are used most in JEE Mains?

The most used limits are sin x / x, tan x / x, (e^x − 1) / x, and log(1 + x) / x. Each tends to 1 as x approaches 0.

When can I apply Rolle's theorem?

Apply it only when the function is continuous on the closed interval, differentiable on the open interval, and has equal values at both ends. Then f'(c) = 0 for some c.

How many questions come from this chapter in JEE Mains?

This chapter usually contributes one to three questions per paper. The exact number changes each year and shifts. Cover the full unit, since limits and derivatives also support other calculus questions.
