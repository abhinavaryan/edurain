Class 11 Biology Chapter 4 Animal Kingdom: Phylum-Wise Classification and NCERT Notes for NEET | EduRain Blog

Phylum wise classification depends on body symmetry, germ layers, coelom, segmentation, and notochord. NCERT groups animals into phyla from Porifera to Chordata. This guide covers Class 11 Biology Chapter 4 Animal Kingdom for NEET UG. It includes notes, tables, common mistakes, and practice questions.

What Is the Basis of Animal Classification in NCERT?

NCERT classifies animals using level of organisation, body symmetry, number of germ layers, type of coelom, segmentation, and notochord. These features decide where an animal fits in the Animal Kingdom Class 11 NCERT scheme.

FeatureTypesExample
Level of organisationCellular, tissue, organ, organ systemPorifera is cellular; Annelida has organ systems
SymmetryAsymmetrical, radial, bilateralSponges, Coelenterata, Annelida

CoelomAcoelomate, pseudocoelomate, coelomatePlatyhelminthes, Aschelminthes, Annelida
SegmentationMetameric bodyAnnelida
NotochordPresent or absentChordates and non chordates


A coelom is a body cavity lined by mesoderm. In a pseudocoelom, mesoderm appears as scattered pouches between ectoderm and endoderm.

What Does the Official Syllabus Say for NEET UG 2027?

Animal Kingdom belongs to the NCERT based NEET UG Biology syllabus. The latest official syllabus confirmed in sources checked is for NEET UG 2026. Students should follow official notices for NEET UG Exams 2027.

At the time of writing, no separate official 2027 syllabus was confirmed. This is an official status, not an expectation. Check neet.nta.nic.in and nmc.org.in before finalising your plan.

NTA does not publish chapter wise weightage. Any percentage you see online is an estimate from past paper analysis. Questions on NEET Biology Animal Kingdom are commonly based on NCERT facts and examples.

Which Features Define Non Chordate Phyla?

Non chordate phyla lack a notochord. NCERT covers Porifera, Coelenterata, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes, Aschelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, and Hemichordata. Each has distinct organisation, symmetry, and body cavity features.

What Should Students Remember About Phylum Chordata and Its Classes?

Chordates have a notochord, a dorsal hollow nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits, a post anal tail, and a ventral heart. They are divided into Urochordata, Cephalochordata, and Vertebrata.

In Urochordata, the notochord is present only in the larval tail. In Cephalochordata, it runs from head to tail throughout life. In Vertebrata, a vertebral column replaces it in adults.

Vertebrata includes jawless Cyclostomata and jawed Gnathostomata. Gnathostomata covers fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals.

Which Common Mistakes Cost Marks in This Chapter?

Students often mix up body cavity types, circulatory systems, and heart chambers. They also confuse similar examples. Comparison tables and NCERT based revision help avoid these errors.

  1. Hemichordata is a separate phylum in NCERT, not a subphylum of Chordata.
  2. Aschelminthes are pseudocoelomate, while Annelida are coelomate.
  3. Arthropoda have an open circulatory system, not a closed one.
  4. Nereis is dioecious, but earthworms and leeches are monoecious.
  5. Air bladder is present in Osteichthyes and absent in Chondrichthyes.
  6. Not all reptiles have a three chambered heart. Crocodiles have four chambers.
  7. Echinoderm adults are radially symmetrical, but their larvae are bilateral.

How Can You Prepare This Chapter Step by Step?

Read the NCERT chapter first, then build phylum tables, learn examples with features, and finish with timed practice. This order keeps facts linked, which helps in NEET style statement questions.

  1. Read Class 11 Biology Chapter 4 Animal Kingdom line by line and mark every feature word.
  2. Build one comparison table for non chordates and one for vertebrate classes.
  3. Learn each example with its common name and one key feature.
  4. Write your own Animal Kingdom NEET Notes from memory, then correct them using NCERT.
  5. Solve previous year NEET questions from this chapter and review every wrong option.
  6. Take a short chapter test each week until errors drop.

Which Practice Questions Test Phylum Wise Classification?

Good practice questions test phylum identification, body organisation, and examples. Try these five NCERT based questions and check the answer after each one. Write reasons in your own words for better recall.

  1. Which phylum has collar cells called choanocytes?
  2. Answer: Porifera.
  3. Cnidoblasts are characteristic of which phylum?
  4. Answer: Coelenterata (Cnidaria).
  5. Radula, a rasping organ, is found in which phylum?
  6. Answer: Mollusca.
  7. Which vertebrate class has placoid scales and no air bladder?
  8. Answer: Chondrichthyes.
  9. Which group has a four chambered heart: (a) Amphibia (b) Osteichthyes (c) Aves (d) Chondrichthyes?
  10. Answer: (c) Aves.

Key Takeaways

  1. Classification rests on symmetry, germ layers, coelom, segmentation, and notochord.
  2. Porifera are cellular, Coelenterata and Ctenophora are diploblastic, and later phyla are triploblastic.
  3. Aschelminthes are pseudocoelomate, while Annelida to Hemichordata are coelomate.
  4. Chordates are defined by notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, and paired pharyngeal gill slits.
  5. Heart chambers and air bladder are frequent points of confusion among vertebrate classes.
  6. Official syllabus details must be checked on NTA and NMC websites, not guessed.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: Which phyla should NEET students revise first?

Start with Chordata and Arthropoda because they carry the most classification details. However, every phylum in the NCERT chapter can be tested, so revise all of them with examples.

FAQ 2: Is Hemichordata a part of Chordata?

No. NCERT now treats Hemichordata as a separate phylum among non chordates. It was earlier placed as a subphylum of Chordata, which still causes confusion in older notes.

FAQ 3: What is the difference between Chondrichthyes and Osteichthyes?

Chondrichthyes have a cartilaginous skeleton, placoid scales, and no air bladder. Osteichthyes have a bony skeleton, an operculum covering the gills, and an air bladder.

FAQ 4: Which vertebrate classes have a four chambered heart?

Aves and Mammalia have a four chambered heart. Among reptiles, crocodiles also have four chambers, while most other reptiles have three.

FAQ 5: How can I remember examples from each phylum?

Make a table with phylum, scientific name, common name, and one key feature. Revise it in short daily sessions and test yourself by covering one column at a time.

FAQ 6: What is the difference between coelomate, pseudocoelomate, and acoelomate animals?

Coelomates, such as Annelida, have a true coelom lined by mesoderm. Pseudocoelomates, such as Aschelminthes, have mesoderm only as scattered pouches between ectoderm and endoderm. Acoelomates, such as Platyhelminthes, have no body cavity.
