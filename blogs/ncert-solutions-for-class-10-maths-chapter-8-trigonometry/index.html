What Does Chapter 8 Cover in Class 10 Maths?

Chapter 8, Introduction to Trigonometry, studies six ratios of an acute angle in a right-angled triangle. It also covers standard angle values, complementary angles, and basic identities. Chapter 9 then applies these ideas to heights and distances.

Which Formulas Must You Remember for Chapter 8?

Remember the six ratios, the values at 0, 30, 45, 60, and 90 degrees, the complementary angle rules, and three identities. These cover nearly every question in the chapter.

Six ratios for angle A:

  1. sin A = opposite/hypotenuse
  2. cos A = adjacent / hypotenuse
  3. tan A = opposite / adjacent = sin A / cos A
  4. cosec A, sec A, cot A are the reciprocals of sin A, cos A, tan A

Complementary angles: sin (90° − A) = cos A, cos (90° − A) = sin A, and tan (90° − A) = cot A. Also, sec (90° − A) = cosec A.

Identities:

  1. sin²A + cos²A = 1
  2. 1 + tan²A = sec²A
  3. 1 + cot²A = cosec²A

Which Important Questions Appear Often, and How Do You Solve Them?

Four question types repeat in tests: finding ratios from one ratio, evaluating expressions with standard values, using complementary angles, and proving identities. Practise these after the NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Maths: Chapter 8 Trigonometry for each exercise.

Question 1: Find sin A and sec A when tan A = 3/4

If tan A equals 3/4, then sin A equals 3/5 and sec A equals 5/4. Draw a right triangle with opposite side 3 and adjacent side 4. The hypotenuse follows from Pythagoras.

  1. Hypotenuse = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5.
  2. sin A = opposite / hypotenuse = 3/5.
  3. sec A = hypotenuse / adjacent = 5/4.

Question 2: Evaluate sin 60° cos 30° + sin 30° cos 60°

The value of this expression is 1. Replace each ratio with its standard value, multiply the pairs, and add. No identity is needed here.

  1. sin 60° cos 30° = (√3/2)(√3/2) = 3/4.
  2. sin 30° cos 60° = (1/2)(1/2) = 1/4.
  3. Sum = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1.

Question 3: Evaluate tan 15° tan 25° tan 30° tan 65° tan 75°

The value of this product is 1/√3. Pair the angles that add up to 90 degrees. Each such pair of tangents multiplies to 1.

  1. tan 75° = tan (90° − 15°) = cot 15°, so tan 15° tan 75° = 1.
  2. tan 65° = cot 25°, so tan 25° tan 65° = 1.
  3. Remaining term: tan 30° = 1/√3.

Question 4: Prove sec A (1 − sin A)(sec A + tan A) = 1

Start with the left side and convert everything to sin A and cos A. The expression then simplifies to 1 using sin²A + cos²A = 1.

  1. sec A + tan A = (1 + sin A) / cos A.
  2. (1 − sin A)(1 + sin A) = 1 − sin²A = cos²A.
  3. So the product is cos²A / cos A = cos A.
  4. Multiply by sec A: (1/cos A) × cos A = 1. Hence proved.

What Is the Best Step-by-Step Study Plan for This Chapter?

A good plan takes about one week. Learn the ratios first, then the values, then complementary angles, and finish with identities. Daily practice matters more than long sessions.

  1. Day 1: Draw triangles and write all six ratios for different angles.
  2. Day 2: Memorise the standard value table and solve evaluation questions.
  3. Day 3: Practise complementary angle questions until pairing feels natural.
  4. Day 4: Learn the three identities and derive each from sin²A + cos²A = 1.
  5. Day 5: Prove identities from NCERT, working on one side only.
  6. Day 6: Join CBSE Class 10 Online Classes or a session led by a teacher if a proof stays unclear.
  7. Day 7: Attempt a timed test and mark every wrong answer.

What Mistakes Do Students Make in This Chapter?

The most common errors come from side confusion and weak identity handling. Fix these early, since they cost marks in every test.

  1. Mixing opposite and adjacent sides for the chosen angle.
  2. Treating sin A as sin multiplied by A.
  3. Writing tan 90° as 0 or 1 instead of not defined.
  4. Confusing sin 30° with sin 60°, or cos 30° with cos 60°.
  5. Writing sin²A as sin A².
  6. Pairing sec with cosec incorrectly in complementary angle questions.
  7. Working on both sides together while proving an identity.
  8. Making errors while rationalising values with √2 and √3.

Key Takeaways

  1. Always label the opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides with respect to the given angle.
  2. Learn the standard value table by heart, since many questions use it directly.
  3. Pair complementary angles first when a product or sum has many angles.
  4. Prove identities by working on the left side until it matches the right side.
  5. Use CBSE Class 10 Online Classes as support for NCERT practice, not a replacement for it.
  6. Keep an error list and revise it before every test.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Which formulas are most important in Chapter 8?

The six ratios, the standard value table, the complementary angle rules, and sin²A + cos²A = 1 matter most. The other two identities follow from the last one.

2. What is the value of tan 90°?

It is not defined. Since tan 90° equals sin 90° divided by cos 90°, the denominator is zero.

3. How do I prove trigonometric identities easily?

Convert every term into sin and cos. Work on one side only, simplify step by step, and use sin²A + cos²A = 1 when needed.

4. How many marks does this chapter carry in the board exam?

The Trigonometry unit has carried around 12 marks along with Chapter 9. Check the latest CBSE marking scheme for the exact split.

5. Which NCERT questions should I solve first?

Start with solved examples, then the evaluation questions, then identity proofs. These patterns repeat most often in school and board papers.

6. How should I revise this chapter before a test?

Rewrite the value table and identities, then solve one question from each type. Review your error list last.
