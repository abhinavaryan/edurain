What Is Heredity and Evolution?

Heredity is the passing of traits from parents to offspring, and evolution is the gradual change in living organisms over generations. Together, these topics explain how living beings inherit features and how species change over time.

Key points to remember:

  1. Heredity works through genes present in chromosomes.
  2. Evolution happens through natural selection over long periods.
  3. Both topics connect biology with genetics in a simple, logical way.

Most Important Questions and Answers

Direct answer: Below are commonly asked questions from this chapter, along with short and exam-ready answers.

Q1. What is heredity?

Heredity is the process by which traits are passed from parents to their children through genes.

Q2. What are dominant and recessive traits?

A dominant trait expresses itself even if only one copy of the gene is present. A recessive trait expresses itself only when both copies of the gene are present.

Q3. What is Mendel's Law of Inheritance?

Mendel's laws explain how traits are inherited, including the Law of Dominance, Law of Segregation, and Law of Independent Assortment.

Q4. What is natural selection?

Natural selection is the process where organisms with useful traits survive and reproduce more successfully than others.

Q5. What is speciation?

Speciation is the formation of new species due to long term genetic changes and isolation.

Q6. How are variations useful for survival?

Variations help organisms adapt to changing environments, increasing their chances of survival.
