CBSE Class 12 Chemistry Investigatory Project Topics and Ideas | EduRain Blog

A CBSE Class 12 Chemistry Investigatory Project is a short scientific investigation that combines laboratory testing with information gathered from other sources. Project Work carries 4 of the 30 practical marks. Students can pick a CBSE-suggested topic, such as food adulterants or casein in milk, or another idea approved by the teacher.

This guide covers the official project marks, the suggested topics, topic selection, report format, and viva preparation. It follows the 2026 to 2027 Chemistry syllabus (Code 043).

What Does the CBSE Syllabus Say About the Chemistry Investigatory Project?

The 2026 to 2027 syllabus gives Project Work 4 marks within the 30-mark practical. It describes projects as scientific investigations that involve laboratory testing and collecting information from other sources.

The syllabus lists Volumetric Analysis (8), Salt Analysis (8), Content-Based Experiment (6), Project Work (4), and Class Record and viva (4) as the practical components. Chemistry carries 100 marks in total, with 70 for theory and 30 for the practical.

Practical componentMarks
Volumetric Analysis8

The syllabus calls its project list "suggested" projects. It also allows any other investigatory project that involves about 10 periods of work, with the teacher's approval. Download the latest syllabus from cbseacademic.nic.in to confirm these details.

Which Chemistry Project Topics for Class 12 Does CBSE Suggest?

CBSE suggests eight projects. They cover oxalate ions in guava, casein in milk, soybean milk, potassium bisulphate as a preservative, starch digestion by salivary amylase, fermentation rates, essential oils, and food adulterants.

Casein in milkCasein in different milk samplesProteins in Biomolecules
Soybean milkComparison with natural milk for curd formation and effect of temperatureProteins in Biomolecules

Essential oilsOils in saunf, ajwain and illaichiOrganic compounds

The related chapter column is a study aid, not an official mapping. Note that Chemistry in Everyday Life is assessed only formatively, so the preservative project is best supported by your own experiments.

Other school lists often include sterilising water with bleaching powder and separating ink pigments by paper chromatography. Treat these as ideas to discuss with your teacher, because approval rests with the school. Experiments from the practical syllabus, such as paper chromatography and the effect of concentration on cell potential, can also inspire a deeper investigation.

How Can Students Choose a Topic and Find Guidance?

Choose a topic that fits the syllabus, needs about 10 periods of work, uses safe materials, and has teacher approval. Prefer a question you can test with simple, repeatable measurements.

Check three things before you commit:

  1. Can your school laboratory supply the chemicals and apparatus?
  2. Can you finish testing and writing within the allotted periods?
  3. Can you explain the principle behind every test you perform?

Students often need help turning a topic into a clear investigation question and a neat report. They can explore Edurain Online Education Platform as an educational guidance platform for Chemistry concepts linked to their project, alongside the NCERT textbooks and the school laboratory manual. Teacher approval and school guidelines always come first.

What Should a Complete Project Report Include?

A typical report has a cover page, certificate, acknowledgement, index, aim, theory, procedure, observations, result, precautions, and bibliography. Follow your school's format, because layouts can differ between schools.

SectionWhat to write
Title and aimOne clear investigation question
TheoryChemical principle in your own words
Materials and apparatusFull list with quantities
ProcedureNumbered steps that another student could repeat
ObservationsTables with readings, colours or measurements
CalculationsShow every step if your project has numerical work
Result and conclusionWhat the data shows, linked to the aim
PrecautionsSafety and accuracy points
BibliographyTextbooks, manuals and websites actually used

Write the theory section after you finish the experiments. This keeps it matched to what you observed.

How Should Students Prepare for Project Evaluation and Viva?

Project Work carries 4 marks, and the class record with viva carries another 4. Be ready to explain your aim, procedure, observations, precautions, and sources of error.

The syllabus does not publish a split within the 4 project marks, so rely on your teacher's instructions. Practise answering these questions aloud:

  1. Why did you choose this topic?
  2. What is the chemical principle behind your main test?
  3. Which apparatus did you use, and why?
  4. What precautions did you follow?
  5. What could change your result?

What Common Mistakes Should Students Avoid in Chemistry Projects?

The most common mistakes are copying a ready-made report, skipping the theory, and recording incomplete observations. Each one is easy to correct with early planning.

Common mistakeBetter approach
Downloading a sample report and changing the namePerform your own tests and record your own readings
Choosing a topic that needs unavailable chemicalsConfirm the materials list with your teacher first
Writing observations from memoryNote readings in the laboratory as you work
Ignoring safety stepsMention precautions in the report and follow them
Leaving the report until the last weekPlan experiments, drafting and revision across the term

Key Takeaways

  1. Project Work carries 4 of the 30 practical marks in Chemistry (Code 043).
  2. CBSE suggests eight projects, and the teacher can approve other investigations of about 10 periods.
  3. A good topic is safe, testable, and linked to a chapter you understand.
  4. Your report should include aim, theory, procedure, observations, result, precautions, and bibliography.
  5. Viva questions usually test the principle, apparatus, procedure, and precautions behind your own work.

Conclusion and Next Steps

A well-planned CBSE Class 12 Chemistry Investigatory Project turns classroom chemistry into real measurements and clear explanations. Your next step is to shortlist two topics from the suggested list. Check the materials for each, then ask your teacher to approve one.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What is a chemistry investigatory project for Class 12?

It is a scientific investigation that combines laboratory testing with information from other sources. Students record their aim, procedure, observations, and conclusion in a report and explain it in the viva.

FAQ 2: Which are the best chemistry project topics for Class 12?

Start with the CBSE-suggested topics, such as casein in milk, food adulterants, or fermentation rates. The best topic is one you can test safely, explain clearly, and finish in about 10 periods.

FAQ 3: How many marks does the chemistry project carry in Class 12?

Project Work carries 4 marks within the 30-mark practical. Class record and viva carry another 4 marks, and the theory paper carries 70.

FAQ 4: How do I make a chemistry investigatory project for Class 12?

Choose an approved topic, perform the experiments, and record readings immediately. Then write the theory, observations, result, and precautions in your school's format, and practise explaining each step for the viva.

FAQ 5: Where can I download a Class 12 Chemistry project PDF?

Download the official syllabus from cbseacademic.nic.in for the suggested project list. Sample report PDFs from other websites vary in quality, so use them only as a format reference and never copy them.

FAQ 6: Can students choose a project that is not in the CBSE list?

Yes. The syllabus allows any other investigatory project that involves about 10 periods of work, with the teacher's approval.
