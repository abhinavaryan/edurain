CBSE Class 10 Science Most Repeated Questions in Board Exams | EduRain Blog

The CBSE Class 10 Science Most Repeated Questions usually come from chemical reactions, acids, bases and salts, carbon compounds, life processes, heredity, light, electricity, and magnetic effects. Board papers repeat concepts, diagrams, formulas, and question patterns, not exact wording. This guide lists those patterns and shows how to practise them.

Which Chapters Carry the Most Weight in Class 10 Science?

Chemistry and Biology together carry about 50 of the 80 theory marks in the recent CBSE paper design. Physics carries most of the rest. Students who master these units first cover the largest scoring area.

UnitMarks (recent pattern)Chapters covered
Chemical Substances25Reactions, Acids, Bases and Salts, Metals and Non-metals, Carbon Compounds
World of Living25Life Processes, Control and Coordination, Reproduction, Heredity
Natural Phenomena12Light: Reflection and Refraction, Human Eye
Effects of Current13Electricity, Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
Natural Resources5Our Environment

These marks reflect the recent CBSE pattern, not a prediction. Always confirm the current syllabus and paper design on the official CBSE website before planning revision. Repetition in past papers is an observed trend, not an official announcement.

Which Chemistry Questions Come Up Again and Again?

Balancing equations, identifying reaction types, pH and salts, the reactivity series, and carbon compound naming are the most repeated Chemistry areas. Short-answer and case-based questions often test these ideas in new situations.

Here are the usual patterns, chapter by chapter:

  1. Chemical Reactions and Equations: Balance a given equation. Identify combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, and redox reactions. Explain rancidity and corrosion.
  2. Acids, Bases and Salts: Explain the uses of baking soda, washing soda, bleaching powder, and plaster of Paris. Describe the pH scale and its role in daily life.
  3. Metals and Non-metals: Use the reactivity series to predict displacement. Explain ionic bond formation in NaCl or MgCl₂.
  4. Carbon and its Compounds: Write names and structures of simple compounds. Explain the homologous series, esterification, saponification, and the cleansing action of soap.

Practise writing balanced equations with state symbols. Examiners award marks for the correct formula, balancing, and conditions.

Which Biology Questions Are Asked Almost Every Year?

Diagrams and process explanations dominate Biology. The human heart, the nephron, the reflex arc, the reproductive system, and Mendel's crosses appear very often. Practise labelling and writing points in a clear sequence.

Focus on these repeated areas:

  1. Life Processes: Compare aerobic and anaerobic respiration. Explain double circulation and urine formation.
  2. Control and Coordination: Draw and explain the reflex arc. Name hormones and their functions.
  3. How Do Organisms Reproduce: Compare binary fission and budding. Describe the human male and female reproductive systems.
  4. Heredity: Work out the monohybrid cross ratio of 3:1 and the dihybrid ratio of 9:3:3:1. Explain sex determination in humans.
  5. Our Environment: Describe food chains, the ten percent energy flow rule, and ozone layer depletion.

Which Physics Formulas and Numericals Appear Often?

Mirror and lens formulas, Ohm's law, resistors in series and parallel, and electric power are the most repeated Physics numericals. Ray diagrams, eye defects, and the right-hand thumb rule also appear regularly.

ConceptFormulaMeaning of symbols
Mirror formula1/v + 1/u = 1/fv is image distance, u is object distance, f is focal length
Magnification (mirror)m = −v/uRatio of image height to object height
Lens formula1/v − 1/u = 1/fUsed with the Cartesian sign convention
Power of a lensP = 1/ff in metres gives P in dioptres
Snell's lawn = sin i/sin rRefractive index of the medium
Ohm's lawV = IRVoltage, current, resistance
ResistivityR = ρl/AResistance of a wire
Heating effectH = I²RtHeat produced in joules
Electric powerP = VIPower in watts

Worked example 1: A concave mirror has f = −15 cm. An object is placed at u = −30 cm. Then 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/15 + 1/30 = −1/30, so v = −30 cm. The magnification is m = −(−30)/(−30) = −1. The image is real, inverted, and the same size as the object.

Worked example 2: Resistors of 4 Ω and 6 Ω are connected in parallel across 12 V. The equivalent resistance is (4 × 6)/(4 + 6) = 2.4 Ω. The total current is 12/2.4 = 5 A.

What Common Mistakes Cost Marks in Science Answers?

Most lost marks come from wrong sign conventions, unbalanced equations, unlabelled diagrams, and missing units. These errors are avoidable with regular written practice and careful checking.

MistakeHow to fix it
Ignoring signs in mirror and lens numericalsWrite the sign convention before solving
Writing unbalanced chemical equationsCount atoms on both sides every time
Leaving diagrams unlabelledLabel every part and add a heading
Mixing up Fleming's left-hand rule and the right-hand thumb ruleLink each rule to its use: motor action and magnetic field direction
Skipping units in final answersWrite the unit with every numerical result
Giving long paragraphs for 3-mark answersWrite three or four clear points

How Should You Practise These Questions Step by Step?

Start with NCERT, then solve Class 10 Science important questions with answers, and finish with timed papers. This order builds understanding first and speed later. It also suits students who want to score 90-plus.

  1. Read NCERT line by line. Many board questions come directly from NCERT text, examples, and exercises. NCERT-based practice is the safest base for revision.
  2. Solve chapter-wise questions. Take one chapter at a time. Cover the in-text questions, exercise questions, and short answers before moving ahead.
  3. Study previous year questions. Solve CBSE Class 10 Science previous year questions and the sample papers released by CBSE. Then revisit the CBSE Class 10 Science Most Repeated Questions pattern list and tick off each concept you can explain.
  4. Practise all question types. Include multiple-choice, assertion and reason, short answer, long answer, and case-based questions.
  5. Write timed papers. Attempt a full 3-hour paper weekly. Review every mistake in a separate notebook.
  6. Revise formulas and diagrams daily. Spend 20 minutes on formulas, reactions, and labelled diagrams.

Key Takeaways

  1. Chemistry and Biology together carry about 50 of the 80 theory marks in the recent pattern.
  2. Repeated questions are concept and format repeats, not guaranteed exact questions.
  3. Balanced equations, labelled diagrams, and correct units protect easy marks.
  4. Mirror, lens, and electricity formulas need numerical practice, not memorising alone.
  5. NCERT is the primary base. Sample papers and previous year questions build exam readiness.
  6. Always check the latest official CBSE syllabus and paper design before final revision.

Conclusion and Next Steps

Repeated board questions follow patterns in reactions, life processes, heredity, light, and electricity. Learn those patterns through NCERT, solve past papers, and correct errors regularly. As a next step, pick one chapter today, solve its important questions, and check your answers against NCERT.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: Which chapters are most important in Class 10 Science?

Chemical Reactions and Equations, Acids, Bases and Salts, Carbon Compounds, Life Processes, Heredity, Light, and Electricity are the most scoring chapters. Do not skip other chapters, as every unit contributes marks.

FAQ 2: How can I prepare Class 10 Science for the board exam?

Read NCERT carefully, practise chapter-wise questions, and solve sample papers under timed conditions. Keep a short notebook for formulas, equations, and diagrams. Revise it every day in the last month.

FAQ 3: Are repeated questions guaranteed to come in the board exam?

No. Repetition is an observed trend in past papers. CBSE does not announce or promise repeated questions. Treat them as important practice areas, not as a shortcut or a guarantee.

FAQ 4: Which Physics numericals should students practise most?

Practise mirror and lens formula problems, series and parallel resistor circuits, and problems using P = VI and H = I²Rt. Also solve questions on lens power and refractive index.

FAQ 5: How should I answer case-based questions in Science?

Read the passage fully before the questions. Use the given data and your NCERT concepts. Write short, direct answers, and avoid adding information that the question does not ask for.

FAQ 6: Where can I find reliable Class 10 Science board exam questions?

Use NCERT exercises, CBSE sample papers, and previous year papers from the official CBSE website. These sources match the board's format and syllabus, so they make the most reliable practice material.
