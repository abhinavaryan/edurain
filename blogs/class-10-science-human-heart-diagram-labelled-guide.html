Class 10 Science: Human Heart Diagram and Double Circulation | EduRain Blog

The human heart is a four-chambered muscular organ that pumps blood through two circuits, one to the lungs and one to the body. Double circulation means blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle. This guide explains the Class 10 Science: Human Heart Diagram, its labels, blood flow, and exam tips.

What Does the Human Heart Diagram Show in Class 10 Science?

A human heart labelled diagram shows the four chambers, major blood vessels, valves, and the septum. It helps students trace how oxygen-rich and carbon dioxide-rich blood move through the heart without mixing.

Questions on the human heart diagram that Class 10 students study come from the Life Processes chapter, in the transportation section. NCERT describes the heart as a muscular organ about as big as your fist. Board papers often ask you to draw it and label the parts.

A CBSE Class 10 human heart diagram is simple, but marks depend on correct labels. The heart diagram in NCERT Class 10 is a good reference for what examiners expect. A human heart diagram with labels should also show the direction of blood flow.

Syllabus details can change between academic years. Check the official CBSE website and the latest NCERT textbook before you finalise your notes.

How Do the Four Chambers and Valves Work Together?

The upper chambers, called atria, receive blood. The lower chambers, called ventricles, pump blood out. Valves between the chambers and at the exits stop backflow, so blood moves in one direction only.

Heart partReceives blood fromSends blood toType of blood
Right atriumBody, through vena cavaRight ventricleDeoxygenated
Right ventricleRight atriumLungs, through pulmonary arteryDeoxygenated
Left atriumLungs, through pulmonary veinsLeft ventricleOxygenated
Left ventricleLeft atriumBody, through aortaOxygenated

A thick wall called the septum separates the right and left sides. This keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood from mixing.

Ventricle walls are thicker than atrial walls because ventricles pump blood out of the heart. The left ventricle has the thickest wall because it sends blood to the whole body.

The right side has the tricuspid valve between its atrium and ventricle. The left side has the bicuspid valve. Semilunar valves guard the exits into the pulmonary artery and the aorta.

How Can You Draw and Label the Heart Step by Step?

Draw the outer outline, divide it with a septum, add four chambers, attach the main vessels, and label with straight, uncrossed lines. A neat diagram with correct labels earns marks in board examinations.

  1. Sketch the outer outline of the heart with a sharp pencil.
  2. Draw a vertical septum, then a horizontal division to mark the atria above and the ventricles below.
  3. Make the left ventricle wall visibly thicker than the right.
  4. Attach the vena cava to the right atrium and the pulmonary artery to the right ventricle.
  5. Add the aorta rising from the left ventricle and the pulmonary veins entering the left atrium.
  6. Mark the valves, then label using ruled lines that do not cross each other.

Remember that the diagram shows the body from the front. The right atrium therefore appears on the left side of your page. This is the most common labelling error.

How Does Double Circulation Move Blood Through the Body?

Double circulation means blood travels through the heart twice in one complete cycle. Pulmonary circulation carries blood between the heart and lungs. Systemic circulation carries blood between the heart and the rest of the body.

Pulmonary circulation: The right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood into the pulmonary artery. The blood reaches the lungs, picks up oxygen, and returns through the pulmonary veins to the left atrium.

Systemic circulation: The left atrium passes blood to the left ventricle. The left ventricle pumps it into the aorta, which supplies the body. Deoxygenated blood returns through the vena cava to the right atrium.

Double circulation matters because it keeps oxygen-rich and carbon dioxide-rich blood separate. Birds and mammals have high energy needs and maintain a constant body temperature, so this arrangement suits them well.

Animal groupHeart chambersCirculation
FishTwoSingle, blood passes through the heart once in each cycle
Amphibians and many reptilesThreeSome mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood is tolerated
Birds and mammalsFourDouble, with complete separation of the two blood types

Which Blood Vessels Should You Remember for Exams?

Four vessels are essential: the vena cava, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein, and aorta. Knowing what each carries and where it connects helps you label the diagram and answer reasoning questions correctly.

VesselDirection of flowType of blood
Vena cavaBody to right atriumDeoxygenated
Pulmonary arteryRight ventricle to lungsDeoxygenated
Pulmonary veinLungs to left atriumOxygenated
AortaLeft ventricle to bodyOxygenated

Arteries usually carry oxygenated blood, and veins usually carry deoxygenated blood. The pulmonary artery and pulmonary vein are the exceptions. Examiners like to test this point.

What Common Mistakes Should Students Avoid?

Most errors come from mixing up sides, vessels, or blood types. Slow down, check the direction of each arrow, and revise the table of chambers and vessels before writing your answer.

  1. Swapping left and right: Label from the patient's point of view, not your own.
  2. Wrong vessel links: The aorta leaves the left ventricle, not the right.
  3. Equal wall thickness: Show the left ventricle with a clearly thicker wall.
  4. Missing valves: Include valves when the question asks for a detailed diagram.
  5. Confusing circulation types: Fish have single circulation, while birds and mammals have double circulation.

Which Practice Questions Help Revise This Topic?

Short practice questions on labelling, blood flow, and reasoning are the quickest way to revise. Attempt them without notes, then check every answer against the NCERT text and correct your mistakes.

  1. Name the chamber that pumps blood to the whole body. (Left ventricle)
  2. Why are ventricle walls thicker than atrial walls? (Ventricles pump blood out of the heart.)
  3. Which vessel carries oxygenated blood to the left atrium? (Pulmonary vein)
  4. Why is double circulation useful in mammals? (It separates the two types of blood and supplies oxygen efficiently.)
  5. Trace one full cycle of blood starting from the right atrium.

Key Takeaways

  1. The heart has four chambers: two atria and two ventricles, separated by the septum.
  2. Valves ensure one-way flow, and the left ventricle has the thickest wall.
  3. Double circulation has two loops: pulmonary (heart to lungs) and systemic (heart to body).
  4. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood, and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.
  5. Label the diagram from the patient's point of view to avoid left and right errors.
  6. Practise drawing from memory and revise the vessel table before tests.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the main parts to label in the human heart diagram?

Label the right and left atria, right and left ventricles, septum, vena cava, aorta, pulmonary artery, pulmonary veins, and the valves. Also show which side carries oxygenated and which carries deoxygenated blood.

FAQ 2: What is double circulation in simple words?

Double circulation means blood passes through the heart twice in one full cycle. It first goes to the lungs to collect oxygen, returns to the heart, and is then pumped to the entire body.

FAQ 3: Why do ventricles have thicker walls than atria?

Ventricles pump blood out of the heart, so they need stronger muscle. The left ventricle is the thickest because it must push blood to all parts of the body.

FAQ 4: Which vessel is the exception to the artery and vein rule?

The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood to the heart. Most arteries and veins follow the opposite pattern, so remember these two for exams.

FAQ 5: How should I practise the heart diagram for board exams?

Draw it from memory, compare it with the NCERT figure, and correct every missing label or arrow. Repeat this several times a week and time yourself to build speed and accuracy.

FAQ 6: Do fish have double circulation?

No. A fish heart has two chambers, and blood passes through the heart once in each cycle. Birds and mammals have four chambered hearts and double circulation.
