Trigonometry Class 10 Maths: Formulas, Tips & Guide

Trigonometry links the sides and angles of a right-angled triangle, and it is a scoring unit in the CBSE Class 10 board paper. Most lost marks come from forgotten values and weak identity proofs. This guide on Trigonometry class 10 maths covers chapter weightage, formula tables, identity-solving tips, a study plan, books, and common mistakes.

Chapter Overview and Weightage

The CBSE Class 10 Trigonometry unit has two NCERT chapters: Introduction to Trigonometry and Some Applications of Trigonometry. Together, they carry about 12 marks in the 80-mark board paper.

ChapterKey ConceptsApprox. Marks
Introduction to TrigonometryRatios, standard angle values, complementary angles, identities8
Some Applications of TrigonometryHeights and distances, angle of elevation, angle of depression4

Marks can vary slightly each year. Check the latest CBSE curriculum document for the current pattern. Chapter numbers may also change in newer NCERT editions, but the concepts stay the same. Recent papers also include case-based questions from this unit.

Important Trigonometric Ratios and Formulas

The six ratios come from the sides of a right-angled triangle. Sine, cosine, and tangent are the base ratios, while cosecant, secant, and cotangent are their reciprocals. Learn these first.

RatioFormulaReciprocal
sin θPerpendicular / Hypotenusecosec θ
cos θBase / Hypotenusesec θ
tan θPerpendicular / Basecot θ

Also remember that tan θ = sin θ / cos θ and cot θ = cos θ / sin θ. These two forms help you simplify almost every expression.

The Three Core Identities

Class 10 has three main identities, and each comes from the Pythagorean theorem. Every proof question in this unit depends on them. Memorise all three with their rearranged forms.

IdentityUseful Rearrangements
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ, cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ
1 + tan²θ = sec²θsec²θ − tan²θ = 1
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θcosec²θ − cot²θ = 1

The complementary angle rules are equally important. For example, sin (90° − θ) = cos θ, and tan (90° − θ) = cot θ. Questions on these rules are usually quick marks.

Identity Solving Tips That Work in Exams

To prove an identity, start with the more complex side and simplify it until it matches the other side. Do not move terms across the equals sign. Follow these steps every time.

  1. Write everything in terms of sin θ and cos θ.
  2. Pick the side with more terms, and work only on that side.
  3. Take the common factor out, or find a common denominator.
  4. Use a²− b² = (a − b)(a + b) for terms like 1 − sin²θ.
  5. Multiply by the conjugate when a denominator has 1 + sin θ or 1 − cos θ.
  6. Stop when both sides match, and write "Hence proved."

Practice with expressions like (1 + tan²θ)(1 − sin θ)(1 + sin θ). Here, the last two brackets give cos²θ. Then the first bracket gives sec²θ, so the result is 1.

Step-by-Step Study Plan for Trigonometry

A ten-day plan works well: learn ratios first, then identities, and then heights and distances. Give each stage its own days. Do not rush to the next stage before finishing the first.

  1. Day 1 to 2: Learn the ratios, reciprocals, and the standard angle table.
  2. Day 3 to 4: Solve NCERT Exercise 8.1 and 8.2 on ratios and values.
  3. Day 5 to 6: Learn the identities and solve Exercise 8.4 proofs.
  4. Day 7 to 8: Practise heights and distances with clear diagrams.
  5. Day 9: Solve one full previous year question set on the unit.
  6. Day 10: Revise formulas and rework every wrong answer.

Recommended Books for Trigonometry

NCERT Mathematics for Class 10 is the main source, since board questions follow its style closely. Extra books help only after you finish every NCERT example and exercise.

  1. NCERT Mathematics Class 10: Solve all examples and exercises first.
  2. NCERT Exemplar Class 10 Mathematics: Good for higher-order and competency questions.
  3. RS Aggarwal Class 10 Mathematics: Offers a large set of graded identity problems.
  4. CBSE sample papers and previous year papers: Best for board pattern and marking scheme.

Pick one extra book and finish it. Collecting many books wastes time.

Common Mistakes in Trigonometry

Students mostly lose marks through wrong values, sign errors, and identity proofs that skip steps. These errors are easy to fix with regular checking. Watch for each one below.

  1. Writing sin 30° as √3/2 instead of 1/2.
  2. Treating sin²θ as sin θ², or dropping the square.
  3. Assuming sin (A + B) equals sin A + sin B.
  4. Cross-multiplying in an identity proof, which is not allowed.
  5. Drawing a wrong diagram in heights and distances questions.
  6. Mixing up angle of elevation with angle of depression.

Keep a list of your own repeated errors. Revise it before every test.

Key Takeaways

  1. Learn the three identities and their rearranged forms perfectly.
  2. Memorise the standard angle table using the pattern in sine and cosine rows.
  3. Convert to sin θ and cos θ first when a proof looks difficult.
  4. Always work on one side of an identity, never both sides together.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many marks does trigonometry carry in the CBSE Class 10 board exam?

The Trigonometry unit carries about 12 marks in the 80-mark paper. This includes Introduction to Trigonometry and Some Applications of Trigonometry. Check the current CBSE curriculum for the latest marking pattern.

Which trigonometry formulas are most important for Class 10?

The three identities, the six ratio definitions, and the standard angle values are the most important. Complementary angle relations also appear often in short answer and proof questions.

How do I prove trigonometric identities easily?

Convert every term to sin θ and cos θ. Then simplify the more complex side using factorisation, common denominators, or conjugates. Stop when both sides match.

What is the best way to remember the standard angle values?

Use the pattern in the sine row: 0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2, 1. The cosine row follows the same numbers in reverse order. Tangent equals sine divided by cosine.

Which book is best for Class 10 trigonometry practice?

Start with NCERT examples and exercises. Then use NCERT Exemplar for tougher questions and RS Aggarwal for extra practice. Finish with CBSE sample papers.

How do I solve heights and distances questions correctly?

Draw a clear diagram first and mark the angle at the right point. Choose the ratio that connects the known and unknown sides. Then solve and check the units.
