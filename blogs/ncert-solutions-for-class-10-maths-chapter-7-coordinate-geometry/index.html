NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Maths Chapter 7: Coordinate Geometry

Coordinate geometry lets students find distances, midpoints, and dividing points using algebra on the Cartesian plane. This guide on NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Maths Chapter 7: Coordinate Geometry covers key formulas, solved board-style questions, common mistakes, and a preparation plan. It is a scoring unit because most questions rely on two formulas.

What Coordinate Geometry Covers in Class 10 Maths

Class 10 coordinate geometry focuses on the distance between two points and the section formula for internal division. Midpoint and centroid questions are applications of these two ideas. Recent CBSE marking schemes gave this unit 6 marks, so confirm the current year's scheme.

The chapter builds on Class 9 knowledge of plotting points and reading coordinates. Students should know the four quadrants and the signs of x and y in each. The rationalised CBSE syllabus has not listed the area of a triangle in recent years. Check the latest CBSE syllabus before including it in your revision.

Essential Formulas You Must Remember

Two formulas carry most of this chapter: the distance formula and the section formula. The midpoint formula is the section formula with ratio 1:1. Learn them with their conditions so you can apply them correctly.

ConceptFormula
Distance between A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂)√[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
Distance of P(x, y) from the origin√(x² + y²)
Section formula (internal ratio m:n)[(mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n)]
Midpoint of AB[(x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2]
Centroid of a triangle[(x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3, (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3]


Write the formula first in every answer. Board examiners usually award marks for the correct formula and substitution.

Important Questions with Worked Solutions

These question types appear often in board-style papers. Each one tests a formula and a clear sequence of steps. Practice all three types below.

Distance Formula Questions

Distance formula questions ask for the distance between points, a point equidistant from two given points, or an unknown coordinate value. Set up an equation using squared distances to avoid square roots.

Question: Find a point on the x axis equidistant from A(2, −5) and B(−2, 9).

Step 1: Let the point be P(x, 0), since it lies on the x axis.

Step 2: Equidistant means PA = PB, so PA² = PB².

Step 3: Substitute: (x − 2)² + (0 + 5)² = (x + 2)² + (0 − 9)².

Step 4: Expand: x² − 4x + 4 + 25 = x² + 4x + 4 + 81.

Step 5: Simplify: −8x = 56, so x = −7.

Conclusion: The point is (−7, 0). Check: both distances equal √106.

Section Formula Questions

Section formula questions ask for the point dividing a line segment in a given ratio, or for the ratio itself. Identify m and n first, then substitute carefully into each coordinate.

Question: Find the point dividing the line joining A(4, −3) and B(8, 5) in the ratio 2:3.

Step 1: Here m = 2, n = 3, (x₁, y₁) = (4, −3) and (x₂, y₂) = (8, 5).

Step 2: Find x: x = (2 × 8 + 3 × 4)/(2 + 3) = 28/5.

Step 3: Find y: y = (2 × 5 + 3 × (−3))/(2 + 3) = 1/5.

Conclusion: The point is (28/5, 1/5).

Question: In what ratio does the x-axis divide the line joining A(−4, −6) and B(−1, 7)?

Step 1: Let the x-axis divide AB in the ratio k:1 at a point where y = 0.

Step 2: Apply the section formula to the y-coordinate: (7k + 1 × (−6))/(k + 1) = 0.

Step 3: Solve: 7k − 6 = 0, so k = 6/7.

Step 4: Find the ratio: k:1 = 6/7: 1 = 6:7.

Conclusion: The x-axis divides AB in the ratio 6:7. The x-coordinate of the point is −34/13.

Collinearity and Quadrilateral Questions

These questions ask students to prove that points are collinear or that they form a particular quadrilateral. Calculate all required lengths, then state the conclusion with a reason.

Question: Show that A(1, 7), B(4, 2), C(−1, −1) and D(−4, 4) are the vertices of a square.

Step 1: Find the four sides: AB² = 9 + 25 = 34, BC² = 25 + 9 = 34, CD² = 9 + 25 = 34, DA² = 25 + 9 = 34.

Step 2: So AB = BC = CD = DA = √34. All four sides are equal.

Step 3: Find the diagonals: AC² = 4 + 64 = 68 and BD² = 64 + 4 = 68. Both diagonals equal √68.

Conclusion: Four equal sides and equal diagonals prove that ABCD is a square.

For three collinear points, show that the sum of two distances equals the third.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Most lost marks come from sign errors and substitution slips, not from weak concepts. Slow down at the substitution step and review each bracket before you simplify.

  1. Reversing coordinates: Keep x with x and y with y in every subtraction.
  2. Dropping the square root: The distance formula needs the final root unless you equate squared distances.
  3. Mixing m and n: In the section formula, m multiplies the second point's coordinates and n multiplies the first point's.
  4. Sign errors with negatives: Put negative values in brackets before squaring.
  5. Skipping the conclusion: Always write a final statement, such as "Hence ABCD is a square."

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most important topics in this chapter for board exams?

The distance formula, the section formula and the midpoint formula are the core topics. Questions usually ask for unknown coordinates, ratios of division, collinearity or a proof about a quadrilateral.

FAQ 2: Which formulas should students remember?

Remember the distance formula, the distance from the origin, the section formula for internal division, the midpoint formula and the centroid formula. Practise writing them from memory every day.

FAQ 3: How can I avoid sign mistakes in distance problems?

Write each coordinate difference inside brackets before squaring. Keep x values with x values and y values with y values. Recheck negative numbers before adding the squares.

FAQ 4: How do I prove that three points are collinear?

Find the three distances AB, BC, and AC. If the sum of the two shorter distances equals the longest distance, the points lie on one straight line. Write this reason in your answer.

FAQ 5: How should I find the ratio in which a point divides a line?

Assume the ratio as k:1 and apply the section formula. Equate the required coordinate to the given value, solve for k, and write the ratio. For the x-axis, set y equal to 0.

FAQ 6: How many marks does this unit carry in the board exam?

Recent CBSE marking schemes have allotted 6 marks to the Coordinate Geometry unit. Marks can change, so check the official CBSE sample paper and marking scheme for the current year.
