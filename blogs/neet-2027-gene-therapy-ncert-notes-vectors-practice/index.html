NEET 2027 Gene Therapy: NCERT Notes, Vectors & Practice

Gene therapy treats a genetic disorder by delivering a normal gene into a patient's cells. NEET questions on this topic come mostly from the ADA deficiency example, vectors, and the gap between gene therapy and enzyme replacement. This guide covers principles, types, applications, practice questions, and a study plan.

Gene Therapy Overview

Gene therapy is a set of methods that correct a gene defect by delivering a normal gene into a patient's cells. The normal gene then takes over the work of the faulty one.

NCERT places this topic in the Biotechnology and its Applications chapter of Class 12 Biology. It sits under medicine, along with recombinant insulin, molecular diagnosis, and transgenic animals. Check the latest NMC and NTA syllabus before you begin, since NCERT editions have been revised.

Questions are usually direct. You may be asked to identify the disease, the vector, or the first patient treated. Preparing NEET 2027 Gene Therapy as a short, fact-based unit makes these marks easy to secure.

Principles of Gene Therapy

The principle of gene therapy is to place a functional gene inside cells so they make the protein the body lacks. A vector carries the gene in. The cell then expresses it.

The process follows these steps:

  1. Identify the faulty gene that causes the disorder.
  2. Prepare a normal copy of the gene, usually as cDNA.
  3. Insert the copy into a suitable vector.
  4. Deliver the vector into the patient's cells.
  5. The cells express the gene and make the missing protein.

Common Vectors

A vector is a carrier that moves the therapeutic gene into the host cell. Viral vectors are the most common choice for NEET-level questions.

VectorNatureKey Point
RetrovirusRNA virusUses reverse transcriptase and integrates into host DNA. Used in the first ADA trial.
AdenovirusDNA virusInfects dividing and resting cells. Expression is short-lived.
Adeno-associated virusSmall DNA virusLow immune response. Widely used in current therapies.
LiposomesLipid vesiclesNonviral method of delivery.


Remember the retrovirus link. Retroviruses integrate their genetic material into the host genome, so the gene is passed on when the cell divides.

Types of Gene Therapy

Gene therapy is classified by the cell type targeted and by the place where the gene is delivered. Somatic therapy changes body cells and is not inherited. Germline therapy changes gametes or embryos and can pass to offspring.

BasisTypeMeaning
Cell typeSomaticBody cells are corrected. The effect ends with the patient.
Cell typeGermlineGametes or embryos are altered. The effect is inherited.
DeliveryEx vivoCells are modified outside the body and returned.
DeliveryIn vivoThe gene is delivered directly inside the body.


Clinical use today is limited to somatic therapy. Germline editing raises serious ethical concerns.

ADA Deficiency: The NCERT Case

ADA deficiency is a genetic disorder in which the enzyme adenosine deaminase is missing, so the immune system fails. It was the first disease treated by gene therapy, in 1990, in a four year old girl.

The enzyme is crucial for the working of the immune system. Its absence is a form of severe combined immunodeficiency, called SCID. The disorder is autosomal recessive.

Three treatment options exist:

  1. Bone marrow transplantation.
  2. Enzyme replacement therapy, where the enzyme is given by injection.
  3. Gene therapy, where the gene itself is supplied.

In the gene therapy method, lymphocytes are taken from the patient's blood. They are grown in culture outside the body. A functional ADA cDNA is inserted using a retroviral vector. The corrected lymphocytes are then returned to the patient.

This is not a permanent cure. Lymphocytes are not immortal, so the patient needs periodic infusions. A permanent cure is possible only if the gene is introduced into marrow cells at an early embryonic stage.

Other Conditions Studied

Cystic fibrosis, hemophilia, and sickle cell disease are single gene disorders studied for gene therapy. Treat these as extra reading. NCERT based questions focus on ADA deficiency.

Common Mistakes on Gene Therapy

Most marks are lost on this topic because of mixed-up terms, not weak effort. Watch for these errors.

  1. Calling ADA therapy a permanent cure: The ex vivo method needs repeated infusions.
  2. Mixing somatic and germline therapy: Only germline changes are inherited.
  3. Naming the wrong vector: The first ADA trial used a retrovirus, not an adenovirus.
  4. Confusing enzyme replacement with gene therapy: One supplies the enzyme, the other the gene.
  5. Mixing it with recombinant insulin: Humulin is made in bacteria, not delivered into the patient as a gene.
  6. Ignoring cDNA: The inserted copy is cDNA, which has no introns.

Keep a short error log and read it before each mock test.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is gene therapy in NEET Biology?

It is a method that corrects a gene defect by inserting a normal gene into cells. NCERT explains it through the ADA deficiency example.

2. Which disease was first treated by gene therapy?

ADA deficiency was the first. The treatment was given in 1990 to a four-year-old girl.

3. Why is ADA gene therapy not a permanent cure?

The corrected lymphocytes do not live forever. The patient therefore needs periodic infusions of fresh corrected cells.

4. Which vector was used for ADA gene therapy?

A retroviral vector carried the functional ADA cDNA into the patient's lymphocytes. Retroviruses integrate into the host genome.

5. How is gene therapy different from enzyme replacement therapy?

Gene therapy supplies a working gene so the body makes the enzyme. Enzyme replacement therapy injects the enzyme itself.

6. How many NEET questions come from gene therapy?

Usually one question at most, often combined with other biotechnology applications. Weightage changes yearly, so practice the whole chapter.
