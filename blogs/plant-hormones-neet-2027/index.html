Chapter Overview: Plant Hormones in NEET Biology

Plant hormones, also called phytohormones, are chemical substances that regulate growth, differentiation, and development in plants at very low concentrations.

This topic falls under Plant Growth and Development in the NCERT Class 11 Biology textbook. NTA regularly picks two to three questions from this chapter in NEET Biology sections.

The rationalized NCERT syllabus retains all five major hormone groups. Students must focus on discovery experiments and the physiological effects of each hormone class.

High Weightage Subtopics in Plant Hormones

The highest scoring subtopics are auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, ethylene, and abscisic acid, along with their discovery experiments and specific physiological roles.

Auxins

Auxins were the first hormones discovered through Charles Darwin's experiments on canary grass coleoptiles. Indole acetic acid, or IAA, remains the natural auxin tested most often.

Questions frequently ask about apical dominance, phototropism, and root initiation. Students should remember Went's experiment and the role of auxin in cell elongation.

Gibberellins

Gibberellins were first isolated from the fungus Gibberella fujikuroi, which causes bakanae disease in rice seedlings. Gibberellic acid, or GA3, is the form tested most in NEET papers.

NEET frequently tests bolting in cabbage, breaking of seed dormancy, and induction of parthenocarpy. The barley aleurone layer experiment also appears often.

Cytokinins

Cytokinins promote cell division and were discovered through coconut milk experiments by Miller and Skoog. Kinetin was the first cytokinin identified, from herring sperm DNA.

Delay of senescence, called the Richmond-Lang effect, is a frequently tested application. The ratio of auxin to cytokinin controlling root and shoot formation is another favorite question area.

Ethylene

Ethylene is the only gaseous plant hormone and controls fruit ripening along with senescence. It also promotes leaf abscission and breaks seed dormancy in some species.

Commercial use of ethephon in fruit ripening is a common application question. The triple response in etiolated seedlings appears repeatedly in previous year papers.

Abscisic Acid

Abscisic acid, or ABA, is known as the stress hormone because it helps plants survive unfavorable conditions. It induces stomatal closure and maintains seed and bud dormancy.

NEET often asks about ABA acting as an antagonist to gibberellins. Its role in wilting and stress response is a recurring exam focus.

Common Mistakes Students Make in Plant Hormones

Students often confuse hormone functions, mix up discoverer names, and forget the antagonistic relationship between gibberellins and abscisic acid.

Many learners mix up Kurosawa with Miller and Skoog while naming discovery scientists. This costs marks in assertion-reason and matching type questions.

Another frequent error is confusing apical dominance with phototropism during auxin-based questions. Students also forget that ethylene exists as a gas, while other hormones remain liquid or solid.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Which hormone is called the stress hormone in NEET Biology?

Abscisic acid is called the stress hormone. It helps plants tolerate drought, salinity, and other unfavorable conditions effectively.

Q2: How many previous-year questions come from plant hormones in NEET?

Typically, two to three questions appear from this chapter every year. Most focus on functions, discovery scientists, and hormone comparisons.

Q3: Which experiment is most important for auxin in NEET preparation?

Went's coleoptile bending experiment is the most referenced auxin experiment. It explains phototropism and unequal auxin distribution clearly.

Q4: Why is ethylene considered different from other plant hormones?

Ethylene is the only naturally gaseous plant hormone among the five major groups. It primarily controls fruit ripening and organ abscission.

Q5: Is NCERT enough to score well in the plant hormones chapter?

NCERT covers nearly all tested concepts for this chapter thoroughly. Adding previous-year question practice further strengthens exam readiness.

Q6: What is the antagonistic relationship tested between hormones in this chapter?

Gibberellins and abscisic acid often act as antagonists in seed dormancy. NEET frequently tests this opposing relationship in question papers.
