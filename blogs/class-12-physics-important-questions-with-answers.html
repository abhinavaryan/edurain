Class 12 Physics Important Questions with Answers for CBSE Board Exam 2027 | EduRain Blog

The best way to use Class 12 Physics Important Questions with Answers for the CBSE Board Exam 2027 is to practise them chapter by chapter, starting with NCERT. Focus on derivations, numericals, and case-based questions, check each solution against the marking scheme, and revise formulas regularly. This guide covers the paper pattern, solved examples, and common mistakes.

What Is the CBSE Class 12 Physics Paper Pattern for 2027?

As per the syllabus for the 2026 and 2027 session, Physics carries 70 marks for theory and 30 for practicals. The theory paper lasts three hours and covers nine units from fourteen NCERT chapters.

Recent board papers used five sections:

  1. Section A: 16 questions of one mark each.
  2. Section B: five questions of two marks each.
  3. Section C: seven questions of three marks each.
  4. Section D: two case-based questions of four marks each.
  5. Section E: three long-answer questions of five marks each.

This pattern comes from recent papers, not from a 2027 announcement. Confirm the exact format in the official sample paper on cbseacademic.nic.in.

Which Physics Chapters and Units Carry Higher Marks?

Electrostatics is listed at 16 marks and Electronic Devices at 7 marks in the 2026 and 2027 course structure. The full total is 70 marks, so revise every unit instead of skipping any.

Past papers suggest that many CBSE Class 12 Physics Important Questions come from Current Electricity, Moving Charges and Magnetism, Electromagnetic Induction, Ray Optics, Wave Optics, Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter, and Semiconductor Electronics. This is a practice trend, not an official list. Any syllabus topic can be tested.

How Should Students Practise Important Questions for Board Exams?

Start with NCERT examples and exercises, then solve previous year papers and sample papers under timed conditions. Review every wrong answer, rewrite it in marking scheme style, and revisit the concept behind the error.

  1. Read each NCERT chapter and mark definitions, laws, and derivations.
  2. Solve all solved examples first, then the exercise questions.
  3. Attempt previous year questions chapter-wise, with a timer.
  4. Write answers in full, with diagrams, units, and final statements.
  5. Revise formulas and derivations every week.

Many students find it hard to organise revision on their own. Use any such resource alongside NCERT, and compare every answer with the textbook.

Which Derivations Are Commonly Asked in Class 12 Physics?

Past papers have included three- and five-mark derivations such as the electric field of a dipole, capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor, solenoid magnetic field, lens formula, and fringe width in Young's experiment.

  1. Electrostatics: dipole field on the axis and equatorial line, Gauss law applications, energy stored in a capacitor.
  2. Current and magnetism: drift velocity and current, Biot-Savart law for a circular loop, Ampere's law for a solenoid.
  3. EMI and alternating current: motional emf, impedance of a series LCR circuit.
  4. Optics: refraction at a spherical surface, lens maker's formula, Young's double slit experiment.
  5. Modern physics: Einstein's photoelectric equation, Bohr's radius and energy levels.

Learn the diagram, assumptions, steps, and final result for each derivation.

Which Solved Questions Show the Right Answer Format?

The solved examples below show how to write board-style answers: state the formula, substitute values with units, and give the final result clearly. Use them as models for similar numericals. These Class 12 Physics questions and answers are practice samples, not predicted paper questions.

Numerical 1 (Electrostatics): Two point charges of 2 µC and 3 µC are 3 cm apart in vacuum. Find the force between them.

F = k q₁q₂ / r² = (9 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻⁶ × 3 × 10⁻⁶) / (3 × 10⁻²)² = 60 N. The force is repulsive.

Numerical 2 (Current Electricity): A cell of emf 12 V and internal resistance 1 Ω is connected to a 5 Ω resistor. Find the current and terminal voltage.

I = E / (R + r) = 12 / 6 = 2 A. Terminal voltage V = E minus Ir = 12 minus 2 = 10 V.

Numerical 3 (Ray Optics): An object is 30 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the image distance and magnification.

Using 1/v minus 1/u = 1/f with u = minus 30 cm: 1/v = 1/10 minus 1/30 = 1/15. So v = 15 cm, a real image. Magnification m = v/u = minus 0.5, so the image is inverted and half the size.

Numerical 4 (Dual Nature): Light with photon energy 3.5 eV falls on a metal with a work function 2.0 eV. Find the maximum kinetic energy and stopping potential.

Maximum kinetic energy = 3.5 minus 2.0 = 1.5 eV. The stopping potential is 1.5 V.

Which Common Mistakes Lower Marks in Physics Answers?

Students commonly lose marks by missing units, drawing incomplete ray diagrams, using wrong sign conventions, and skipping steps in derivations. Careful presentation can recover such marks without learning any extra content.

  1. Write the formula before substituting values.
  2. Label diagrams fully, including arrows on rays, field directions, and circuit symbols.
  3. Apply the Cartesian sign convention consistently in optics.
  4. Convert units before calculating, such as cm to m and µC to C.
  5. Read case-based passages twice before answering.
  6. Understand each derivation instead of memorising it, because stepwise marks are usually awarded.

Key Takeaways

  1. Physics has 70 theory marks and 30 practical marks, so do not neglect practicals.
  2. NCERT examples and exercises are the base for all board-level practice.
  3. Past paper trends help practice, but they are not official predictions.
  4. Derivations need a diagram, steps, and a final result.
  5. Units, signs, and labelled diagrams protect marks in numericals.
  6. Timed practice with self-review builds accuracy and speed.

Conclusion and Next Steps

Board success in Physics depends on clear concepts, regular numerical practice, and neat answer writing. Pick one chapter today, solve its NCERT examples, and then attempt a timed set of previous year questions. Compare your answers with the marking scheme and note each error. Before the exam, verify the pattern and syllabus on the official CBSE website.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: How many marks does Physics carry in the CBSE Class 12 board exam?

Physics carries 70 marks for theory and 30 marks for practicals, making a total of 100. The theory paper lasts three hours.

FAQ 2: Which derivations should students prepare first?

Begin with the electric dipole field, Gauss law applications, solenoid field, lens maker's formula, Young's double slit fringe width, and the photoelectric equation. These have appeared in past papers.

FAQ 3: How many numericals should students practise?

No official number exists. Solve all NCERT examples and exercises, then add several numericals from each chapter using previous years' and sample papers.

FAQ 4: Are case-based questions important for the board exam?

Recent papers carried two four-mark case-based questions. Practise reading the passage carefully, identifying the given data, and applying the right concept step by step.

FAQ 5: What is the best way to revise Physics formulas?

Keep a short formula sheet for each chapter with units and conditions of use. Revise it weekly and apply each formula in a numerical.

FAQ 6: Can students rely only on expected questions?

No. Expected question lists are practice aids, not official information. Any part of the syllabus can be tested, so cover every unit.
