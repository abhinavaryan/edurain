Polynomials Class 10 Step-by-Step Explanation with Examples | EduRain Blog

Polynomials are one of the most important topics in Class 10 Mathematics. They form the foundation for algebra and higher-level math concepts that students will study in Classes 11 and 12. This article is written for Class 10 students who want to understand polynomials clearly, from basic definitions to solving problems with confidence. By the end of this guide, you will understand what polynomials are, how to work with them, and how this topic connects to your board exam preparation. Polynomials Class 10 is a chapter that many students find confusing at first, but with the right explanation and practice, it becomes easy to handle. Whether you are studying through school textbooks or online education platforms, this guide will walk you through every key concept in simple language.


What Are Polynomials in Class 10 Math?


A polynomial is a mathematical expression made up of variables and coefficients, connected by addition, subtraction, or multiplication. The variable is usually written as x, and each part of the expression is called a term.


Example:


3x² + 5x – 2 is a polynomial

7x + 4 is also a polynomial


The highest power of the variable in a polynomial is called its degree.


Type of PolynomialDegreeExample
Linear12x + 3
Quadratic2x² – 5x + 6
Cubic3x³ + 2x² – x + 4

This chapter is covered in the NCERT textbook for Class 10 under Chapter 2, which follows the CBSE syllabus.


What Are the Key Terms Every Student Must Know?


Before solving problems, you need to know these basic terms clearly.


Degree of a Polynomial

The degree is the highest exponent of the variable. For example, in 4x³ + x – 1, the degree is 3.


Zeroes of a Polynomial

The zeroes of a polynomial are the values of x that make the polynomial equal to zero. For example, if p(x) = x – 3, then x = 3 is the zero because p(3) = 0.


Coefficients

These are the numbers written before the variable. In 5x², the coefficient is 5.


Constant Term

This is the number without any variable. In 3x + 7, the constant term is 7.


Understanding these terms helps you solve every type of question in Polynomials Class 10 with more confidence.


How Do You Find the Zeroes of a Polynomial?


Finding zeroes is one of the most tested skills in Class 10 board exams. Here is how to do it clearly.


For a Linear Polynomial: ax + b = 0

Solve directly:

x = –b/a

Example: 2x + 6 = 0

x = –6/2 = –3


So, –3 is the zero of the polynomial.

For a Quadratic Polynomial: ax² + bx + c = 0


You can use three methods:

Factorization method

Completing the square

Quadratic formula: x = [–b ± √(b² – 4ac)] / 2a


Example using factorization:

x² – 5x + 6 = 0

= (x – 2)(x – 3) = 0

So, x = 2 or x = 3


These are the two zeroes of the polynomial.


What Is the Relationship Between Zeroes and Coefficients?


This is one of the most important sections in this chapter. The CBSE board frequently asks questions based on this relationship.


For a quadratic polynomial ax² + bx + c, if α and β are the two zeroes, then:

Sum of zeroes: α + β = –b/a

Product of zeroes: α × β = c/a


Example:

For x² – 7x + 10:

a = 1, b = –7, c = 10

Sum of zeroes = –(–7)/1 = 7

Product of zeroes = 10/1 = 10


Let us verify:

Zeroes are 2 and 5

Sum = 2 + 5 = 7 ✓

Product = 2 × 5 = 10 ✓


This formula saves a lot of time during exams when you need to find zeroes without full factorization.


How to Divide Polynomials Using the Division Algorithm?


Polynomial division follows a rule called the Division Algorithm for Polynomials, which states:

Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder

This is similar to how you divide numbers in arithmetic.


Steps to divide polynomials:


Arrange both polynomials in descending order of degree

Divide the first term of the dividend by the first term of the divisor

Multiply the result with the entire divisor

Subtract and bring down the next term

Repeat until the degree of the remainder is less than the degree of the divisor


Example:

Divide x³ – 3x² + 5x – 3 by x – 1

Step 1: x³ ÷ x = x²

Step 2: x²(x – 1) = x³ – x²

Step 3: Subtract: –2x² + 5x – 3

Continue until remainder = 0


This method is used to verify zeroes and to factorize higher degree polynomials.


What Are Common Mistakes Students Make in This Chapter?


Many students lose marks due to small errors. Here are the most common mistakes and how to avoid them.


Mistake 1: Confusing degree with number of terms

The degree is the highest power, not the count of terms.


Mistake 2: Wrong sign while applying the sum and product formula

Remember: Sum = –b/a, not +b/a. The negative sign matters.


Mistake 3: Skipping verification

Always substitute your zeroes back into the original polynomial to check if the answer is correct.


Mistake 4: Not arranging terms in descending order before division

This causes confusion and wrong answers during long division.


Practical Tip: Solve at least five NCERT examples and five exercise problems daily. Consistency is the best strategy for scoring well in board exams.

Key Takeaways


  1. A polynomial is an expression with variables and coefficients connected by basic operations
  2. The degree of a polynomial is determined by the highest exponent of the variable
  3. Zeroes of a polynomial are the values of x where the polynomial equals zero
  4. For a quadratic polynomial, Sum of zeroes = –b/a and Product of zeroes = c/a
  5. The Division Algorithm states: Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder
  6. Always verify your zeroes by substituting them back into the original expression
  7. Practicing NCERT exercises regularly is the most effective exam preparation strategy

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)


1. Is the Polynomials chapter difficult in Class 10?

No, it is not difficult if you understand the basic concepts first. The formulas are simple, and with regular practice, most students find this chapter easy to score well in.


2. How many exercises are there in Polynomials Class 10 NCERT?

The NCERT Class 10 Chapter 2 on Polynomials has four exercises covering all major concepts, including zeroes, the relationship between zeroes and coefficients, and the division algorithm.


3. What is the weightage of Polynomials in the Class 10 board exam?

The Algebra unit, which includes polynomials, carries significant marks in the CBSE board exam. Polynomials themselves typically contribute around 6 to 8 marks through short-answer and long-answer type questions.


4. Can I study Polynomials without a tutor?

Yes, you can study independently using the NCERT textbook, video lectures, and practice worksheets available through schools and trusted learning resources.


5. What types of questions are asked from Polynomials in board exams?

Board exams commonly ask questions on finding zeroes, verifying relationships between zeroes and coefficients, and polynomial division. Occasionally, questions based on the Division Algorithm are also included.


6. How long does it take to complete the Polynomials chapter?

Most students can complete the full chapter in about five to seven days with one to two hours of focused study each day, including solving all NCERT exercises and examples.
