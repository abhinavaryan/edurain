CBSE Class 10 Science Previous Year Repeated Questions (2021–2025) – Chapter-Wise | EduRain Blog

Board papers return to the same core ideas every year, but the wording changes. This guide lists the CBSE Class 10 Science Previous Year Repeated Questions chapter by chapter, with formulas, common mistakes, and a revision plan. It is based on question trends from past papers and NCERT, not on any official list.

What Counts as a Repeated Question in Board Papers

A repeated question is a concept or question type that appears across several years, even when the framing differs. Examiners reuse ideas like balancing equations, ray diagrams, and Mendel's crosses in new formats.

The 2021 Class 10 board exams were cancelled, so no 2021 board paper exists. The 2022 exam was held in two terms, and 2023, 2024, and 2025 followed a single annual paper. Trend analysis therefore rests mainly on the 2022 to 2025 papers, plus CBSE sample papers.

The theory paper is for 80 marks, and internal assessment carries 20. CBSE has also raised the share of competency-based questions in recent sessions. Confirm the pattern for the 2026 27 session on the official CBSE Academic website.

Plan to Revise Repeated Questions

Begin with NCERT, then solve past papers chapter by chapter, and finish with timed full papers. This order builds clarity first and speed later, which suits students preparing for the 2027 board exam.

  1. Read every NCERT chapter and underline definitions, reactions, and labelled diagrams.
  2. Solve all NCERT exercises and in-text questions without looking at answers.
  3. Collect past paper questions chapter-wise and mark the types that repeat.
  4. Practise diagrams until you can draw them neatly in two minutes.
  5. Attempt two full timed papers every week in the last two months.

Try these questions before checking solutions in NCERT:

  1. Balance the equation for the reaction between zinc and copper sulphate, and name the reaction type.
  2. Draw a ray diagram for an object placed between the focus and optical centre of a convex lens.
  3. Explain how a reflex arc works, with a labelled diagram.
  4. Draw the electric circuit for two resistors in parallel and write the equivalent resistance formula.
  5. State two effects of ozone layer depletion and one step that limits it.

Essential Formulas and Equations to Remember

Numericals mainly come from Light and Electricity, so these formulas deserve daily revision. Always write the formula first, then substitute values with units.

TopicFormula
Ohm's lawV = IR
Resistance of a conductorR = ρl/A
Resistors in seriesR = R1 + R2 + R3
Resistors in parallel1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3
Electric powerP = VI
Heat producedH = I²Rt
Mirror formula1/v + 1/u = 1/f
Lens formula1/v − 1/u = 1/f
Power of a lensP = 1/f, with f in metres

Here is a quick solved example. A 20-ohm resistor connected to a 6 V battery draws a current of I = V/R = 6/20 = 0.3 A.

Chemistry papers also repeat a few standard equations. Practise these until balancing becomes automatic:

  1. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
  2. CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂
  3. Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

Common Mistakes That Lower Science Marks

Most lost marks come from missing labels, wrong sign conventions, skipped units, and unbalanced equations. Careful presentation can raise a score without learning any new topic.

  1. Chemical equations: Students forget to balance the equation or omit state symbols.
  2. Ray diagrams: Arrows on rays are missing, or the sign convention is applied inconsistently.
  3. Biology diagrams: Labels are incomplete, or the diagram is drawn too small to read.
  4. Numericals: The formula is not written first, and the final answer carries no unit.
  5. Long answers: Key terms are left out, so step marks are lost.

Key Takeaways

  1. Board papers repeat concepts and question types, not exact wording.
  2. The 2021 board exams were cancelled, so patterns rely mainly on 2022 to 2025.
  3. Revising CBSE Class 10 Science Previous Year Repeated Questions chapter-wise builds both speed and accuracy.
  4. Diagrams, balanced equations, and numericals with units are the most dependable scoring areas.
  5. Check the latest syllabus and paper design on the official CBSE website.
  6. Past papers show patterns but never guarantee a future question.

Conclusion and Next Steps

Repeated topics reward students who understand concepts and present answers carefully. Pick one chapter today, solve its past paper questions, and note every mistake. Repeat this cycle chapter by chapter until the syllabus is complete.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: What are the most repeated questions in Class 10 Science board exams?

Balancing equations, ray diagrams, Ohm's law numericals, Mendel's crosses, reflex arc and food chain questions appear often. Definitions and reasoning questions on acids, metals and carbon compounds also return regularly.

FAQ 2: Which chapters are most important for CBSE Class 10 Science?

Chemical Substances and World of Living Things carry the largest share of marks in the official unit-wise distribution. Light and Electricity also include many numericals. Confirm the current marks split on the CBSE website.

FAQ 3: Are there board papers from 2021 to analyse?

No. CBSE cancelled the 2021 Class 10 board exams. Students analyse 2022 to 2025 papers and use sample papers to cover the gap.

FAQ 4: Which formulas should students remember for Class 10 Science?

Remember Ohm's law, resistances in series and parallel, power, heat produced, the mirror formula, the lens formula, and the power of a lens. Write the formula before substituting values.

FAQ 5: How should students prepare for the CBSE Class 10 Science board exam?

Finish NCERT first, practise diagrams and numericals, then solve past and sample papers within the time limit. Review a mistake notebook every week.

FAQ 6: Are previous year questions enough for Science preparation?

They show question patterns, but they are not enough alone. Combine them with NCERT text, in-text questions, and sample papers so you can handle new framing of familiar concepts.
