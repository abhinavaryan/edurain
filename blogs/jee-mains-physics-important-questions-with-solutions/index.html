Mechanics: Important Questions with Solutions

Mechanics carries one of the highest weightages in JEE Main Physics and often blends force, motion, and energy concepts together.

Question 1: A block of mass 4 kg rests on a rough horizontal surface with coefficient of friction 0.3. A horizontal force of 20 N is applied. Find the acceleration of the block. Take g equal to 10 metre per second squared.

Solution: Friction force equals mu times normal force, which is 0.3 times 4 times 10, giving 12 N. Net force equals applied force minus friction, so 20 minus 12 equals 8 N. Acceleration equals net force divided by mass, giving 8 divided by 4, which is 2 metre per second squared.

Question 2: A ball is projected at an angle of 45 degrees with speed 20 metre per second. Find its maximum height above the ground.

Solution: Maximum height equals u squared sin squared theta divided by 2g. Here u is 20, sin 45 is 1 over root 2, so sin squared 45 equals 0.5. Height equals 400 times 0.5 divided by 20, which equals 10 metres.

Electrodynamics: Important Questions with Solutions

Electrodynamics questions in JEE Mains frequently combine circuit analysis with conceptual understanding of fields and forces.

Question 3: Three resistors of 2 ohm, 3 ohm, and 6 ohm are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance of the combination.

Solution: For parallel resistors, one over R equals one over 2 plus one over 3 plus one over 6. This equals 3 over 6 plus 2 over 6 plus 1 over 6, giving 6 over 6, which is 1. So the equivalent resistance R equals 1 ohm.

Question 4: A charge of 2 microcoulombs is placed at the centre of a sphere of radius 10 centimetres. Find the electric flux through the sphere.

Solution: By Gauss's law, electric flux equals charge divided by epsilon naught. Substituting the charge as 2 times 10 to the power negative 6 and epsilon naught as 8.85 times 10 to the power negative 12, the flux comes out to approximately 2.26 times 10 to the power 5 newton metre squared per coulomb.

Modern Physics: Important Questions with Solutions

Modern Physics questions test understanding of atomic structure, photoelectric effect, and nuclear phenomena almost every year.

Question 5: The work function of a metal is 2 eV. Find the maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons when light of energy 5 eV falls on it.

Solution: By the photoelectric equation, kinetic energy equals photon energy minus work function. This gives 5 minus 2, which equals 3 eV as the maximum kinetic energy.

Question 6: A radioactive sample has a half-life of 20 minutes. Find the fraction of the sample remaining after 60 minutes.

Solution: Sixty minutes equals three half-lives, since 60 divided by 20 equals 3. The remaining fraction equals one over 2 raised to the power 3, which equals one eighth of the original sample.

Thermodynamics and Waves: Important Questions with Solutions

Thermodynamics and Waves chapters test conceptual clarity alongside standard formula-based calculations in JEE Mains Physics.

Question 7: One mole of an ideal gas undergoes an isothermal process at 300 K, with volume increasing from 2 litre to 4 litre. Find the work done by the gas.

Solution: For isothermal expansion, work done equals nRT times the natural log of V2 over V1. Substituting n as 1, R as 8.31, T as 300, and the ratio as 2, the work done comes to approximately 1729 joules.

Question 8: Two waves of the same frequency and amplitude 5 cm superpose with a phase difference of 60 degrees. Find the resultant amplitude.

Solution: The resultant amplitude equals the square root of A squared plus A squared plus 2A squared cos phi. Substituting A as 5 and phi as 60 degrees, cos 60 equals 0.5, giving amplitude equal to square root of 75, which is approximately 8.66 cm.

Common Mistakes Students Make While Solving These Questions

Many students lose marks on these exact question types due to avoidable errors rather than lack of preparation.

  1. Forgetting to convert units, especially centimetre to metre in field and force problems.
  2. Mixing up sine and cosine while resolving vectors in projectile questions.
  3. Applying the wrong resistor combination formula under exam pressure.
  4. Skipping the negative sign convention in thermodynamics work done calculations.
  5. Rounding intermediate steps too early, leading to a wrong final numerical answer.

Reviewing each mistake after solving builds real accuracy for the actual exam.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these important questions enough to cover JEE Mains Physics 2027?

These questions cover core patterns but should be supplemented with full chapter practice. Use them as a checkpoint, not the only preparation resource.

Which chapter should students start solving important questions from first?

Mechanics is a good starting point since it carries high weightage and builds problem-solving habits. Electrodynamics can follow once Mechanics feels comfortable.

How should students approach numerical value-based questions in Physics?

Write down given values with correct units before applying any formula. This prevents calculation errors that come from rushing into substitution.

Is it necessary to memorize every formula before attempting these questions?

Core formulas should be memorized, but understanding when to apply them matters more. Blind memorization without context often leads to wrong answers.

How many times should a student redo an important question they got wrong?

Redoing a wrong question after two or three days helps confirm the concept is clear. One attempt right after checking the solution is not enough.
