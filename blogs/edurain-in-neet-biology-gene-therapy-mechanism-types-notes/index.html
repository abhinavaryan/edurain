What Is Gene Therapy and Why Is It Important?

Gene therapy is a medical technique that involves correcting or replacing faulty genes responsible for causing diseases. In simple words, if a person has a defective gene that causes a disorder, scientists can insert a healthy copy of that gene into the patient's cells to treat or prevent the disease.

This concept is part of Chapter 12 (Biotechnology and Its Applications) in the NCERT Class 12 Biology textbook. NEET regularly tests students on the basic definition, mechanism, and the first successful case of this treatment.

Key Points to Remember

  1. It targets the root cause of genetic disorders, not just the symptoms.
  2. It uses vectors (usually viruses) to deliver the correct gene into patient cells.
  3. The first successful clinical application was for Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency.
  4. It is classified under the broader topic of biotechnology in medicine.

How Does Gene Therapy Work? A Simple Explanation


The process can be understood in a few clear stages. NEET questions often ask about the sequence of events, so pay close attention.

  1. Identification of the defective gene: Scientists first identify which gene is faulty or missing in the patient's body.
  2. Preparation of the therapeutic gene: A functional copy of the gene is prepared in the laboratory using recombinant DNA technology.
  3. Selection of a vector: A suitable vector, usually a retrovirus, is chosen to carry the healthy gene into the patient's cells.
  4. Delivery into target cells: The vector carrying the correct gene is introduced into the patient's body. This can be done either inside the body (in vivo) or outside the body in a lab and then returned to the patient (ex vivo).
  5. Integration and expression: The healthy gene integrates into the patient's DNA and begins producing the correct protein, restoring normal function.

NEET Specific Tip

For NEET, remember the ADA deficiency example thoroughly. In this case, lymphocytes are extracted from the patient's blood, a functional ADA gene is introduced using a retroviral vector, and the modified lymphocytes are returned to the patient. However, this is not a permanent cure because the modified lymphocytes have a limited lifespan and the procedure must be repeated.

A permanent cure would require introducing the gene into embryonic cells at an early developmental stage.

Types of Gene Therapy You Should Know

There are two main types that students must understand clearly.

Somatic Gene Therapy

  1. The therapeutic gene is introduced into somatic (body) cells.
  2. Changes are not passed on to the next generation.
  3. This is the type currently used in clinical practice.
  4. Example: Treatment of ADA deficiency.

Germline Gene Therapy

  1. The gene is introduced into germ cells (eggs or sperm) or embryos.
  2. Changes become heritable and pass to future generations.
  3. This type raises serious ethical concerns and is not permitted in most countries.

For NEET, somatic gene therapy is the most relevant type. Focus your revision on this.

Which Diseases Can Be Treated Through This Method?

Gene therapy holds promise for treating several genetic and acquired diseases. Here are the most commonly discussed ones in academic syllabi:

  1. ADA deficiency (Severe Combined Immunodeficiency)
  2. Cystic fibrosis
  3. Hemophilia
  4. Sickle cell anemia
  5. Certain types of cancer
  6. Thalassemia
  7. Muscular dystrophy

For NEET, ADA deficiency is the most important example. Other diseases are good to know for a competitive edge and for courses beyond Class 12.

Common Mistakes Students Make in This Topic

Many students lose marks not because the topic is hard, but because they confuse small details. Here are mistakes you should avoid.

  1. Confusing somatic and germline therapy: Remember, somatic therapy affects only the patient. Germline therapy affects future generations.
  2. Forgetting that ADA therapy is not permanent: The modified lymphocytes die over time, so the treatment needs to be repeated.
  3. Mixing up vectors: Retroviruses are the most commonly used vectors in this treatment. Do not confuse them with plasmids used in general genetic engineering.
  4. Ignoring ethical aspects: NEET may include assertion-reason questions touching on the ethical dimensions of germline modifications.

Career Scope in Biotechnology and Genetic Research

If this topic excites you beyond NEET, there is a vast career landscape waiting in biotechnology and genetic research.

Eligibility for Higher Studies

  1. A bachelor's degree in Biotechnology, Genetics, Microbiology, or Molecular Biology is the starting point.
  2. Entrance exams include NEET, CUET, JNU CEEB, and GAT B (for IISERs and CSIR labs).
  3. Master's and doctoral programs are available at institutions recognized by UGC and AICTE.

Skills You Need

  1. Strong foundation in molecular biology and genetics
  2. Laboratory techniques (PCR, gel electrophoresis, cloning)
  3. Analytical thinking and data interpretation
  4. Research writing and scientific communication

Frequently Asked Questions(FAQs)

1. Is gene therapy important for NEET?

Yes, it is a regularly tested topic under Biotechnology and Its Applications. Questions can appear as MCQs, assertion-reason, or statement-based formats.

2. Which example of gene therapy is most important for NEET?

The treatment of ADA (Adenosine Deaminase) deficiency is the most frequently asked example. Learn the complete process from the NCERT textbook.

3. Is the treatment for ADA deficiency permanent?

No. When the functional gene is introduced into somatic cells such as lymphocytes, the effect is temporary because these cells eventually die. A permanent solution would require introducing the gene at the embryonic stage.

4. What is the difference between somatic and germline gene therapy?

Somatic therapy targets body cells, and changes are limited to the patient. Germline therapy targets reproductive cells or embryos, and changes are inherited by future generations.

5. What career options are available in this field after NEET?

After completing MBBS or a degree in biotechnology or genetics, students can pursue careers in genetic counseling, clinical research, genomics, molecular biology research, and pharmaceutical development.

6. How much can a genetic researcher earn in India?

Entry-level salaries range from ₹3.5 to ₹6 LPA, while experienced professionals in research or industry can earn ₹10 LPA and above. Actual earnings depend on qualifications, experience, and the hiring organization.
