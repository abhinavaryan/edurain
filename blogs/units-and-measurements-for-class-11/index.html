Chapter Overview: What This Topic Covers

Units and Measurements deals with how physical quantities are expressed, compared, and converted using standard reference systems. It introduces the International System of Units along with dimensional analysis.

Students learn fundamental and derived quantities, significant figures, error analysis, and dimensional formulas. These tools reappear constantly in mechanics, thermodynamics, and electromagnetism chapters later.

For JEE, this chapter rarely appears as a standalone question. Instead, it silently supports numerical accuracy and unit conversion in almost every other physics chapter.

Important Formulas for Units and Measurements

A dimensional formula expresses a physical quantity in terms of the seven base quantities of the SI system. This tool checks equation correctness and derives relations between quantities.

QuantityDimensional FormulaSI Unit
Velocity[M0 L1 T-1]metre per second
Force[M1 L1 T-2]newton
Energy[M1 L2 T-2]joule
Pressure[M1 L-1 T-2]pascal
Density[M1 L-3 T0]kilogram per cubic metre


For error analysis, remember that the maximum percentage error in a product or quotient equals the sum of individual percentage errors. This single rule solves most JEE error-based problems.

Common Mistakes Students Make in Units and Measurements

Mixing up fundamental and derived quantities remains a frequent error among Class 11 students. Length, mass, and time are fundamental, while velocity and force are derived.

Many students round off significant figures incorrectly during multiplication or division steps. The final answer should always match the least accurate measurement in the calculation.

Another common mistake appears in dimensional formula questions, where students forget that dimensionless constants carry no dimensional formula at all. This detail often costs marks in JEE.

Error analysis questions also trip up students who add percentage errors instead of following the correct product or quotient rule. Always identify the operation before applying the formula.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the difference between fundamental and derived units?

Fundamental units measure base quantities like length, mass, and time directly. Derived units come from combinations of these fundamental units, such as velocity or force.

2. How is dimensional analysis used in JEE numericals?

It checks whether a physics equation is dimensionally correct before solving it. It also helps derive relationships between different physical quantities quickly.

3. What is the rule for calculating percentage error in multiplication?

Add the individual percentage errors of each quantity involved in the multiplication. The same rule applies for division problems as well.

4. How many significant figures should a final answer have?

The final answer should match the measurement with the fewest significant figures. This keeps the result consistent with the least accurate input value.

5. Can a physical quantity have more than one unit system?

Yes, quantities can be expressed in SI, CGS, or FPS systems depending on convention. JEE questions mostly expect answers in the SI system unless stated otherwise.

6. Why do dimensionless quantities have no dimensional formula?

Dimensionless quantities are ratios of similar physical quantities, so their dimensions cancel out completely. Examples include strain, refractive index, and angle in radians.
