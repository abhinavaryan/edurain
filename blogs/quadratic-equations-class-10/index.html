What Is a Quadratic Equation in Class 10 Maths?

A quadratic equation is a polynomial equation of degree two, usually written as ax² + bx + c = 0, where "a" is not zero. In the Quadratic Equations Class 10 chapter, students learn to identify, form, and solve such equations using different methods.

Key points to remember:

  1. The equation always has two roots (values of x).
  2. "a", "b", and "c" are real numbers.
  3. The highest power of x is always 2.

Once students understand this basic form, solving even tricky questions becomes much easier.

How Do You Solve Quadratic Equations Step by Step?

There are three main methods taught in Class 10:

  1. Factorization method – Split the middle term and factor the equation into two brackets.
  2. Completing the square method – Rearrange the equation to form a perfect square.
  3. Quadratic formula – Use x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a when factorization is difficult.

For most board exam questions, the quadratic formula works fastest, especially when the roots are not whole numbers.

What Makes a Quadratic Equation Question Tricky?

Some questions look simple but hide a twist. Common tricky patterns include:

  1. Word problems where students must first form the equation.
  2. Equations with fractions or brackets that need simplifying first.
  3. Questions based on the discriminant (b² − 4ac) to check the nature of roots.
  4. Equations where one root is given, and students must find the missing constant.

Reading the question twice and writing down what is given helps avoid confusion in these cases.

What Are Common Mistakes Students Make?

Many students lose marks due to small, avoidable errors:

  1. Sign errors while shifting terms across the equals sign.
  2. Forgetting that a quadratic equation usually has two roots, not one.
  3. Using the wrong values of a, b, and c in the formula.
  4. Skipping the simplification step before applying a method.

Practicing a few extra questions daily builds accuracy and speed over time.

What Tips Help Solve Tricky Questions Quickly?

Direct answer: Checking the discriminant first often saves time on tricky problems.

Useful tips for students:

  1. Always check the discriminant before choosing a method.
  2. Verify both roots by substituting them back into the original equation.
  3. Practice word problems separately, since they test understanding, not just calculation.
  4. Keep formula sheets handy during revision, not during the actual exam.
