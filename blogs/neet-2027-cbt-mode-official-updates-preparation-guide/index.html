NEET 2027 CBT Mode: Complete Preparation Guide for the Expected Computer-Based Exam | EduRain Blog

The Education Minister announced on 15 May 2026 that NEET UG will be a computer-based test from 2027. NTA has not yet released the 2027 bulletin with full details. This guide explains NEET 2027 CBT Mode, what is confirmed, and how to prepare for the change.

Is NEET UG 2027 Officially Moving to Computer-Based Testing?

Yes, the Education Minister announced on 15 May 2026 that NEET UG will be computer-based from 2027. Reports say NTA told the Supreme Court the same. The official 2027 bulletin is still awaited.

The announcement followed the cancellation of NEET UG 2026 over paper leak allegations. A repeat exam was held on 21 June 2026. The move aims to remove the OMR sheet process.

Treat details beyond the exam mode as unconfirmed until NTA publishes the bulletin.

ItemStatus
Computer-based mode from 2027Announced by the Education Minister
Total marks and subjectsReported to stay the same; confirm in the bulletin
Number of questions and durationNot officially announced for 2027
Multiple shifts or daysNot announced
Normalisation methodNot announced
Exam dateNot announced

What Changes for Students in a Computer-Based Exam?

Students will read questions on a screen and select answers with a mouse. There will be no OMR sheet. The syllabus and subjects are reported to stay the same, so NCERT-based preparation remains valid.

The change affects how the exam is conducted, not what it tests. Three things need new practice:

  1. Screen reading: Long questions and diagrams must be read on a monitor.
  2. Navigation: You must move between questions and mark them for review.
  3. Rough work: Check the bulletin for rules on how rough work is allowed.

Will NEET 2027 Be Held in Multiple Shifts With Normalisation?

NTA has not confirmed shifts, exam days, or a normalisation formula. NEET has over 20 lakh candidates, so reports expect multiple shifts. Do not plan around unofficial numbers such as 5 to 6 days.

NTA already runs JEE Main and CUET as computer-based tests in multiple shifts. If NEET follows this model, NTA must publish a scoring method. Until then, focus on what you can control, which is your preparation.

How Should Students Prepare for a Computer-Based NEET?

Keep NCERT and the NMC syllabus as your base. Then add computer-based mock tests, screen reading practice, and timed full-length papers. This builds speed and comfort with the new format.

  1. Finish NCERT Biology, Physics, and Chemistry against the latest NMC syllabus.
  2. Take one full-length mock test each week on a computer or laptop.
  3. Use the 2026 pattern of 180 questions in three hours until NTA confirms 2027.
  4. Practise reading long questions on screen. Solve Physics numericals on a rough sheet.
  5. Track official updates on NEET 2027 CBT Mode at neet.nta.nic.in.
  6. Review every wrong answer and note whether it was a concept or a reading error.
  7. Students who want regular screen-based practice can look at Best NEET Online Coaching 2027-28 programmes that offer an online test series and doubt support.

What Mock Test Routine Builds Speed and Accuracy?

A weekly routine of full-length tests, error review, and chapter practice works well. Increase test frequency as the exam gets closer. The table below is a suggested plan, not an official guideline.

Biology carries 90 of 180 questions under the latest known pattern. So protect daily time for Botany and Zoology. During a mock test, try different question orders and keep the one that suits you.

Which Books and Resources Are Useful?

Your main resources are NCERT Biology, Physics, and Chemistry for Class 11 and Class 12. Add previous years' NEET papers and computer-based mock tests. Use official NTA practice material if NTA releases it for 2027.

NCERT builds your concepts, but a computer-based exam also needs regular screen practice. What Common Mistakes Should NEET Aspirants Avoid?

The most common mistakes are trusting unofficial patterns, practising only on paper, and delaying preparation until the bulletin appears. Each one costs time and confidence.

  1. Trusting unofficial numbers: Wait for NTA before you plan around shifts, days, or normalisation.
  2. Practising only on paper: Take mock tests on a screen at least once a week.
  3. Skipping NCERT: Most conceptual questions come from the textbook content.
  4. Ignoring negative marking: The latest known pattern deducts 1 mark for a wrong answer.
  5. Waiting for the bulletin: The syllabus is already available from NMC, so start now.
  6. Fearing the new format: The exam mode changes, but the subjects and concepts remain familiar.

Conclusion and Next Steps

The shift to a computer-based exam changes how you answer, not what you study. Stay with NCERT, practise on a screen, and wait for the official bulletin before you rely on any pattern details. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

FAQ 1: Is NEET 2027 confirmed to be a computer-based test?

The Education Minister announced it on 15 May 2026, and reports say the NTA told the Supreme Court the same. The detailed 2027 bulletin is still awaited, so confirm the final rules there.

FAQ 2: Will the NEET syllabus change with the computer-based exam?

No syllabus change has been confirmed for 2027. NMC prescribes the syllabus, so check nmc.org.in. NCERT Class 11 and Class 12 remain the base for preparation.

FAQ 3: Will NEET 2027 be held in multiple shifts?

NTA has not announced this. Because NEET has over 20 lakh candidates, reports expect multiple shifts. Wait for the official bulletin before planning around any number of shifts or days.

FAQ 4: How can students practise for a computer-based NEET?

Take full-length mock tests on a computer or laptop every week. Practise reading questions on screen, navigating between questions, and solving numericals on a rough sheet within the time limit.

FAQ 5: Will the number of questions and marks change in 2027?

NTA has not announced the 2027 pattern. The 2026 exam had 180 compulsory questions for 720 marks in three hours. News reports say the 720 marks remain unchanged.

FAQ 6: Should students wait for the bulletin before starting preparation?

No. The syllabus is already published by NMC, and the subjects are not expected to change. Start NCERT revision and mock practice now, then adjust once NTA releases the bulletin.
