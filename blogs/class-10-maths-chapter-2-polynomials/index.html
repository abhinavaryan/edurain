Important Formulas and Results

A quadratic polynomial ax² + bx + c has two zeros, α and β, with fixed relationships to its coefficients. Memorise these results exactly. Most numerical questions in this chapter use them directly.

ConceptFormula or Result
Sum of zerosα + β = −b/a
Product of zerosαβ = c/a
Polynomial from sum S and product Px² − Sx + P
Division algorithmp(x) = g(x) × q(x) + r(x)
Condition on remainderr(x) = 0 or degree of r(x) is less than degree of g(x)
Zeros from a graphNumber of points where the curve meets the x-axis


A linear polynomial has at most one zero. A quadratic has at most two. A cubic has at most three. The graph of a quadratic is a parabola.

Exercise-wise Solving Approach

Solve polynomial problems by identifying the type of question first. Then apply the matching formula or method. This habit prevents most errors in Class 10 Maths Chapter 2: Polynomials exercises.

  1. Graph questions: Count the points where the curve touches or crosses the x-axis. Each point is one real zero.
  2. Finding zeros: Factorise the quadratic by splitting the middle term. Equate each factor to zero.
  3. Verification questions: Find the zeros first. Then check the sum and product against −b/a and c/a.
  4. Forming a polynomial: Compute the sum and product of the given zeros. Substitute them into x² − Sx + P.
  5. Division problems: Write both polynomials in descending powers. Divide step by step until the remainder has a lower degree.
  6. Final check: Multiply divisor and quotient, then add the remainder. The result must match the dividend.

Common Mistakes Students Make in Polynomials

The most frequent error is a wrong sign in the sum of zeros. Many students write b/a instead of −b/a. This single slip costs marks in almost every verification question.

Watch for these specific mistakes:

  1. Reading a, b, and c before writing the polynomial in standard form.
  2. Forgetting that x² + 1 has no real zeros, because its graph never meets the x-axis.
  3. Skipping a missing term during division. Write it with zero as its coefficient.
  4. Stopping division too early, before the remainder degree falls below the divisor degree.
  5. Confusing a curve that touches the x-axis with one that crosses it. A touch gives one repeated zero.
  6. Making sign errors when subtracting terms during long division.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How many zeros can a quadratic polynomial have?

A quadratic polynomial can have at most two real zeros. Its graph, a parabola, can cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or miss it completely.

2. How do I find zeros of a polynomial from its graph?

Look at the points where the curve meets the x-axis. The x-coordinate of each point is a zero. The number of such points gives the number of zeros.

3. What are the formulas for the sum and product of zeros?

For ax�� + bx + c, the sum of zeros is −b/a. The product of zeros is c/a. Always check the signs before substituting values.

4. How do I form a quadratic polynomial from given zeros?

Find the sum and product of the zeros. Then write x² − (sum)x + (product). Multiply by any nonzero constant if the question needs a specific form.

5. How do I avoid mistakes in polynomial division?

Arrange terms in descending powers and write missing terms with zero coefficients. Divide the leading terms each time. Stop when the remainder degree is less than the divisor degree.

6. Which book is best for extra practice in this chapter?

Finish NCERT first, including examples. Then use NCERT Exemplar for harder reasoning questions. RS Aggarwal or RD Sharma can add more numerical practice.
